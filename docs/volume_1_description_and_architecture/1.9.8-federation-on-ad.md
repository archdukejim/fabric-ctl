## 1.9.8.3 A site's DC: writable, read-only or none

Chosen at the invitation (`fabricctl federation invite lab --dc writable|rodc|none`), changeable later (1.9.8.12).
Every site runs the full stack whatever it chooses (2.1.9.9). The root site always has a writable DC; it holds the
domain-wide roles (FSMO: schema, naming, RID, PDC emulator, infrastructure).

| | Writable | Read-only (RODC) | None |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holds | the whole domain, every password hash | the whole domain read-only; passwords only of the site's *Allowed RODC Password Replication Group* (the site's people and machines), preloaded | nothing: the site's services use a DC of its parent |
| Writes at the site | its own OU (and whatever overrides allow), offline too | none; writes go to a writable DC over the link (refused while cut off) | none locally; over the link |
| Sign-ins while cut off | everyone it may admit | cached people and machines; others refused | Windows' and SSSD's cached sign-ins on the machines only |
| If the host is taken | the domain is | that site's cached passwords are | nothing beyond the site's own services |

- Setup and the Federation tab state the last row next to the choice.
- NTLM forwarding at an RODC for uncached accounts (Q6 follow-up) was confirmed in S1 (2026-10-04, `tests/samba`):
  with the writable DC reachable, PEAP at an RODC site works for everyone; with it cut off, for the site's cached
  people and machines only.
- As built (0.7.1, decision 2.1.6.52): the site's group `<site>-rodc-cache` is put on its read-only DC's allow list
  at the join (`samba/allow_rodc_cache`), and the fabric host's own account goes into it, so the host signs in with
  the link down ([1.6.11.13](1.6.11-host-access.md#161113-the-fabric-host-at-a-read-only-dcs-site-071)). The site's
  people and its other machines are not yet cached by fabric. **Revised 2026-10-13 (decision 2.1.6.55, built for 0.9,
  not run):** they are: `<site>-users` and `<site>-admins` nested in that group, the site's machines members of it,
  preloaded at the join, every apply and hourly; privileged accounts never, the denied list kept whole
  ([1.6.11.14](1.6.11-host-access.md#161114-a-read-only-dcs-site-its-people-and-machines-cached-09)).

## 1.9.8.4 Joining a site

The join of 1.9.4.1 is kept: invitation (fingerprint, one-time secret), the endpoint over TLS, the intermediate CA,
the TSIG key and delegation. The answer adds:

- the AD domain, realm and NetBIOS name, and the root DC's address;
- the site's OU, groups (`<site>-users`, `<site>-admins`), service accounts, ACLs and id block, **created by the
  parent before it answers** (by the parent's agent through its nearest writable DC, which its inherited rights allow, 2.1.6.20);
- for a writable or read-only DC: a **temporary join account** with a random password (in the TLS answer, never on
  a command line), valid for this join only and deleted when the join is done or after one hour. Samba's DC join
  needs domain-admin rights; S8 establishes whether less is enough, and uses the least that works;
- the site's service-account passwords, which go straight into the site's OpenBao.

Setup then joins the DC (`samba-tool domain join … DC|RODC`, into the AD site of the same name), registers its
names in AD's DNS (replication finds partners by them, Q12), preloads an RODC's cache, and starts the rest of the
stack against it. A site with no DC skips the DC and points its services at its parent's DC.

## 1.9.8.5 Replication between DCs

- AD's own replication (DRS) between DCs; its topology is AD's (the KCC) from **AD sites and site links**, which
  fabric writes: one AD site per fabric site, its subnets from the address plan (1.10.2.8), one site link from each
  site to its parent (cost 100, every 15 minutes). Windows machines find their own site's DC by their subnet.
- **Ports between DCs:** 53, 88, 135, 389, 445, 464, 636, 3268, 3269 and the DC's RPC range (1.6.5.4) between
  the two hosts' addresses; the federation link opens them to the peer's address only, as for DNS today (1.9.4.2).
- Conflicts follow AD's rules (Q12): the later write of an attribute wins; a name created on both sides keeps
  both objects, one renamed `CNF:`. fabric's doctor lists `CNF:` objects for an admin to resolve.
- A site cut off longer than AD's tombstone lifetime (180 days) must be re-joined; doctor warns well before.

## 1.9.8.7 Offline behaviour

1.9.3.3 holds with these differences: a site with a writable DC keeps creating and changing its own people,
groups, machines and devices while cut off (they replicate when the link returns); a read-only site is as today
(changes wait for the link); a site with no DC loses domain sign-ins except what machines cache, and fabric's
status says so whenever its DC is unreachable. Every site keeps DNS, DHCP, its CA, its secrets and EAP-TLS.

## 1.9.8.9 The Federation tab: limits, overrides and grants

Built with M6:

- **Sites:** name, parent, relay, DC type, link and replication health, last replication, `CNF:` objects.
- **Limits:** each site's default write limits (1.6.3.6), as a table; **overrides** added and removed by the root's
  admins (`federation:admin`), each with who and when.
- **Access grants** (1.6.3.10): explicit and blanket, per site.
- **Address plan:** site → network → VLAN → notes (1.10.2.8).
- Overrides and grants are kept in fabric's database, a copy at every site ([1.9.8.16](#19816-where-overrides-and-grants-are-kept)).

## 1.9.8.10 Sign-in and 802.1X at each type of site

- **Keycloak** at each site federates its own DC (or, with none, its parent's), writable. At an RODC site writes
  come back as referrals to a writable DC; Keycloak follows them (to be proven in S8; if it cannot, Keycloak there
  is configured with the writable DC for writes and the RODC for reads).
- **FreeRADIUS** uses its own site's DC's winbind; at a site with no DC, PEAP needs the link (EAP-TLS and MAB keep
  working from the site's own data, read from the parent's DC and cached).

## 1.9.8.11 HA partners

Phase F3's "389-DS multi-supplier" becomes **a writable DC on each of the site's nodes** (AD multi-master; each
node's services use their local DC; up to four nodes, 1.9.2.2). Takeover of the rest: 2.1.9.20.

## 1.9.8.12 Removing a site, changing its DC type

- **Changing the type** (`fabricctl federation dc-type <site> writable|rodc|none`): demote, then join again under
  the same name (Q15); the site's services use the parent's DC meanwhile; nothing else of the site changes.
  **Built 2026-10-13 for writable and read-only (2.1.9.23):** requested from a site above, carried out at the site
  with its consent: [1.9.8.22](#19822-changing-a-sites-dc-type-requested-from-above-09). `none` stays open.
- **Removing a site** (`fabricctl federation remove <site>`, by its parent): its DC is removed from the domain
  (`samba-tool domain demote --remove-other-dead-server` when it is gone), its AD site, site link, service
  accounts and grants go; its OU (people, machines, devices) is moved to the parent's or deleted, as the admin
  chooses; its CA is revoked (F7). **Revised 2026-10-10 (2.1.9.17):** the OU's objects go to the Lost OU
  (1.9.8.17).

## 1.9.8.14 Nesting and inheritance

Decided by the owner 2026-10-05 (2.1.6.20); replaces 1.9.8.6.

- **A site's OU is in its parent's OU.** The root site's OU is the only one directly under `OU=sites`; a site that
  joins the root gets `OU=<site>,OU=<root>,OU=sites`, one nested under it `OU=<child>,OU=<site>,OU=<root>,OU=sites`.
  The parent is also recorded on the OU (`fabricParentSite`). Nesting in the directory follows the attachment of
  1.9.5.1; the CA's path length is unchanged.
- **Inherited by default, from the domain down:**
  - Group Policy: AD's own GPO inheritance. A GPO linked to the domain or to a parent's OU applies to every site
    below it. A child overrides a setting with its own GPO (the closer link wins) or blocks inheritance on its OU
    (`<site>-gpo-admins`, 2.1.9.13).
  - **Controls:** a parent links a GPO **enforced** when its children may not change it. An enforced link wins
    over the child's own GPOs and passes a blocked inheritance; only the parent's GPO admins set or clear it.
  - Permissions: AD's ACL inheritance. A parent's agent and roles reach every site below it; a child cannot remove
    an inherited ACE.
- **A site with no DC** uses its nearest ancestor that has one, writable for writes (the parent's, else the next
  one up): its services read and write there over LDAPS with the site's own service accounts, whose ACLs are
  limited to its OU (1.6.3.6). While that DC is unreachable it keeps what 1.9.8.7 says.
- **A nested parent prepares its child** with its own agent through its nearest writable DC (its inherited rights
  cover the child's OU); the root is asked only when the parent has no writable DC on its path.
- **Re-parenting moves the OU** (an LDAP rename of the subtree), with its CA and site link as today. The new
  parent recognises a site the domain already holds (`read_sites.py`) and moves its OU and AD site link only: no
  join account, no new id block, the site's service accounts untouched, and never the site's GPOs, which its own
  DC owns (S8.4) and rewrites for its new parents when it converges next; a site cannot move under one of its
  own descendants. Objects keep
  their SIDs and GUIDs, so GPO links and group memberships hold; fabric's own code finds a site's OU by its mark
  and name, never by a stored DN. What a host or a machine keeps by DN is rewritten: the site's `ad_site_ou`
  (FreeRADIUS, Keycloak), and its joined Linux machines' sudo search bases (the installer run again).
- **Relays forward joins and status only.** DC replication goes directly between the site's DC and its parent's
  over routed connectivity (2.1.9.10); a relay node is an ordinary site.

## 1.9.8.15 Group Policy between sites over SMB

Decided by the owner 2026-10-05 (2.1.6.16 revised); replaces 1.9.8.8.

- **Each GPO has an owning site:** the site whose OU it is linked to; the root for domain-linked GPOs. Only the
  owner's agent (or its GPO admins with RSAT at the owner's DC) writes its folder.
- **Every DC pulls the GPO folders it does not own** from the owner's DC over SMB (`smbclient` in the DC's
  container, Kerberos as the DC's own machine account; 445 is open between DCs, 1.9.8.5), every 5 minutes. A copy
  is taken only when the GPO object's version (which AD replicates) is newer than the local folder's `GPT.INI`, and
  only once the owner's `GPT.INI` matches the object: object and files that disagree are reported, never applied
  half. Each folder's ACLs are then set from its object, as for a GPO fabric writes itself.
- The owner's DC is a writable DC in the owner's AD site, else in its nearest ancestor's (an RODC site's GPOs, and
  those of a site with no DC, are written there). An RODC pulls everything.
- A GPO changed at a DC that does not own it raises the object's version (which replicates) without the owner's
  files: every DC then reports that the owner's files and the object disagree and copies nothing, until the change
  is made again at the owner. Folders are copied whole into a new folder that replaces the old one only once
  complete and checked; the DC's log names each GPO copied or refused (`pull_sysvol:`).
- **SYSVOL and NETLOGON are never advertised** (the owner's condition): both shares are `browseable = no`, so they
  are not listed when `\\<dc>` is opened; NetBIOS is off (`disable netbios`, nothing on 137–139); no mDNS
  (`multicast dns register = no`) and no WS-Discovery service. Windows reads policy by its path
  (`\\<domain>\SYSVOL\...`), which hiding does not change.

## 1.9.8.16 Where overrides and grants are kept

Decided by the owner 2026-10-05 (2.1.9.14).

- **fabric's database:** a `fabric` database and role in each site's Postgres (the server Keycloak uses); its
  password in the site's OpenBao. One table each for overrides and grants (who, when, what, the owning site).
- **One writer per row:** a row belongs to the site that made it (a parent's override on a child is the parent's);
  only that site writes it.
- **A copy everywhere:** Postgres's logical replication along the site tree; each site publishes to its parent and
  its children the rows they do not own, so every site holds every row and nothing loops. A site that loses its
  database gets every row back from its parent when it is reinstalled or rejoined.
- **Postgres is never on the host.** A link reaches the peer's Postgres through nginx's `stream` proxy: one port,
  open in the firewall to the linked sites' addresses only; TLS that requires a client certificate signed by the
  organisation's root, whose name nginx checks against the linked sites (a certificate of any other subject is
  sent to a closed port); then a Unix socket nginx and Postgres share, so the inner hop never touches a network
  (proven 2.3.6.1.25: nginx 1.30's stream proxy cannot open Postgres's direct TLS, which requires the ALPN
  `postgresql` nginx does not send upstream); and a replication role per link, signing in with a password kept in
  a passfile (never in the subscription's catalog), that may only replicate these tables. The peer connects with
  `sslnegotiation=direct` (Postgres 17+), which nginx terminates.
- **The effects are in AD**, as before (ACLs for overrides; GPO, FreeRADIUS and Keycloak changes for grants), so
  losing the table never takes anyone's access away by itself.

## 1.9.8.17 The Lost OU

Owner, 2026-10-10 (2.1.9.17). A site with no DC is still a site: its OU in the domain, served by a parent's DC
(1.9.8.3). Removing a site never deletes its people, machines or devices:

- **Where they go.** `fabricctl federation remove <site>` moves everything in the site's OU into one default
  **Lost OU** (`OU=lost`), recording which site each object came from, so it can be restored or moved on.
- **What they lose.** Objects in the Lost OU have no connection to the domain's resources: no group gives them
  access, no site's grants reach them, no GPO but the Lost OU's applies.
- **What people keep, by default.** Signing in with a cached profile still works, so a person on a laptop is not
  locked out of their own machine. An admin can restrict this in the web console.

To settle when it is built:
- **How access is cut.** Removing the objects from every group (kept on record for a restore), a GPO linked to the
  Lost OU, or both. A removed site's own groups go with its OU.
- **What "restricted" means.** Online sign-in still works while an account is enabled and a DC answers; stopping
  every sign-in means disabling the accounts.
- **Who works the Lost OU**, and whether objects there expire.

## 1.9.8.18 The Domain-Join role

Owner, 2026-10-10 (2.1.9.18). Joining is a **role, Domain-Join**, beside the site roles of 2.1.9.13:

- It covers joining machines to the domain and preparing a site's join (the temporary join account of 1.9.8.4).
- It can be delegated to any group an admin makes. By default Domain Admins hold it.
- The temporary join account a parent hands a joining site holds the least that works.

To settle when it is built: whether a **DC's** join (`samba-tool domain join ... DC|RODC`) works with less than
Domain Admin rights in Samba (S8, 2.3.6.1.20). If it does not, a DC's join keeps a Domain Admin join account, and
the Domain-Join role grants preparing it, not the rights themselves.

## 1.9.8.19 Moving an existing install into a federation

Owner, 2026-10-10 (2.1.9.1). Only a fresh install joins a federation; an existing install moves into it with a
**migration option**:

- **What it carries** into the new domain: people (with their groups), machines, and SSH keys.
- **What it cannot carry:** each host still has to be moved (left and joined again); the option guides it, host by
  host, with what is left to do shown in the web console.

To settle when it is designed:
- **Passwords.** Whether people's password hashes can be carried, or each person sets a new password at their
  first sign-in.
- **Identity.** Whether a moved person or machine keeps its old SID (SID history) so files and permissions on
  moved hosts still match.
- **Scope.** Which of an existing install's settings (DNS records, DHCP reservations, certificates) come along.

## 1.9.8.20 Layered roles and sibling isolation

**Decisions 2.1.9.21 and 2.1.9.22 (owner, 2026-10-13), placed in 0.9; built 2026-10-13, not yet run against
containers (2.3.9.2).** They pull forward the per-site role mapping of 1.9.3.1's decision F4 and 1.9.6.1's
"group-to-bundle mapping aware of site-scoped groups", which 1.0's S8.6 had. The AD rights are
[1.6.3.18](1.6.3-samba-directory.md#16318-layered-roles-in-the-directory-09); the console's bundles
[1.6.8.2](1.6.8-rbac.md#1682-the-site-helpdesk-and-roles-granted-from-above). Promoting a branch's DC to read-write,
which a growing site needs too, is its own work (owner, the same day).

1.9.8.20.1 **The layers.** Central IT (the organisation's groups) manages every site, its installs and its device
lifecycle, as before. A branch's local admins (`<site>-admins`) are its helpdesk: they unlock, reset passwords, add
and disable the branch's own people. When a branch grows, a site above grants it more, one role at a time.

1.9.8.20.2 **Granting.** A site above grants a role at a site below to a group or a person — by default the site's
own `<site>-admins` — by putting it in the role's group `<site>-<role>` in the site's `OU=groups`:

- console roles (their bundle in that site's console only): `network-operators`, `equipment-operators`,
  `pki-operators`, `helpdesk`, `auditors`;
- AD roles (their rights in that site's OU and, as every parent's role, the sites below it, 2.1.6.20): `ou-admins`,
  `machine-admins`, `gpo-admins`, `windows-admins`, `fabric-admins`.

From the host, `fabricctl federation roles grant|revoke <site> <role> [--to|--from <group|person>]`; in the console,
the Federation tab's Roles card (`federation:admin`, the admin bundle). The DC's operation `grant_site_role` refuses
unless the target is **below** this install's site, so a site never grants itself, a sibling or a parent; AD refuses a
write outside this site's agent's OU besides. A console role's group missing at a site that has not converged 0.9 yet
is made by the grant. Every change is audited (`SITE_ROLE_GRANT`, `SITE_ROLE_REVOKE`). A site above may also **remake** a site below's group — one the site made itself, which convergence refuses, or a missing one, `<site>-admins` included (`fabricctl federation roles remake <site> <role|admins>`, the Roles card's action `remake`; DC operation `remake_site_group`; audited `SITE_GROUP_REMAKE`); never at the site itself, a sibling or a site above.

1.9.8.20.3 **Seeing what is granted.** `fabricctl federation roles [<site>]`, a line per site in `fabricctl federation
status`, and the Roles card list, for this site and every site below it, each role's group and its members, and anyone holding a role beyond their own level (2.1.9.24, reported, never moved); a role
still held through the pre-0.9 default is marked pending (the next convergence takes it away). This site's own roles
are listed too, marked as granted only from above.

1.9.8.20.4 **Sibling isolation (2.1.9.22).** The Federation tab's address plan and overlaps, `fabricctl federation
networks`, `fabricctl ipam find`, and (revised 2026-10-13) `fabricctl ipam show <site>` and `ipam export [<site>]`
show only this site's networks and those of the sites below it — the same rule as
the Networks tab (`visible_sites`, 2.1.10.27); the top (an install with no upstream) sees every site
(`federation/common/sites_shown`, `visible_plan`). An overlap with a site not shown is counted, never named: the branch
learns to ask the top, not its sibling's networks. The checks that keep sites from overlapping (a join, a DHCP subnet)
still check against every site, and their refusals name the other network, since they must. `ipam show` or `export`
of a site not shown is refused before AD is read; `export` without a site writes every site shown.

1.9.8.20.5 **What fabric does not hide.** AD itself stays readable domain-wide to every authenticated account, as in
any AD: a person with a directory tool can still read other sites' objects (the address plan included). fabric does
not try to hide that; sibling isolation is what fabric's own console and commands show.

1.9.8.20.6 **Granted roles reach the sites below** (revision of 2026-10-13, 2.1.9.21): a role granted at a site works
there and at every site below it — in the console through each site's Keycloak mapping its ancestors' console role
groups ([1.6.8.2.5](1.6.8-rbac.md#1682-the-site-helpdesk-and-roles-granted-from-above)), in AD through inheritance and
the entries each site's convergence repeats for the sites above it
([1.6.3.18.7](1.6.3-samba-directory.md#16318-layered-roles-in-the-directory-09)). A role is still granted only from a
site above the one it is granted at.

## 1.9.8.21 A branch's read-only DC is lost or stolen (0.9)

Decision 2.1.6.58 (the owner, 2026-10-13, option B of 2.3.6.9.4 point 1). Built for 0.9; container-free tests run,
the `samba` suite's written, not run (2.3.9.3.4). A read-only DC holds copies of the passwords it was allowed to cache
(2.1.6.52, 2.1.6.55). In normal operation that is harmless (2.1.6.55's revision); when the DC is lost or stolen,
every password it held must be treated as known to whoever has it.

1.9.8.21.1 **Where and by whom.** `fabricctl federation rodc-lost <site>` as root on the fabric host of a site
**above** the lost DC's site (its OU contains the site's, 1.9.8.14), whose own DC is **writable**; or the same from
the console (1.9.8.21.5). Refused, nothing changed, naming why: from the site itself, a sibling or a site below; at a
read-only DC; a site not in the domain; **this site's writable DC does not answer** (the work runs inside it). Every
refusal is audited `FED_RODC_LOST_REFUSED`.

1.9.8.21.2 **The list** (`federation/rodc_lost` → `samba/run_rodc_lost` → `src/containers/samba/rodc_lost.py`
inside the writable DC, as the system on its own database): the site's read-only DCs are the server objects of its
AD site (`CN=<site>,CN=Sites`) whose computer account carries `PARTIAL_SECRETS_ACCOUNT`. For each, the accounts it
was told the password of are read from its computer account's **`msDS-RevealedUsers`** (the stored record AD keeps
of every secret sent to that RODC; Samba keeps it since 4.7) and `msDS-RevealedList` where the DC constructs it
(2.3.9.3.4 cites both), each account once. Each is given a kind:
- **person**: an account of a person;
- **machine**: a computer account;
- **service**: an account in a site's `OU=service-accounts`, or with a password that never expires;
- **dc** and **krbtgt**: the read-only DC's own computer account and its own `krbtgt_<n>` account
  (`msDS-KrbTgtLink`): never reset, removed with the DC;
- **protected**: a krbtgt account that is not this DC's (never revealed to a read-only DC by AD's built-in deny
  list; if one ever is, it is shown and left for 4.9.1.3's manual step);
- **gone**: no longer in the domain.

The list is shown (every account, its kind and what happens to it) and recorded before anything changes:
`<config>/rodc-lost-<site>.json` (root, 0600, never a password). Audited `FED_RODC_LOST_LISTED`.

1.9.8.21.3 **On confirmation** (the site's name typed; at the command line a terminal is required, there is no
`--yes`):
- **Resets, only from the list.** People: a random password nobody is told, changed at the next sign-in
  (`pwdLastSet` 0). Machines and service accounts: a random password (rotated). Sent to the DC in batches of 50; the
  DC refuses any account that is not in a read-only DC's revealed list while that DC still exists, and any kind but
  these three. Each reset is recorded and audited `FED_RODC_LOST_RESET` with its kind and DN.
- **The DC removed** as Samba removes a dead DC (`samba.remove_dc.remove_dc`, what `samba-tool domain demote
  --remove-other-dead-server` runs): its server and NTDS Settings objects, its computer account (and with it its
  password replication allow list: the `<site>-rodc-cache` allowance, cleared first), its own krbtgt account (every
  ticket it issued stops working), its DNS records and SYSVOL references. Audited `FED_RODC_LOST_REMOVED`. The site
  has **no DC** until a new one joins (a new install at the site, joined read-only again through an invitation, or a
  DC type change later).
- Finished: recorded and audited `FED_RODC_LOST_DONE`.

1.9.8.21.4 **How each kind recovers.**
- **People** sign in again once an admin of their site (or a site above) gives them a one-time password:
  `fabricctl people reset <uid>` or Directory > Reset sign-in (1.6.3.8); they change it at that sign-in.
- **Machines** sign in to the domain again only once they are joined again: Linux machines through the join script
  (4.6.6), Windows machines by rejoining (4.6.5). Their SSH host certificates are unaffected.
- **Service accounts**: fabric's own (`fabric-agent-<site>`, `fabric-keycloak-<site>`, `fabric-radius-<site>`) are
  set again by the site's fabric when it is set up again (`ensure_service_accounts` sets the password it keeps when a
  sign-in with it fails, 1.6.3.7); any other service account's owner sets a new password.
- **The read-only DC's krbtgt** is gone with it; the domain's own krbtgt is not touched (it is never revealed to a
  read-only DC).

1.9.8.21.5 **The console** (Federation tab): with `federation:admin` (central IT's permission for the sites below;
`system:admin` until the 0.9 integration, 2.1.6.58 revised 2026-10-14), a field names the site; the page lists its
read-only DC's accounts and what happens to each (agent `GET /v1/federation/rodc-lost/<site>`); the form needs the
site's name typed and a sign-in from the last 5 minutes (as the vault's changes) and posts to
`/federation/rodc-lost/<site>` (agent `POST /v1/federation/rodc-lost/<site>`, `federation:admin`). Listing is audited
too.

1.9.8.21.6 **Idempotent.** Each step is recorded as it is done; a run after a failure carries on and never resets an
account twice (accounts listed earlier stay in the record, so the list survives the DC's removal); a finished run says
so and changes nothing. A later read-only DC of the same site, lost again, starts a new record (the old one's date is
kept in it).

1.9.8.21.7 **Tests** (2.3.9.3.4): `tests/federation/rodc_lost_test.py` (container-free, run: exactly the revealed
accounts listed; only them reset, by kind; refused without confirmation, from the site itself, a sibling or below,
at a read-only DC, with no writable DC answering; the record, carrying on after a failure, a finished run doing
nothing; the agent's and console's routes and the command line); `tests/samba/dc.py` (written, not run: the RODC's
revealed list read at the writable DC, an account outside it refused, the resets, the removal, the site left with no
DC).

## 1.9.8.22 Changing a site's DC type, requested from above (0.9)

Decision 2.1.9.23 (the owner, 2026-10-13). Built for 0.9, tests written, not run (2.3.9.3). A site that has grown
(more people, device management, roles of its own) gets a writable DC; one that shrinks, a read-only one. Central IT
decides at a site above; the site's own admin carries it out, with its consent.

1.9.8.22.1 **The request, at a site above** (`federation/request_dc_type`, `fabricctl federation dc-type <site>
writable|rodc`, as root on that site's fabric host):
- **Checked first, nothing written when refused:** the type is `writable` or `rodc` (`none` is not built); the site
  is in the domain and **below** this one (its OU inside this site's OU, 1.9.8.14): this site itself, a sibling or a
  site above is refused, naming where it sits; it is not already of that type (judged from AD's configuration
  partition: the DCs of its AD site and whether each is read-only, `userAccountControl`'s `PARTIAL_SECRETS_ACCOUNT`,
  directory operation `read_site_dcs`); this site's DC is writable (it prepares the join account); this site's
  replication is healthy (below); the federation endpoint is on (the token is redeemed there).
- **Written:** a `serviceConnectionPoint`, `CN=fabric-dc-type-request`, in the site's OU (directory operation
  `set_dc_type_request`, as this site's agent, whose inherited rights reach the site's OU): its `keywords` hold
  `fabric-dc-type-request`, `to=<type>`, `by=<this site>`, `actor=<who>`, `at=<UTC time>`, `id=<token id>`. A
  second request replaces the first (and its token).
- **The token:** `fabric-dctype-1.<base64url JSON>` with the token's id and secret, the site, the type, this site's
  federation host name and address, the root's SHA-256 fingerprint and when it expires (one hour). Kept in fabric's
  secrets as `federation_invitations[id]` with `kind: dc-type` (a hash of the secret only). Shown once; the top's
  admin sends it to the site's admin over a channel they trust. Audited `FED_DC_TYPE_REQUEST`.

1.9.8.22.2 **At the site** (`federation/change_dc_type`, `fabricctl federation dc-type show|accept`):
- `show`: the request (type, by, who, when) read at this site's own DC (`read_dc_type_request`), and this site's
  current type; nothing pending says so.
- `accept [@FILE|-] [--yes]`: the plan (below) is printed; then consent: `--yes`, or a typed `yes` at the prompt
  (never a default; refused without a terminal and without `--yes`). The token is read from the file or standard
  input or a prompt, never from the command line. Checked before anything changes, each refusal saying why:
  a request in AD and a token for this site and the same type; the token's root fingerprint is the pinned root's;
  **a writable DC answers** (the list of 2.1.6.57, `samba/writable_server`; for a change to read-only, the same list,
  which must name a DC other than this one); **replication is healthy** (`samba/replication_healthy`: this DC's
  inbound partners all succeeded within the last hour with no failures; a DC with no partner is unhealthy); a change to
  read-only refused while this DC holds an FSMO role.
- **The steps**, each recorded in `<config>/dc-type-change.json` (root, 0600) as done, so a second run goes on from
  the first not done:
  1. *token*: POST `/v1/dc-type` at the top's endpoint (TLS verified against the pinned root, as a join is): the top
     checks the token (one message for unknown, used, expired or wrong), that its request is still in AD with the
     same type, makes the temporary join account (the converge's join account alone, one hour, Domain Admins, 1.9.8.4)
     and answers `{join_user, join_password, dc_host, dc_address, to}`; the token is used up. The account goes into
     fabric's secrets (`ad_join`), never onto a command line.
  2. *demote*: `samba-tool domain demote --server=<writable DC>` in this site's DC container, with the join account's
     credentials file (`samba/secrets/join.auth`, 0600, mounted read-only). A demote that fails stops here, saying
     why; run `accept` again once it is fixed.
  3. *leftover*: the demoted DC's computer account (moved to `CN=Computers` by the demote) deleted at the writable DC
     as the join account (`ldbdel`, while the container still runs), so the join can take the same name; none left is
     no error.
  4. *set aside*: the DC stopped, `samba/data` moved to `samba/data.before-dc-type-<UTC time>` (0700; kept, never put
     back into service).
  5. *join*: `ad_dc_type` set to the new type (and `ad_join_server`, `ad_join_server_name` to the writable DC that
     answered, if they were empty); then `fabricctl setup --yes` (unattended), whose DC starts and joins (no
     provisioned data, a join account in the secrets: setup's join path, `JOIN_ROLE`), `allow_rodc_cache` for a
     read-only DC and `finish_join` deleting the join account.
  6. *done*: the request removed from AD (now at the site's writable DC, or routed to one), audited
     `FED_DC_TYPE_CHANGED`.
- **Idempotent:** with the site already of the requested type, `accept` removes the request and changes nothing else.
- **Undo** is the reverse change, requested from above the same way; a demoted DC is never restored from its old
  database (AD has removed it). A refusal before step 2 changes nothing.
- **Meanwhile** the site's services use the writable DC (1.9.8.12): reads and writes over the link.

1.9.8.22.3 **What follows a promotion to writable:** writes stay local (`writable_server` follows `ad_dc_type`);
Group Policy and per-OU log-on edits are allowed again; the DC converges the site's OU and GPOs as every writable
DC does. `<site>-rodc-cache` is kept with its members (nothing reads it; the hourly timer is installed only at a
read-only DC's site); the fabric host's account is unchanged. **A change to read-only** re-runs the join's allow list
and preload (1.6.11.13, 1.6.11.14) and routes writes to the list (1.6.11.13.1).

1.9.8.22.4 **The console** (Federation tab, `federation:read`): a site's DC type in the sites table (from the
registry); at this site, a pending request (type, by, who, when, read from AD by `federation_overview`) and the
commands to accept it. **Requesting from the console** (2.1.9.23 revised 2026-10-14, settling 2.3.9.3.5 point 1):
with `federation:admin`, a form under the sites table (a site that joined here, the type) posts to
`/federation/dc-type` after a sign-in from the last 5 minutes; the console calls the agent's
`POST /v1/federation/dc-type/<site>` (`request_dc_type`, the same checks as the command, audited from `web`) and shows
the change token on the answering page only — never in a URL, never kept by the console — with how to withdraw it
(`fabricctl federation revoke <id>`). Without the permission the tab names the command, as before. Accepting stays at
the site, on its fabric host.

1.9.8.22.5 **Tests** (2.3.9.3.3): `tests/federation/dc_type_test.py` (container-free, run: the request refused from
the site itself, a sibling and a site below and for `none`, an unhealthy replication, a read-only top, the type the
site has; the token's checks, a change token refused as a join invitation and the reverse; the plan's refusals with
no writable DC answering, each one named, replication unhealthy and an FSMO role; the steps resumed from the record;
the command's and its questions' positive and negative cases); `tests/samba/dc.py` (the `samba` suite, written, not
run: the request object written from above, read at the site, refused from below by AD, removed by the site). The
whole promotion with real DCs waits for a lab with two DCs of one site (2.3.9.3.5).
