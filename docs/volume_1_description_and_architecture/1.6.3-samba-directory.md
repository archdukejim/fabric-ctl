## 1.6.3.2 What the directory is

One Samba AD domain is fabric's only directory (2.1.6.6). It holds everything 389-DS holds today — people, groups,
POSIX ids, devices and their roles, the address plan — plus what only AD gives: Windows machine accounts, Group
Policy, Kerberos for Linux, the NT hashes PEAP-MSCHAPv2 needs, one password per person. Every site runs a domain
controller (DC) of the type it chose — writable, read-only or none (S2 revised, 2.1.6.7) — and the full stack beside
it (2.1.9.9).

## 1.6.3.3 The domain and its name

- The **AD domain** is chosen at setup on the first node (the root site), with no default, and is permanent (2.1.6.11).
  The installer suggests a **protected sibling** at the top of the organisation's name, apart from the sites'
  names: `ad.` plus the domain without its first label (`lan.j-j.family` → `ad.j-j.family`). A two-label domain has
  no parent to sit beside, so it gets a sub-domain: `ad.` plus the domain (`home.arpa` → `ad.home.arpa`; owner
  2026-10-08, so Enter always takes a suggestion; until 0.6.1 it got none). Any domain will do except fabric's own or a parent of it — **same-domain mode was dropped** (2.1.6.11
  revised, 2026-10-04), so fabric's zones and the AD zone never share a name; a sub-domain (`ad.lan.j-j.family`)
  stays allowed. Sites get the domain from their invitation.
- Realm = the domain upper-cased; the NetBIOS name is its first label (at most 15 characters, settable once).
- The base DN follows from the domain (`DC=ad,DC=example,DC=org`); `ldap_base_dn` (M1b, F10) is no longer a
  setting.
- One domain, one forest: Samba has no child domains (Q5). Sites are OUs.

## 1.6.3.4 Layout: everything under OU=sites

Everything fabric manages lives under `OU=sites`, one OU per site (2.1.6.12). AD's own containers (`CN=Users`,
`CN=Computers`, `CN=Builtin`, `OU=Domain Controllers`) are left as AD makes them; AD's defaults for new users
and new computers (`redirusr`, `redircmp`) point at the root site's `OU=people` and `OU=machines`, so nothing
created by Windows' own tools lands outside `OU=sites`.

```
DC=ad,DC=example,DC=org
└── OU=sites
    ├── OU=lan                      root site
    │   ├── OU=organisation         organisation-wide (only in the root site's OU)
    │   │   ├── CN=dhcp-options     the organisation's DHCP options, definitions, classes (2.1.10.23)
    │   │   ├── OU=groups           fabric-admins, the RBAC bundle groups, fabric-break-glass, shared groups,
    │   │   │                       fabric-dhcp-org-editors (the sites that may edit the organisation's DHCP options)
    │   │   ├── OU=device-roles     roles any site may use
    │   │   └── OU=sudoers          sudo rules for every site
    │   ├── OU=people               this site's people
    │   ├── OU=groups               <site>-users, <site>-admins, the four role groups (2.1.9.13), <site>-<role> (2.1.9.11),
    │   │                           <site>-fabric-admins, <site>-fabric-machines (1.6.11.2),
    │   │                           <site>-server-remote-users, <site>-desktop-remote-users (1.6.11.15)
    │   ├── OU=machines             domain-joined computers; a Windows one that joined itself lands here first
    │   │   ├── OU=servers          servers: <site>-server-remote-users may sign in remotely (1.6.11.15)
    │   │   │   ├── OU=linux        Linux machines: the join places them by class (1.6.11.5)
    │   │   │   └── OU=windows      Windows machines: moved here once by fabric-machines.timer (1.6.11.5)
    │   │   └── OU=desktops         desktops: <site>-desktop-remote-users may sign in remotely (1.6.11.15)
    │   │       ├── OU=linux
    │   │       └── OU=windows
    │   ├── OU=fabric-hosts         the site's fabric host (the appliance), a machine fabric manages (1.6.11.11)
    │   ├── OU=devices              802.1X devices (fabric's classes)
    │   ├── OU=device-roles         this site's own roles
    │   ├── OU=networks             this site's part of the address plan (1.10.2.8)
    │   ├── CN=dhcp-options         this site's DHCP options, definitions, classes (1.10.4.24)
    │   ├── OU=sudoers              this site's sudo rules
    │   ├── OU=service-accounts     this site's fabric-agent, Keycloak and FreeRADIUS accounts
    │   └── OU=lab                  a site whose parent is lan: the same, without OU=organisation (2.1.6.20)
    │       └── OU=lab2             a site nested under lab
```

- **A site's OU is in its parent's OU** (revised 2026-10-05, 2.1.6.20; was: every site's OU one level under
  `OU=sites`). The root's OU is the only one directly under `OU=sites`; the parent is also recorded on the OU
  (`fabricParentSite`), and re-parenting moves the OU
  ([1.9.8.14](1.9.8-federation-on-ad.md#19814-nesting-and-inheritance)).
- A search that means one site's objects starts at that site's own sub-OU (`OU=people`, `OU=devices`, ...), never
  at the site's OU with a subtree scope, which would take in the sites below it.
- Site names are unique and never `organisation`.
- The site's AD Group Policy objects are linked to its OU (logon rights, 1.6.3.10); the trust GPO is linked to
  the domain.

## 1.6.3.5 fabric's schema in AD

fabric's own attributes and classes (devices, device roles, networks, sites: today's 11 attributes and 4 classes,
plus `fabricParentSite` and the per-site id block of 1.6.3.9, and from 0.7.0 the ranges' marks `fabricIdMark` in
the auxiliary class `fabricIdLayout`, 1.6.3.17) are added to AD's schema (Q3):

- OIDs under Microsoft's arc for unregistered extensions, derived from fabric's UUID:
  `1.2.840.113556.1.8000.2554.39383.57305.1842.18547.37174.2331831.2413377` (AD refuses the `2.25.<UUID>` arc).
- Loaded in two sessions (attributes, then the classes using them); every structural class carries a
  `defaultSecurityDescriptor` granting authenticated users read (Q9).
- sudo's schema with sudo's own registered OIDs (`1.3.6.1.4.1.15953.9`); Ubuntu no longer ships it.
- A schema extension is permanent: fabric adds, never changes or removes; a new attribute is a new OID.
- Devices are `device` + `ieee802Device` + fabric's classes, the MAC in `macAddress`; device roles are AD groups
  with the `fabricRole` class (members are device objects; `fabricRoleName` on devices stays, M1).
- From 0.9, SSH host certificates (decision 2.1.7.13, 1.6.11.9.7): `fabricSshHostKey` (37) and `fabricSshHostCert`
  (38), IA5 and multi-valued, in the auxiliary class `fabricSshHost` (14) (35, 36 and 13 are 0.8.0's scan
  policy, 1.10.4.27; renumbered 2026-10-13 before any release, 2.1.7.13), added by fabric to each managed machine's
  computer account. A machine writes its own `fabricSshHostKey` only (SELF, below `OU=machines`, 1.6.3.6).

## 1.6.3.6 Who may write what: delegation per site

AD limits **who** writes, not which DC takes the write (Q12). The per-site limits of S2 are therefore limits on
accounts:

| Account or group | May write | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Domain Admins, the Administrator | everything | Used only at the root site, by setup and joins; the Administrator's password is in the root's OpenBao. No person is in Domain Admins by default |
| `fabric-agent-<site>` (each site's agent) | full control below `OU=<site>`, except `OU=service-accounts`; create and link GPOs for its own OU | read everywhere else |
| `fabric-agent-<root>` | as above, plus `OU=organisation`, the domain-linked trust GPO and the password policy | |
| `<site>-admins` (people) | since 0.9 the site helpdesk only: add people in the site's `OU=people`, reset their passwords, unlock, disable or enable them, put them in `<site>-users`; never a person who holds a role ([1.6.3.18](#16318-layered-roles-in-the-directory-09)). Until 0.9: a member of the four role groups below by default | 2.1.9.21 (revises 2.1.9.13) |
| `<site>-ou-admins` | the site's OU: people, groups, sub-OUs (not `OU=service-accounts`); since 0.9 empty by default, granted from a site above, and never the members of its own site's role groups (1.6.3.18) | 2.1.9.13, 2.1.9.21 |
| `<site>-machine-admins` | the site's `OU=machines` and `OU=devices` | 2.1.9.13 |
| each computer account below `OU=machines` (SELF) | its own `fabricSshHostKey` only: the SSH host keys it publishes for fabric to sign (1.6.11.9.7) | 2.1.7.13 |
| `<site>-gpo-admins` | the site's two admin GPOs (admin settings, controls) and their SYSVOL folders; the site's OU's `gPLink` and `gPOptions` (its links and blocked inheritance; a parent's enforced link still wins) | 2.1.9.13 |
| `<site>-linux-sudo` | **[DELETED]** replaced in 0.9 by `<site>-fabric-admins` (1.6.11.3, 2.1.6.37): its members are moved there on upgrade | 2.1.9.13 |
| `<site>-fabric-admins`, `fabric-admins` (people) | nothing in AD: the site's `sudoRole` (`CN=<site>-fabric-admins`) lets them run any command as root on the site's Linux machines and those of the sites below (1.6.11) | 2.1.6.37 |
| `<site>-windows-admins` | nothing in AD: the site's log-on GPO makes the group a member of each Windows machine's local Administrators (Restricted Groups "member of": added, never replacing) | 2.1.9.13 |
| Keycloak's account | people and groups of its site (writable sign-in, password changes) | |
| FreeRADIUS's account | read only | |

- **Overrides** (S2's "listed limits, overrides in the Federation tab"): an override adds an ACL granting a site's
  agent and admins rights on another site's OU (all of it, or one kind: people, devices, machines) or on
  `OU=organisation`. Each override is listed with who set it and when; removing it removes the ACL. Only the
  root's admins (`federation:admin`) set overrides.
- ACLs are written with `samba-tool dsacl`, container-inherited (Q4), and are checked by every DC, offline too (Q12).
  Because a site's OU is in its parent's (2.1.6.20), a parent's agent and roles reach every site below it, except its
  service accounts (the parent's principals are denied there too). No site's admins or agent may change
  permissions or owners (`WD`, `WO` are left out of their rights; fabric's convergence sets ACLs as the system),
  so a child can neither remove an inherited ACE nor protect its OU from inheritance.
- The web UI never writes as the person: it asks fabric-agent, which checks the person's permissions (RBAC,
  1.6.8) and writes with its site's account, so a site's web UI cannot exceed its site's limits.
- **A writable DC holds every secret of the domain**; a read-only DC only those it may cache (1.9.8.3). The
  limits above stop a site's people and services, not someone who controls a writable DC's host.

## 1.6.3.7 Service accounts and secrets

- AD users in each site's `OU=service-accounts`: `fabric-agent-<site>`, `fabric-keycloak-<site>`,
  `fabric-radius-<site>`. Random passwords, generated per install, kept in that site's OpenBao and delivered to
  containers as files (2.1.7.3); never on a command line. 389-DS's seven role accounts are not carried over (Q11).
- `fabric-sso-<site>` (0.6.1, 2.3.6.2.6.2): Keycloak's Kerberos sign-in account, made by the DC's converge (`ensure_sso_account.py`) with the SPNs `HTTP/<hostname_keycloak>` and `HTTP/<hostname>.<domain>`, AES keys only (`msDS-SupportedEncryptionTypes` 24: Java refuses RC4 tickets) and a random password that never leaves the DC. Its keytab is exported to the DC's `/data/sso.keytab` and copied to Keycloak. None at a read-only DC.
- The Administrator's password: random at provisioning, set from a file (Q1), kept in the root site's OpenBao.
  Provisioning's own output is discarded (it prints a temporary password).
- The DC does not need OpenBao to start (the core-services rule, 1.2.8): its own secrets are in its database.

## 1.6.3.8 Passwords

- **The policy is the admin's** (2.1.6.13): minimum length, complexity, history, minimum and maximum age, and the
  lockout threshold, duration and window. A new domain starts from fabric's default (2.1.6.35: 12 characters,
  complexity, 5 remembered, never expires, locked for 15 minutes after 10 failures in 15), not asked at setup
  (2.1.2.16); a vars file may set any of it; changed later with `fabricctl domain password-policy` (`domain:admin`). fabric writes them as the domain's policy
  (`samba-tool domain passwordsettings`).
- **One-time passwords** fabric generates (a new person, a reset) satisfy the policy and must be
  changed at the next sign-in.
- **The old-password window:** AD accepts a previous password for network sign-ins for 60 minutes after a change
  (Q7). Proposed: 0 (2.1.6.15).
- Where a password is set — Keycloak, Windows (Ctrl-Alt-Del), `passwd` on a joined Linux machine, a reset in the
  web UI — it is set in AD; there is no second store and nothing to synchronise.

## 1.6.3.9 POSIX identities

- People and groups carry RFC 2307 attributes (`--use-rfc2307`): `uidNumber`, `gidNumber`, `homeDirectory`,
  `loginShell`, assigned by fabric as today (1.6.6 step 1), never reused.
- **Each site has its own id block** (2.1.6.17), handed out by its parent at join (`fabricIdRange` on the site's OU;
  block size `posix_id_block`, default 100 000), and a high-water mark in its OU. The parent takes the block after
  every one its DC holds (each site OU's, replicated), so a nested parent never repeats another branch's; two
  parents handing out blocks while cut off from each other could (not detected yet: 2.3.6.3). Writable
  site DCs cut off from the root then never assign an id another site uses.
- fabric's everyone-group `users` becomes AD's **Domain Users** with fabric's gid (5000) (Q11).

## 1.6.3.10 Access is authorized per site

People and machines are known across the domain; **access is granted per site** (2.1.6.14):

- Every person created in a site's `OU=people` is a member of `<site>-users`. Default: a site's people may log on
  only to that site's machines, join that site's network with 802.1X, and sign in to that site's web services.
- **Log-on rights:** a GPO per site (`<site> logon rights`, linked to its OU) grants interactive and remote log-on
  to `<site>-users`, `<site>-admins`, the `windows-admins` role and the fabric-admins of the site and of every site
  above it (the `linux-sudo` role before 0.9, 1.6.11.3), the admins of the sites above it (their roles reach this one, 2.1.9.13), the organisation's admin group (`webui_admin_group`, default `admins`), **`fabric-break-glass`** and each machine's own local
  Administrators (the policy replaces Windows' local lists; added in S1.7) (so a mistake cannot lock
  everyone out; Q16), plus the grants below. Windows applies it itself; Linux machines through SSSD
  (`ad_gpo_access_control = enforcing`, 1.6.7). Since 0.9 this is the site's log-on policy only while its setting
  `logon_policies` is null (an upgraded site); a site's log-on rights are now set **per OU**, one GPO per machine OU
  with the groups allowed at the keyboard and remotely, and a fresh install grants `<site>-machine-admins` on its
  `OU=machines`: [1.6.11.4](1.6.11-host-access.md#16114-who-may-sign-in-per-ou).
- **802.1X:** FreeRADIUS at a site admits people in the groups that site allows (its `radius_people` mappings name
  site groups) and devices in its own OU, plus the grants below. Domain machines signing in with PEAP (as
  `host/<name>`) follow the same mappings: an enabled computer in the site's `OU=machines` whose groups include a
  mapped one, on that mapping's VLAN (2.1.11.3). Only groups under `OU=sites` match a mapping (plus `users`, AD's
  Domain Users).
- **Web sign-in:** each site's Keycloak admits its site's groups and the organisation's admin groups.
- **Grants** (Federation tab, `federation:admin`): explicit — a person, group or site allowed at another site — or
  **blanket** — everyone in the organisation (Domain Users) at a site. One grant changes all three (log-on GPO,
  FreeRADIUS, Keycloak) together. This replaces decision F3's replication by consent (2.1.9.12).

## 1.6.3.13 What this design changes, and its impact

The impact of the replacement as a whole was assessed and approved (2.3.6.1.4). What this design adds to it:

- **Trust between sites changes** (1.9.1.3): with 389-DS a compromised site held only read-only copies; with AD a
  writable site DC holds every password hash of the domain. The admin chooses the DC type knowing it; setup and
  the Federation tab say so. Read-only DCs keep the old property for everything but cached site passwords.
- **People are no longer created only at the root:** a site with a writable DC creates its own people offline;
  ids cannot collide (1.6.3.9).
- **Decisions changed by this design**, each for the owner (2.1.6): 2.1.9.3 becomes an access grant (2.1.9.12), F4
  (2.1.9.4) groups move into the site's OU (2.1.9.11), 2.1.9.8 is replaced by 2.1.6.11. New: 2.1.6.15 (old-password window), 2.1.6.16
  (SYSVOL), 2.1.6.17 (id blocks), 2.1.6.18 (the DC on the host network), 2.1.13.1 (Windows time), 2.1.1.31 (no migration before 1.0).

## 1.6.3.14 No migration before the 1.0 milestone

There are no users to keep updated until the 1.0 milestone release (2.1.1.31). Installs made before AD are rebuilt, not
migrated: build step S7 removes 389-DS and everything that served it, with no migration command and no upgrade path.

## 1.6.3.15 Agent routes added with Samba AD (planned)

Moved here from 1.3.3.2. Each route joins the fixed API ([1.3.3.1](1.3.3-agent-api.md#1331-fixed-api)) in the build step that builds it.

The routes of 1.3.3.1 keep their shape; from
build step S2 the people, devices, roles and DHCP-network routes are served from AD. `/v1/people/<uid>/reset`
returns a one-time password that satisfies the domain's policy (2.1.6.13). New routes and permissions:

| Method | Route | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| GET | `/v1/domain` (DC type, roles, replication, password policy) | `domain:read` |
| POST | `/v1/domain/password-policy` | `domain:admin` |
| GET | `/v1/machines` | `machines:read` |
| POST | `/v1/machines` (pre-create, one-time join password), `/v1/machines/<name>/{groups,disable,enable,delete}` | `machines:admin` |
| GET | `/v1/gpos`, `/v1/gpos/<guid>`, `/v1/admx`, `/v1/admx/<template>` | `gpo:read` |
| POST | `/v1/gpos`, `/v1/gpos/<guid>/{settings,link,unlink,delete}`, `/v1/admx` (upload a template) | `gpo:admin` |
| GET | `/v1/federation/limits`, `/v1/federation/grants` | `federation:read` |
| POST | `/v1/federation/overrides`, `/v1/federation/overrides/<id>/delete`, `/v1/federation/grants`, `/v1/federation/grants/<id>/delete` | `federation:admin` |

The new permissions join the admin bundle; `domain:read`, `machines:read` and `gpo:read` the auditor bundle;
`machines:admin` the equipment operator bundle ([1.6.8](1.6.8-rbac.md)). Each write is limited further by the site's own account
(1.6.3.6): a site's agent cannot change another site's objects whatever the caller holds.

## 1.6.3.16 Disabling, removing and regrouping people

Built 2026-10-08 for 0.6.3 (decision 2.1.6.30; design 2.3.1.11.7). The same functions serve `fabricctl people`
([4.6.1.3](../volume_4_operations/4.6.1-people.md#4613-fabricctl-people)) and the web console's People page; the
directory operations run in the DC as the site's agent (1.6.3.6), so AD's per-site access control applies too.

- **Only this site's people.** The DC's `set_person` and `remove_person` find the person in the site's own
  `OU=people` only: a person of another site, nested or parent, or a service account is "no such entry".
- **Disable** sets the account's `ACCOUNTDISABLE` flag (every sign-in through the domain is refused: Keycloak, 802.1X,
  Windows and Linux logons) and ends the person's Keycloak sessions at once (`keycloak/sign_out_person`). Their
  account, groups and certificates stay; **enable** clears the flag, and enables Keycloak's copy of the user
  (`keycloak/enable_person`): a sign-in tried while disabled makes Keycloak's AD mapper disable its own copy.
- **Remove** needs the user name typed back (the agent checks it, not only the page). Their sessions end, the account
  is deleted with its group memberships, Keycloak's copy of the user goes, and every certificate fabric issued to
  their name that is still in force is revoked (`cessationOfOperation`, the CRLs published once, 2.1.5.10). Their uid
  number is never reused (1.6.3.9).
- **Groups:** `add_group_member` / `remove_group_member` for the site's groups, and the organisation's at the root
  site. Keycloak reads a change at the person's next sign-in.
- **Guards** (`directory/common/person_guard`), the same for the CLI, the agent and the page: a member of a fabric
  group (one carrying a bundle) is disabled, removed or reset only by an admin (`system:admin`, or root on the host),
  and only an admin changes a fabric group's members; nobody disables or removes themselves; and the admin group's
  last member who can still sign in is never disabled, removed or taken out of it.
- **Audit:** `PERSON_DISABLE`, `PERSON_ENABLE`, `PERSON_REMOVE` (with the number of certificates revoked, each also
  `PKI_REVOKE`), `PERSON_GROUP_ADD`, `PERSON_GROUP_REMOVE`.
- **Permissions:** `people:disable` (in the helpdesk bundle), `people:remove` and `people:groups` (admins only):
  [1.8.2](1.8.2-webui-security.md), routes [1.3.3](1.3.3-agent-api.md).
- **Tests:** the `samba` suite (a real DC: disable refuses the sign-in, enable restores it, groups in and out once,
  removal, and refusals for a service account and another site's person); the `keycloak` suite (a live session ended
  by disable, sign-in refused and restored, removal drops Keycloak's user, the guards); the `webui` suites (the page's
  forms per bundle, the agent's 403s).

## 1.6.3.17 The ranges inside a site's id block (0.7.0)

**Designed 2026-10-11 for 0.7.0 (decision 2.1.6.46); built 2026-10-11** (as built: the end of this topic).
Revises 1.6.3.9's numbers for new installs.

- **The blocks:** each site's block is 1 000 000 ids, site *S*'s from *S* × 1 000 000 (the root is site 1:
  1000000-1999999; the next site to join 2000000-2999999, as the parent hands out the next block, 1.6.3.9). A uid or
  gid names its site at a glance. `posix_id_block` becomes 1 000 000 and the root's start 1 000 000: both permanent,
  marked so at setup (2.1.2.20).
- **At most 999 sites:** site 999's block (999000000-999999999) is the last, so every id fabric gives stays below
  1 000 000 000, well inside AD's signed 32-bit ids (to 2 147 483 647). The 1000th join is refused, with the
  reason; there is no setting to raise it. Not tested: fabric is designed and tested for 20 (1.9.1.4).
- **Fixed ranges inside every block** (offsets from its start; the round numbers between ranges stay unused):

  | Offset | Root (site 1) | Ids | For |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 000000 | 1000000 | 1 | The site's first admin (setup's first admin at the root) |
  | 000001-019999 | 1000001-1019999 | 19 999 | Admin accounts: site admins, operators' privileged identities |
  | 020001-039999 | 1020001-1039999 | 19 999 | Service accounts: fabric's own per-site domain accounts (`fabric-agent-<site>`, `fabric-keycloak-<site>`, `fabric-radius-<site>`) and service identities admins make |
  | 040001-049999 | 1040001-1049999 | 9 999 | fabric's built-in groups: the organisation's (`admins`, `auditors`, the operator bundles, `network-staff`, `network-guests`, `users`) in the root's, each site's own (`<site>-admins`, `<site>-users`, `<site>-linux-sudo`, `<site>-fabric-machines`...) in its own |
  | 050001-099999 | 1050001-1099999 | 49 999 | Groups admins make |
  | 100001-899999 | 1100001-1899999 | 799 999 | People |
  | 900001-949999 | 1900001-1949999 | 49 999 | Guests and temporary accounts: found and expired by their range |
  | 950001-999999 | 1950001-1999999 | 49 999 | Reserved for later (machines' POSIX ids, should NFS ever need them) |

  Accounts take uids from their range, groups gids from theirs; everyone keeps `users` as primary group. The offsets
  are fabric's, not settings: every site alike, an id readable anywhere. A range that fills is refused, with which.
- **Clear of the ids Linux reserves:** below 1 000 000 are the local users' and groups' (1000-60000 on Ubuntu),
  systemd's dynamic users (61184-65519), `nobody` (65534), 65535, and the subordinate ids rootless Podman and Docker
  give local users (`/etc/subuid`, `/etc/subgid`: from 100 000, 65 536 each, so past 1 000 000 only after 14 local
  users). fabric's organisation-wide groups, until now at fixed gids 1100-1121 and 5000 (inside the local range: a
  joined machine with enough local accounts would clash), move into the root's built-in range.
- **Subordinate ids, the safety net:** they are not stored in AD, so the Linux join (1.6.7) sets `SUB_UID_MIN` and
  `SUB_GID_MIN` in `/etc/login.defs` above 2 147 483 647 for local users made after the join, and warns, naming them,
  about local users whose ranges already overlap a block (never remapped: that breaks their container storage).
  systemd-nspawn's convention for container ids (524288-1879048191) overlaps most sites, as sssd's and FreeIPA's ids
  do: noted, not avoided.
- **Existing installs keep their numbers** (the root's block from 5001, the groups at 1100-1121 and 5000); new
  installs from 0.7.0 use this layout. The unused `hosts` range (`uid_range: 2000-4900`, from 389-DS) goes, and the
  users OU's `uid_range` stops being read. *Revised 2026-10-13 (2.1.6.51, stale code S10):* there is no install from
  before 0.7.0 (the owner, 2026-10-12); the settings upgrade that kept its numbers (`keep_old_install`) is deleted, so
  every block starts at site 1's. The DC-side one-run handing out of an older block and the template's tolerance of
  an older vars file's groups and `hosts` OU stay, registered as stale code S11. *Revised again 2026-10-13 (S11
  done):* the one-run handing out is deleted too. A block must be a site's, a million ids from a multiple of a
  million: `posix_id_start` must be a multiple of 1000000 and `posix_id_block` 1000000 (refused before an apply
  renders anything), and the DC's converge refuses any other block before it writes anything
  (`id_layout.check_block`). The template still keeps the gids an admin lists for fabric's groups. *Revised
  2026-10-13 (2.1.6.54):* 0.8.0 keeps this refusal; an install with an older block is installed fresh, and each
  refusal says so, with why (no migration before 1.0) and where the steps are
  ([3.3.5](../volume_3_installation/3.3.5-upgrading-to-080.md)).
- **Tests:** render (the root's block, the ranges); the samba suite: a person, an admin, a service account, groups
  and a guest each in their range, a full range refused, the 1000th block refused; site_join: the second site's
  block 2000000-2999999 with the same ranges; linux_join: `login.defs` set, an overlapping local user named.
- **As built:**
  - Settings: `posix_id_start` (1000000) and `posix_id_block` (1000000), both in the vars editor's `IMMUTABLE_KEYS`
    and so marked permanent (setup asks neither: they have no question to mark). The users OU's `uid_range` and the
    `hosts` OU are gone from the template; an install from before 0.7.0 gets `posix_id_start` from its users OU's
    `uid_range` (5001) and keeps `posix_id_block` 100000 and its groups' gids (`deploy/keep_old_install.py`, the same
    step that keeps its DC's name; *deleted 2026-10-13, S10: no such install exists*). A joining site takes the organisation's block size and its groups (with their
    gids) from its parent's answer, so its people get the domain's `users` gid.
  - The organisation's groups: admins 1040001, auditors 1040002, the operator bundles 1040003-1040006, network-staff
    1040007, network-guests 1040008, users (Domain Users) 1040009. **The first hundred of the built-in range
    (040001-040100) are kept for fabric's fixed gids**, so the handed-out built-in gids (`<site>-users`, the role
    groups, `fabric-break-glass`) start at 040101 in every block, and the root converges the organisation's groups
    before its own. A choice made while building (the design gave the range, not where fixed gids sit in it).
  - In the directory: a laid-out block (a million ids from a multiple of a million) carries one high-water mark per
    range, `fabricIdMark` "<kind>=<next>" in a new auxiliary class `fabricIdLayout` (new OIDs: 1.6.3.5's rule), set
    by converge; `alloc_id(kind)` moves the kind's mark with one conditional change and skips a number someone already
    has. An older block (from 5001) keeps its one run, `fabricIdNext`, for every kind. *Revised 2026-10-13 (S11):*
    no longer; every block is laid out, any other is refused (`check_block`), and `fabricIdNext` is no longer written
    (the schema keeps the attribute: AD never removes one). Service accounts get their uid in every block.
  - Who takes what: setup's first admin `first_admin` (the block's first id; a later first admin an admin's), people
    `person`, the site's groups and break-glass `builtin_group`, the site's service accounts `service` (they now get
    a uid, primary gid `users`, shell `/usr/sbin/nologin`; only in a laid-out block). `admin`, `guest` and `group`
    are taken through the directory operation `create_person` (`kind`) and tested, but **no command or page offers
    them yet**: making admin accounts, guests or admins' groups from the console is not designed.
  - The 1000th block: `next_id_block` refuses a block past 999999999 (the invitation is kept).
  - The Linux join sets `SUB_UID_MIN`/`SUB_GID_MIN` 2147483648 and `SUB_UID_MAX`/`SUB_GID_MAX` 4294000000 (a maximum
    is needed: below the minimum `useradd` would fail), reads every site's block from the DC, and warns about each
    `/etc/subuid` or `/etc/subgid` range that overlaps one, changing none.

## 1.6.3.18 Layered roles in the directory (0.9)

**Decision 2.1.9.21 (owner, 2026-10-13), a design modification of 2.1.9.13; built 2026-10-13, not yet run against a
DC (2.3.9.2).** The console side is [1.6.8.2](1.6.8-rbac.md#1682-the-site-helpdesk-and-roles-granted-from-above);
granting from above and the Federation tab are [1.9.8.20](1.9.8-federation-on-ad.md#19820-layered-roles-and-sibling-isolation).

The owner's model: "a traditional company layout of a main IT department managing all site installs, device
lifecycle, while a local admin might just do user unlocks" — layered permissions, a site expanded when it grows.

1.6.3.18.1 **Who holds what.**

- *Central IT* is the organisation's groups. In AD, the organisation's admin group (`webui_admin_group`, default
  `admins`) is kept a member of the root site's four AD role groups, which reach every site below (2.1.6.20): its
  rights are what they were, now through the role groups instead of `<root>-admins`.
- A site's `<site>-admins` are its **helpdesk**. Convergence keeps them out of the site's AD role groups
  (`ou-admins`, `machine-admins`, `gpo-admins`, `windows-admins`) and its host admins (`<site>-fabric-admins`,
  where 0.9's move from `linux-sudo` had put them), unless a site above granted that role to them
  (`narrow_site_admins`; a granted group carries the mark `fabric: granted to the site's admins (2.1.9.21)` in its
  `adminDescription`, so a grant is never undone). Convergence also keeps them members of `<site>-users` (revision
  of 2026-10-13), so Keycloak, which admits the site's users (1.6.8), admits its admins too.
- A site above may grant a site below any AD role, or a console role (1.6.8.2.3), to `<site>-admins` or to any other
  group or person ([1.9.8.20](1.9.8-federation-on-ad.md#19820-layered-roles-and-sibling-isolation)). The site itself
  cannot.

1.6.3.18.2 **The helpdesk's rights, exactly** (`ensure_site_acl`, on the site's `OU=people`, for `<site>-admins`):

| Entry | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `(OA;CI;CC;user;;<site>-admins)` on `OU=people` | create user objects (their attributes set in the same add) | add a person |
| `(OA;CIIO;CR;User-Force-Change-Password;user;…)` | reset a password (control access right `00299570-…`) | reset |
| `(OA;CIIO;RPWP;lockoutTime;user;…)` | read and write `lockoutTime` | unlock |
| `(OA;CIIO;RPWP;userAccountControl;user;…)` | read and write `userAccountControl` | disable, enable, and enable a new person |
| `(OA;CIIO;RPWP;pwdLastSet;user;…)` | read and write `pwdLastSet` | "must change at the next sign-in" |
| `(OA;;RPWP;member;;…)` on `CN=<site>-users` | the members of `<site>-users` | a new person joins it |

The entries name attributes and classes by their `schemaIDGUID`, read from the schema. They sit on the site's own
`OU=people` only: a sub-site's people live in the sub-site's own OU, so the helpdesk is its own site's only ("3 b").
No delete: removing a person stays Central IT's.

1.6.3.18.3 **People who hold a role are out of reach** (`protect_privileged_people`): on each person of the site's
`OU=people` who is a member, nested too, of a fabric group (the console's admin group and the bundle groups,
`fabric-admins`, `fabric-break-glass`), of Domain Admins, Schema Admins, Enterprise Admins or the built-in
Administrators, or of any site's group whose name ends in `-admins`, `-operators`, `-helpdesk` or `-auditors`,
convergence puts an explicit deny for `<site>-admins` of those four rights (`(OD;;CR;…)`, `(OD;;WP;…)`); AD's
explicit deny wins over the inherited allow. The denies are taken off a person who no longer holds a role. So a local
admin can neither take over nor lock out Central IT, another site's admins or each other.

1.6.3.18.4 **A branch cannot grant itself** (revised 2026-10-13: the branch's own agent too). Below the root, on
each of the site's grantable role groups (the AD roles, `<site>-fabric-admins`, the console roles) and on
`<site>-admins`, explicit denies keep the site's own principals out: `<site>-admins` may not write `member`;
`<site>-ou-admins` and the site's own `fabric-agent-<site>` may write nothing on them — not `member`, not their name,
not their permissions or owner, and not delete them (`ROLE_GROUP_DENY` = `WPSDDTWDWO`). So whoever is root on a
branch host cannot grant through the agent's credentials either; grants come only from a site above's agent, whose
inherited rights are untouched. The agent keeps its other per-site rights (full control of its OU, unchanged:
2.1.9.21), and still writes `<site>-users` (a new person joins it) and its other groups.

- *Delete and make again.* The agent's right to delete its OU's children still reaches a role group (AD lets
  delete-child on the parent stand in for delete on the object). A group made that way is owned by the branch
  principal that made it, while fabric's are owned by the domain's administrators (the system's convergence) or by
  a site above's agent (a console role it granted). Convergence refuses to give a role's rights to a role group owned
  by the site's own agent, OU admins or admins (`_check_owners`: the site's convergence stops, and from a site above
  the refusal is reported for that site), and a grant from a site above deletes it and makes it again, empty
  (`grant_site_role`), as does a remake from a site above, for any of them and for `<site>-admins` (`remake_site_group`,
  2.3.9.2.8; operator steps in 4.9.1.2).
- *What fabric itself writes at a branch.* Nothing fabric does at a branch changes those groups' members with the
  branch agent's account: the groups are made, and a new site's `<site>-fabric-admins` put in its
  `<site>-machine-admins` (2.1.6.38), by convergence as the system — at the site's creation on the parent's DC
  (`prepare_site`, answering the join) and later on the site's own DC where it is writable; the narrowing and the
  denies by the convergence of the site above (`_sites_below`); grants by a site above's agent. The console's
  group form at a branch says so before asking AD (`directory/common/granted_from_above`).
- *A writable DC is not a branch's to hold lightly.* Root on a host whose DC is writable can change the domain's
  database directly, below every access entry; this rule binds a branch through its agent's account, so it holds
  fully at a site whose DC is read-only (2.3.9.2.7).

1.6.3.18.5 **AD rights before and after** (at a site that is not the root; "inherited" reaches the sites below):

| Principal | Before 0.9 | From 0.9 |
|---|---|---|
| `<site>-admins` | member of `ou-admins`, `machine-admins`, `gpo-admins`, `windows-admins` (and of `<site>-fabric-admins` after 0.9's move from `linux-sudo`): full control of the site's OU and machines, its GPO links, local Administrators, root on its Linux hosts | its own `OU=people` only: add, reset, unlock, disable or enable people who hold no role; `<site>-users` members; a member of `<site>-users`; nothing else unless granted |
| `<site>-ou-admins` | full control below the site's OU (inherited); `<site>-admins` in it | the same, empty by default, granted from above; may not change its own site's role groups' members |
| `<site>-machine-admins` | full control of `OU=machines`, `OU=devices` | the same, empty by default (a new site's `<site>-fabric-admins` still starts in it, 2.1.6.38) |
| `<site>-gpo-admins` | the OU's `gPLink`, `gPOptions`, the site's admin GPOs | the same, empty by default |
| `<site>-windows-admins` | local Administrators on the site's Windows machines | the same, empty by default |
| `<site>-fabric-admins` | root on the site's Linux hosts (sudo, 1.6.11) | the same; `<site>-admins` no longer in it unless granted |
| `<site>-network-operators`, `-equipment-operators`, `-pki-operators`, `-helpdesk`, `-auditors` | did not exist | empty groups; nothing in AD; their bundle in the site's console when granted |
| the organisation's admin group (Central IT) | AD rights only for people also in `<root>-admins` | member of the root's four AD role groups: every site, inherited |
| `fabric-agent-<site>` | as 1.6.3.6 | the same, except (below the root) no write on its own site's role groups, console role groups or `<site>-admins` (revision of 2026-10-13, 1.6.3.18.4) |
| `fabric-keycloak-<site>`, `fabric-radius-<site>` | as 1.6.3.6 | unchanged |

1.6.3.18.6 **Upgrade.** Nothing released depended on the old default (2.1.1.31). At the first convergence of 0.9,
each site's admins are narrowed and the change list says so per group (`group lab-ou-admins: lab-admins removed
(2.1.9.21: …)`). Every convergence also applies this to the sites below it, so a site whose own DC is read-only, or
not yet upgraded, is narrowed from above; a site below that cannot take it is reported, never fatal. `fabricctl
federation roles` marks a role still held through the old default as pending until then.

1.6.3.18.7 **An AD role granted at a site reaches the sites below it** (revision of 2026-10-13, the owner's choice (b)
of 2.3.9.2.5; 2.1.6.20). How each role gets there, exactly:

| Role group of a site above | How it reaches a site below |
|---|---|
| `<above>-ou-admins` | inheritance: its entry `(A;CI;FULL;;;…)` sits on the site above's own OU, which holds the OUs of the sites below (container-inherit) |
| `<above>-machine-admins` | its own entry sits on the site above's `OU=machines` and `OU=devices`, which a site below is not in; so `ensure_site_acl` puts the same entry, `(A;CI;FULL;;;…)`, on each site below's `OU=machines` and `OU=devices`, for every site above it |
| `<above>-gpo-admins` | its `gPLink` and `gPOptions` entries carry no inheritance; `ensure_site_acl` puts them on each site below's OU too, for every site above it |
| `<above>-windows-admins` | already: each site's log-on GPOs make the windows-admins of the site and every site above it local Administrators (`ensure_logon_policies`) |
| `<above>-fabric-admins` | already: a joined Linux machine's installer reads the sudo rules of its site and of each site above (1.6.7.4) |

The entries are converged by the site's own convergence and by every site above's (`_sites_below`). A role never
reaches a site above or beside: a sibling region's entries name only its own lineage. The service accounts' denies
(1.6.3.6) still hold against every site above.

1.6.3.18.8 **An account holds a role only at its own level or below** (decision 2.1.9.24; built 2026-10-13, not yet
run against a DC). An account lives at the site whose OU holds it (the nearest site OU above its object; the domain's
own accounts, outside every site, above them all). A role lives at the site of its group: a site's role and admin
groups at that site, the organisation's fabric groups and the domain's administrators at the top (`levels.py`).

- *Checked when given.* `grant_site_role` refuses a person, or a group with any person (nested too), not living at the
  target or above it; `add_group_member` refuses a person for a group that gives a role, itself or through nesting,
  at a site they do not live at or below. The refusal names where the account must live and the move that fixes it.
- *Moving.* `move_person` (`fabricctl people move <uid> --site <site>`) renames the account into the target's
  `OU=people` and adds it to `<target>-users`; its name, password, SID, groups and POSIX ids stay. Only a site above
  both places may do it (both must be it or below it: a branch never moves anyone above itself, and AD's limits on
  its agent agree), and never below a site where the person holds a role. Audited `PERSON_MOVE`.
- *Protected.* On a person of a site's `OU=people` whose role reaches beyond that site, `protect_privileged_people`
  puts `(D;;WPCRSDDTWDWO;;;…)` for the site's own `fabric-agent-<site>` and `fabric-keycloak-<site>`: no write, no
  password reset, no delete, no permission change. Root on the branch host cannot take the account over with those
  credentials. The branch's Keycloak then cannot store a password the person sets at its sign-in page: they change it
  at a sign-in page of their own level (the top's, for Central IT).
- *Reported, never moved.* Every convergence lists such people (`misplaced: <uid> lives at <site> but holds a role at
  …`), for its own site and every site below; so do `fabricctl federation roles` and the Roles card.
