## 1.6.12.1 Status and scope

**Designed 2026-10-17 for 0.8.0, not built** (decisions 2.1.1.58, 2.1.6.62). The console's Directory entry becomes
domain management: its pages follow the page pattern and Apply per page of [1.8.6](1.8.6-console-redo.md). What the
Directory tab does today keeps working, reorganised; devices and device roles move to 802.1X (2.1.8.10).
The Overview, People, Groups, Policies, Computers and OUs are built (2026-10-23, [1.8.6.11.5 to 1.8.6.11.9](1.8.6-console-redo.md#18611-as-built));
the other pages follow.

## 1.6.12.2 Who manages what, and what stays fabric's

There are no site admins (2.1.6.68): every right is a group's, in three tiers, each managing only those below it.

| Tier | Who | May change |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise Admin | `fabric-admin` alone, in AD's own top tier; the break-glass account once the first administrator exists (2.1.6.69) | the schema and the forest's top-level configuration, the Directory Administrators, itself |
| Directory Administrators | fabric's group, outside AD's top tier; named by `fabric-admin` | everything else |
| Delegated | grants (1.6.13: who, which rights, where, by overlapping groups; the Helpdesk and Limited Directory Administrator templates unassigned), group managers (2.1.6.67), the role bundles for the other parts (1.6.8.1) | what each is given, and nothing more |

fabric-agent checks the tier and the delegations on every call, console and CLI alike, acting as its own
least-privilege account (1.6.3); AD's own containers are left alone. fabric's own objects are shown, never changed by
hand: the fixed OUs, the tier and bundle groups' scope, the fabric host's computer account, the GPOs fabric writes. The
console marks each "managed by fabric" with why, and offers no action that would change it. The Directory
Administrators hold the same rights in AD's own ACLs, so RSAT works for them too (2.1.6.76).

## 1.6.12.3 Overview

The domain at a glance: the AD domain and its NetBIOS name, the functional level, this site's DCs (writable or
read-only), which holds each of the five roles (named plainly, 1.6.5.25), replication health, the sites, and counts
(people, groups, computers, OUs) each linking to its page. **Built 2026-10-23** (1.8.6.11.5).

## 1.6.12.4 People

- **The list:** the site's people (`OU=people`) with name, user name, e-mail, state (active, disabled, locked) and last
  sign-in; search by any of them; paged (50 a page).
- **A person's page:** their facts, groups, certificates fabric issued them and the 802.1X devices they own; actions:
  **Edit** (AD's standard attributes, 2.1.6.93:
  first and last names (`givenName`, `sn`), display name (`displayName`), e-mail (`mail`), phone
  (`telephoneNumber`), mobile (`mobile`), title, department, company, office (`physicalDeliveryOfficeName`),
  city, state and country (`l`, `st`, `c`: a two-letter code), manager (a person, `manager`) and employee ID
  (`employeeID`)),
  **Reset password** (a one-time password, TOTP removed, sessions ended: as today's
  reset), **Unlock** (a lockout cleared), **Disable** / **Enable**, **Groups** (add to or take out of the site's
  groups), **Remove** (and revoke what fabric issued them: as today).
- **Add person:** as today (a one-time password), now on its own page.
- Every action is pending until People's Apply (1.8.6.5).
- **Built 2026-10-23** (1.8.6.11.5).

## 1.6.12.5 Groups

- **The list:** the site's groups (`OU=groups`) and the organisation's (read-only outside the root site), with member
  count and kind (fabric's or the site's).
- **A group's page, in two levels** (2.1.6.67):
  - **Scope and permissions:** what the group grants (a role or bundle, delegated user groups, a grant, a sudo
    rule, a device role), its managers and what they may do (add, remove, or both; adding only people already in
    another group). Shown to all who see the group; changed only by the tier above it (the Directory Administrators,
    or `fabric-admin` for the tiers' own groups).
  - **Members:** people and nested groups, added and removed by the group's managers within those controls. Any person
    or group may be a manager. A manager changes membership and nothing else.
- **Create group** and **Delete group** (`groups:admin`; a group in use by a delegation, a grant, a sudo rule
  or a device role is refused, naming where it is used).
- Group administration no longer sends people to Keycloak's console.
- **Built 2026-10-23** (1.8.6.11.6).

## 1.6.12.6 Computers

The domain's machines (today's Directory → Machines, 1.6.11), in folders by type (1.6.13.2):

- **The list:** name, type, sites, OS family, folder, machine groups, last sign-in (a date), state; search and paging,
  and filters by type and site.
- **A computer's page:** its facts, the grants that reach it and what each gives (1.6.13), its groups, the 802.1X
  device with the same name or MAC when there is one (a link); actions: **Change type** (its type group; offers to move
  it to that type's folder), **Sites** (add, remove), **Move to folder** (any OU under `OU=machines`; nobody's access
  changes), **Disable** / **Enable**, **Machine groups** (add, remove), **Remove**. Each needs a grant's machine
  rights on it, or the Directory Administrators.
- **Add computer:** as today (pre-created with a one-time join password), with its type and site.
- **Machine groups** are a page of their own under Computers: create, delete, members.
- **Built 2026-10-23** (1.8.6.11.8); the 802.1X device is matched by its name (AD keeps no MAC for a computer).

## 1.6.12.7 OUs

- **The tree** of the domain's folders (1.6.13.2): fabric's fixed OUs marked, the admin's own under them.
- **Create OU** under `OU=people`, `OU=service-accounts`, `OU=groups` and each machine type's OU; **Rename** and
  **Delete** an admin's own OU (delete refused while it holds anything, naming what).
- **An OU's page:** what it holds (counts, linked). OUs grant nothing (2.1.6.72): no policy is set by folder.
- **Built 2026-10-23** (1.8.6.11.9); of what an OU holds, only its computers are linked (People and Groups do not
  know a folder).

## 1.6.12.8 Sites

A site is a group, `site-<name>`, of its people and machines (2.1.6.72):

- **The list:** each site, its people and machines (counts, linked), its DCs (as counts: "1 DC used, 1998 can be
  added") and their replication, and the grants scoped to it.
- **A site's page:** its members (add and remove, as any group's, 2.1.6.67) and its AD site, read-only: fabric writes
  AD's sites, subnets and site links from the address plan and the federation (S8.3,
  [1.9.8](1.9.8-federation-on-ad.md)); Federation changes a site's DCs.

## 1.6.12.9 Kerberos

- **Sign-in with the domain logon** (today on Security, 2.1.6.32) moves here: on, or off (lowering stays a host action,
  as `security:raise` says today).
- **The realm:** its name and the KDCs (the DCs).
- **The ticket policy** (`domain:admin`, owner 2026-10-17: editable): the maximum ticket age (hours), the maximum
  renewal age (days) and the clock skew allowed (minutes), domain-wide, on Kerberos' Apply. Refused with why: a ticket
  age under 1 hour or over the renewal age, a renewal age over 30 days, a skew under 1 or over 15 minutes (Windows' and
  MIT's working range; a wider skew weakens replay protection). The host's command: `fabricctl domain kerberos policy`.
- **Service principals:** the SPNs of the site's computer and service accounts, read-only, with the account each is
  on; a duplicate SPN (which breaks Kerberos for both) is flagged.
- **A keytab for a service on a machine** (owner 2026-10-17: through the machine's administrator, over SSH): the console
  never holds a key. A computer's page has **Add a service** (the service class, such as `HTTP`, and the service's
  local user); it shows one command for the machine's administrator to paste into an SSH session on that machine. The
  command, run with `sudo`, uses the machine's own domain account (the join's `/etc/krb5.keytab`) to add the SPN to
  its own computer account, which AD lets a machine do for its own names, then writes the service's keytab where the
  service reads it, owned by its user, mode 0600, and prints what it did. Nothing secret is in the command; whoever
  may run `sudo` on the machine (its log-on and sudo policies, 1.6.11) is who may install the key. A machine fabric does
  not know as joined, or a name that is not the machine's, is refused by the command with why. The tool is in the
  Linux join kit (1.6.7), built on `adcli`.

## 1.6.12.10 Policies

One page per kind of policy, all on Policies' one Apply:

- **Password policy** (`domain:admin`, the Directory Administrators' bundle alone): as today (1.6.5.25), its only
  home in the console. It is the domain's default policy, for everyone **but the tiers above it** (2.1.6.71): each of
  those has an AD fine-grained password policy of its own, set by the tier above it, only on the host, never from the
  console or fabric-agent: `fabric-admin`'s (`sudo fabricctl domain password-policy --tier enterprise-admin`; 3 failed
  sign-ins, then 15 minutes, a lockout that always ends by itself, 2.1.6.69) and the Directory Administrators' on
  `directory-admins` (`--tier directory-admins`). Each starts as a copy of the default policy at the install that adds
  it (1.6.12.16.4).
- **Group Policy settings** (`gpo:admin`): the site's admin settings GPO, each set policy by its **name** and category
  (from the templates), its value, and **Clear** on its row; **Set a policy** searches the templates and opens one
  form with one way to say "not configured".
- **Log-on:** the per-OU log-on policies are retired (D1, 1.6.11.4); who signs in where is a grant's (1.6.13.4).
- **Sudo rules** (`sudo:admin`): as today (1.6.11.7).
- **Built 2026-10-23** (1.8.6.11.7), but for Group Policy shown by each policy's name, not yet built.

## 1.6.12.11 Permissions

The bundles keep theirs (1.6.8.1), under the Directory Administrators (2.1.6.68); 0.8.0 adds three and widens one
(a group's managers need none: managing is the group's own setting). On people and machines, each is held through a
grant, scoped to the people or machines it names (1.6.13.4); the bundles keep the other parts' permissions:

| Permission | Allows | Bundles |
|---|---|---|
| `people:edit` | Change a person's details (names, contact, organisation, manager; 2.1.6.93) | Admin, Helpdesk |
| `people:reset` (widened) | Also unlock a locked person | Admin, Helpdesk |
| `groups:admin` | Create and delete groups; set a group's scope and managers (level 1, 2.1.6.67) | Directory Administrators |
| `ous:admin` | Create, rename and delete the site's own OUs; move a computer between OUs (with `machines:admin`) | Admin, Equipment operator |

The list and its pages are 1.8.2's to hold; each is checked twice, by the console and by fabric-agent (1.6.8.1).

## 1.6.12.12 The agent's routes and the CLI

Each action is one fabric-agent route over a `fabricctl domain` command, so the console and the CLI never diverge
(1.8.3.1); the routes are listed in 1.3.3 when built. New commands: `fabricctl domain person edit|unlock`,
`fabricctl domain group create|delete|members`, `fabricctl domain ou list|create|rename|delete`,
`fabricctl domain machine move`, `fabricctl domain kerberos show|spns`. Each refuses, with why, what the console
refuses.

## 1.6.12.13 Tests

Against a real Samba AD DC (the samba suite) and the real agent (the webui suite), positive and negative for every
entry point:

- each action done, then seen in AD; each refused when the permission is missing, when the object is fabric's, when no
  grant reaches it, and when the input is wrong (a name AD refuses, an OU not empty, a group in use);
- every collision: a group, OU or computer name that is taken (in the site, in the domain, or by one of fabric's names,
  [1.2.13](1.2.13-name-claims.md)); a duplicate SPN flagged;
- Apply per page on each Directory page (1.8.6.9).

## 1.6.12.16 What D2 built (0.8.0)

Step D2 of 1.6.12.15 (note [2.3.6.18](../volume_2_engineering_decisions/2.3.6-identity-notes.md)):

- **1.6.12.16.1 `fabric-admin`, the Enterprise Admin** (2.1.6.68, 2.1.6.69): setup's admin step makes it at the root's
  DC, always by that name, before anyone else: in OU=people, with the DC range's first id, in Enterprise, Schema and
  Domain Admins and nothing else (not `directory-admins`, no site's group), inside the DC as the system
  (`ensure_enterprise_admin.py`), so the top tier's denies reach it at once. It is made with or without the web
  console. Its password is generated: 24 characters of upper and lower case letters, digits and only `-` `_` `.` `,`
  `:` `+` `=` `@` `^` `~`, each class at least once, from the system's CSPRNG. It goes to the DC on stdin and is
  written once to `~/fabric-admin/fabric-admin-password.txt` of the account that ran setup (folder 0700, file 0600,
  both theirs), the path said in setup's output; fabric keeps it nowhere else (not OpenBao, not the settings, not a
  log). The host records only where it wrote it (`<config>/break-glass.json`). A re-run finds the account and writes
  nothing. Unattended setup does the same.
- **1.6.12.16.2 The first Directory Administrator** (2.1.6.69, 2.1.6.93): setup asks their user name, first name,
  last name and e-mail (`webui_admin_user`, `webui_admin_first_name`, `webui_admin_last_name`, required, no default;
  `webui_admin_email` optional: Enter for none; unattended from the vars file, the password from
  `--admin-password-file`), then their password, typed twice and checked against the domain's policy. Refused, with
  why: a user name that is `fabric-admin` or another of fabric's names (1.2.13.2), an empty first or last name, an
  e-mail address given that is not one, a password the policy refuses. Keycloak's user profile does not require an
  e-mail address (configure_keycloak's `ensure_user_profile`), so a person without one signs in with no page asking
  for it. They are made in OU=people, in
  `directory-admins` and their site's group, with an admin's id, never in AD's top tier.
- **1.6.12.16.3 The reminder** (2.1.6.69): while the file is there, `fabricctl doctor` warns (not a failed check) and
  the console shows it in the top bar of every page from each sign-in of an administrator (`status:read`) and on
  Overview, which reads it afresh: fabric-agent asks `GET /v1/reminders`, worked out in a transient systemd unit
  outside the agent's sandbox (which hides the homes). Once the file is gone, its removal is audited once
  (`BREAK_GLASS_FILE_REMOVED`) and the record dropped.
- **1.6.12.16.4 The tiers' password policies** (2.1.6.69, 2.1.6.71): `ad_tier_password_policies` holds two policies,
  `enterprise_admin` and `directory_admins`, each with every key of `ad_password_policy`; setup adds them as copies of
  the default (fabric-admin's with 3 failed sign-ins, then 15 minutes, window 15). The root's convergence writes them
  as fine-grained password policies in the Password Settings Container: `fabric-enterprise-admin` (precedence 1) on
  fabric-admin's own account (written at once when setup makes it) and `fabric-directory-admins` (precedence 2) on the
  `directory-admins` group; the domain's default stays everyone else's. Checked before anything is rendered
  (`check_tier_policies`): both tiers, each a whole policy, and fabric-admin's lockout never 0 (until an admin unlocks
  it) and never 0 failed sign-ins (no lockout at all, 2.1.6.91). Set only with `sudo fabricctl domain password-policy
  --tier enterprise-admin|directory-admins` (audited
  `AD_TIER_PASSWORD_POLICY`), then applied; they are not a wizard setting, so neither the console's Settings nor a
  settings file reaches them, and `POST /v1/domain/password-policy` refuses a request naming a tier. The first
  Directory Administrator's password is checked against their tier's policy.
- **1.6.12.16.5 Resetting the break-glass password** (2.1.6.75): `sudo fabricctl admin reset-break-glass`, as root
  on a host whose DC is writable (a read-only DC's host is refused, naming the writable DC; so is a host with no
  domain): a new password generated as setup's, set on `fabric-admin` inside the DC as the system (the password on
  stdin only); a lockout in force is left to run its time out, never cleared (2.1.6.92: the command says when it
  ends, and the new password works from then); the file written again to `~/fabric-admin/` of the person
  running sudo (the reminder returns until it is deleted), audited `BREAK_GLASS_RESET` (the file's path, never the
  password, with when a lockout ends). A disabled `fabric-admin` is reported, not enabled.
- **1.6.12.16.6 The DC host's ways in** (2.1.6.78; 1.6.13.6): the member step's sssd lets in `fabric-admin` alone
  (`access_provider = simple`, `simple_allow_users = fabric-admin`); over SSH it signs in with a Kerberos ticket (its
  password over SSH is refused, as every domain password is there: pam_localuser, 1.6.11.11), and `sudo` takes its
  password through PAM; its sudo is a local rule, `/etc/sudoers.d/fabric-admin` (`fabric-admin ALL=(ALL:ALL) ALL`,
  0440), written, kept and undone with the member files. Every other domain account is refused, and no domain sudo
  rule applies.
- **1.6.12.16.7 The local administrator accounts** (2.1.6.78, 2.1.7.15): `fabricctl host accounts list` reads the
  host's own files (never the domain's accounts): uid 0 other than root; members of `sudo`, `wheel` and `admin` (named
  or by primary group); and accounts a sudoers rule (`/etc/sudoers` and the drop-ins sudo reads) grants ALL commands,
  by name, group, `User_Alias` or uid. The account that ran setup is recorded once at setup's admin step and marked.
  Each one not approved is reminded of on each view of the console's top bar and Overview and each `fabricctl doctor`
  (a warning, not a failed check), until it is removed or approved: `sudo fabricctl host accounts approve <name>`, by
  root acting for the Enterprise Admin (audited `LOCAL_ACCOUNT_APPROVE`, the approval stored in
  `<config>/local-accounts.json` with who and when), withdrawn with `unapprove` (`LOCAL_ACCOUNT_UNAPPROVE`). Approving
  an account that is not one, or one already approved, is refused. OpenBao's `local-admin` (D6) is not built yet, so
  no account is listed as managed.

## 1.6.12.17 What D4 built (0.8.0)

Step D4 of 1.6.12.15 (note [2.3.6.20](../volume_2_engineering_decisions/2.3.6-identity-notes.md)): fabric-agent's
checks and the routes and `fabricctl domain` commands of 1.6.12.4 to 1.6.12.10. The console's pages and the pending
store behind Apply per page are not D4's: each route acts at once, and each is one function (route body, then one
fabriclib function, then the DC) that a pending store can call later, unchanged.

- **1.6.12.17.1 Where the check runs:** on every call that changes a person, a machine, a group, the grants or the
  domain's policies, console and CLI alike, before anything is written: `fabriclib/access/check_access` asks the DC
  (`grants_tool.py`, op `authorize`, as the system, a read only), which decides from the directory as it is. The write
  that follows is fabric-agent's own account's, so AD's ACLs (2.1.6.76) hold too. The route's permission (1.6.12.11)
  is checked first, by the console and by fabric-agent (1.6.8.1); the DC's check then scopes it.
- **1.6.12.17.2 The caller's tier** (2.1.6.68): root on a fabric host (the CLI, and a root peer of the agent's socket)
  acts for the Enterprise Admin, as for the tiers' password policies (1.6.12.16.4); a person in AD's top tier is that
  tier; a member of `directory-admins` is a Directory Administrator; anyone else is delegated. A caller who is not a
  person of the domain, or is one of fabric's own accounts, is refused.
- **1.6.12.17.3 Whom nobody touches through fabric-agent:** AD's top tier (`fabric-admin`, break glass: managed only on
  the DC's host, 1.6.12.16.5), fabric's own accounts, the DCs, the hosts in `dc-hosts` and `OU=fabric-hosts`. A
  Directory Administrator is managed only by the Enterprise Admin's tier; a member of a group carrying a role bundle
  (1.8.2) only by a Directory Administrator or above. Setup's own changes of the fabric host's account (its member
  step and that step's undo) are fabric's, not a person's, and are not asked.
- **1.6.12.17.4 People and machines through grants** (2.1.6.66, 1.6.13.4): a delegated caller acts on a person or a
  machine only when an allowing grant whose who holds the caller, and whose where holds the target, gives that right
  (people `edit`, `reset`, `unlock`, `disable`, `add`; machines `add`, `move`, `disable`, `remove`, `groups`); the
  refusal names the right and the target. A new account (people `add`, machines `add`) is allowed when a grant's
  where groups are all among the groups it will be made in (a person's site group; a machine's type group, site group
  and `fabric-managed`; Domain Users or Domain Computers count as every new account's). A person's remove is the
  grant's people `add` right (1.6.13.11.2: add, and remove). The Directory Administrators need no grant. The checks
  hold for the routes built before D4 too: reset, disable, enable, remove, a person's groups, add person, add machine,
  a machine's enable, disable and remove, a machine group's machines, each given the caller the token names (the
  functions' `privileged` flag is gone: `caller=None` is root).
- **1.6.12.17.5 A group's two levels** (2.1.6.67): level 1, its scope and managers, is the tier above it: the
  Enterprise Admin's for `directory-admins`, a Directory Administrator's for every other. Level 2, its members, is that
  tier's, or its managers' within their controls: `add`, `remove` and `only_from` (a group whose people alone may be
  added). A manager is a person, or any member of a group named as one. A manager changes members and nothing else:
  every other action is refused them, their own controls included. AD's own groups, the computed ones (`grant-*`,
  `access-*`), those fabric fills (`machines-*`, `dc-hosts`) and the machine groups (changed on the computer) take no
  members and no managers here; a member that is in AD's top tier, one of fabric's accounts or (for a caller below
  them) a Directory Administrator is refused, and so is a tier's or one of AD's groups nested. The managers are stored
  as the grants are (1.6.13.11.1, 2.3.6.19.3): one `msDS-App-Configuration` per group in
  `CN=groups,CN=fabric,CN=System`, its CN the group's `objectGUID` (a rename keeps them), the JSON
  `{managers, add, remove, only_from}` in `msDS-Settings`; the group's `managedBy` names the first manager, for RSAT.
- **1.6.12.17.6 The routes** (1.3.3), each one fabric-agent route over one fabriclib function, the CLI's too:
  - People: `POST /v1/people/<uid>/edit` (body: any of `first`, `last`, `name`, `email`, `phone`, `mobile`, `title`,
    `department`, `company`, `office`, `city`, `state`, `country`, `manager`, `employee_id`, 2.1.6.93; another field
    refused: names of up to 64 letters and `. , ' -` (the display name also digits and `( )`), never cleared; an
    e-mail address that is one and nobody else's; phones of digits, a leading `+`, spaces and `( ) . / -`; a
    two-letter country, upper-cased; a manager who is a person, never themselves; an employee ID of up to 16; the
    other texts up to 64 without quotes, backslashes, `;` or `<>`; an empty value clears a field), `/v1/people/<uid>/unlock` (`lockoutTime` 0; not
    locked out: nothing changed).
  - Groups: `GET /v1/groups` (each group's kind: AD's, a tier's, a bundle's, fabric's, filled, computed, a machine
    group, an admin's own), `GET /v1/groups/<name>` (both levels: what it grants, through grants, sudo rules, 802.1X
    or a role; its managers and controls; its people and groups), `POST /v1/groups` (body `name`, `description`: in
    OU=groups, a POSIX gid from the DC's range; a name of 2-64 characters `a-z 0-9 . _ -`, not one of fabric's names,
    1.2.13, nor AD's, nor any account's), `/v1/groups/<name>/delete` (an admin's own only; refused while in use, naming
    where: a grant, a sudo rule, another group's managers, 802.1X's people mapping, the settings' groups),
    `/v1/groups/<name>/managers` (body `managers`, `add`, `remove`, `only_from`), `/v1/groups/<name>/members` (body
    `action` add or remove, `member`: a person or a group).
  - My account: `GET /v1/account/groups` (Groups I manage: each group the signed-in person manages, with its controls
    and members, 1.8.6.6), `POST /v1/account/password` (1.6.12.17.9).
  - OUs: `GET /v1/ous` (the tree by path, fabric's marked, what each holds), `POST /v1/ous` (body `parent`, `name`),
    `/v1/ous/rename` (body `ou`, `name`), `/v1/ous/delete` (body `ou`; refused while it holds anything, naming up to
    five of them). An OU is named by its path below the domain (`people/staff`, `machines/servers/web`). An admin's
    own go under `people`, `service-accounts`, `groups` and `machines/servers|desktops|network`; a name is 1-63
    letters, digits, space and `_ . -`, never one of fabric's folders' names.
  - Computers: `POST /v1/machines/<name>/move` (body `path`: any OU under OU=machines; its groups, so everyone's
    access, unchanged; needs `ous:admin` too, 1.6.12.11), `/v1/machines/<name>/type` (body `type`, `move`: its type
    group, compiled at once; with `move`, to its type's folder when it is in one of fabric's, an admin's own folder
    kept and said), `/v1/machines/<name>/sites` (body `action`, `site`), `/v1/machines/<name>/keytab`
    (1.6.12.17.8). Type and sites are a grant's machines `groups` right.
  - Kerberos: `GET /v1/domain/kerberos` (the realm, its KDCs, the ticket policy, the domain logon's sign-in),
    `GET /v1/domain/kerberos/spns` (every SPN and its account; a duplicate flagged on each, and listed),
    `POST /v1/domain/kerberos/policy` (1.6.12.17.7).
  - Policies: `POST /v1/domain/password-policy` and the grants' routes (1.6.13.11.7) are the Directory
    Administrators' alone (2.1.6.71, 2.1.6.73): `domain:admin` or `groups:admin` held through a bundle is not enough.
- **1.6.12.17.7 The ticket policy:** the setting `ad_kerberos_policy` (1.1.9), `{ticket_hours, renew_days,
  skew_minutes}`, default 10 hours, 7 days and 5 minutes (Samba's own). Checked before anything is rendered
  (`check_kerberos_policy`, in the settings' check): exactly those three whole numbers, a ticket of at least 1 hour and
  never longer than its renewal, a renewal of 1 to 30 days, a skew of 1 to 15 minutes. Rendered into the DC's compose
  environment; the DC's entrypoint sets `kdc:user ticket lifetime` and `kdc:service ticket lifetime` (hours) and
  `kdc:renewal lifetime` (hours) in `smb.conf`, and `clockskew` (seconds) in the KDC's `krb5.conf`, at every start.
  Changed with `fabricctl domain kerberos policy` (then applied, the DC restarting, unless `--no-apply`) or the route
  (recorded; applied with the next apply), audited `AD_KERBEROS_POLICY`.
- **1.6.12.17.8 A service's keytab** (1.6.12.9): the route and `fabricctl domain machine keytab` check the machine (in
  the domain, not a DC nor fabric's host), the service class (not the machine's own `host`, `RestrictedKrbHost`,
  `cifs`), the local user and the path, and answer one command, `sudo fabric-keytab --machine <name> --service <class>
  --user <user> [--path <file>]`: nothing secret in it, nothing changed. The Linux join kit installs
  `/usr/local/sbin/fabric-keytab` (1.6.7): run on the machine, it refuses a name that is not the machine's own, adds
  `<class>/<fqdn>` and `<class>/<NAME>` with `adcli update --add-service-principal` (as the machine's own account,
  from `/etc/krb5.keytab`), extracts those keys into the keytab (default `/etc/krb5-<class>.keytab`), owned by the
  user, mode 0600, and prints what it did.
- **1.6.12.17.9 A person's own password** (2.1.1.62, 1.8.6.6): `POST /v1/account/password` (body `current`, `new`),
  the signed-in person's own account only (the token's user, never a name in the body; a root peer is refused). Both
  go to the DC on stdin; inside it, bound as the person over NTLM with the current password, the change is the
  person's own (`unicodePwd` delete and add), so AD checks the current password, the policy and the history as for any
  change. It is immediate, never pending; then Keycloak ends every session of theirs but the one the token names
  (`sid`). Audited `PASSWORD_CHANGE_OWN`, never a password. A wrong current password, a new one AD's policy refuses,
  or one equal to the current, is refused with why and changes nothing. It has no CLI: it is a person's own, and root
  is no person.
- **1.6.12.17.10 Permissions** (1.6.12.11, 1.8.2): `people:edit` (Admin, Helpdesk) and `ous:admin` (Admin, Equipment
  operator) added; `people:reset` also unlocks; `groups:admin` creates and deletes groups and sets their managers.
  Reads are `domain:read`; a group's members, Groups I manage and the own password need only a sign-in (the DC
  decides).
- **1.6.12.17.11 The CLI** (4.6.11), as root: `fabricctl domain person edit|unlock|disable|enable`, `group
  list|create|delete|scope|managers|members`, `ou list|create|rename|delete`, `machine move|type|sites|keytab`,
  `kerberos show|spns|policy`. Each refusal prints `refused: <why>` (exit 1); a wrong usage prints the usage (exit 2).
