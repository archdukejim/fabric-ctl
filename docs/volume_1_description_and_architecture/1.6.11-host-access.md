## 1.6.11.2 Groups

| Group | Where | Members | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fabric-admins` | the organisation's `OU=groups` | people | managing every site's hosts: root through sudo, `fabricctl` on the fabric hosts; member of every `<site>-fabric-admins` |
| `<site>-fabric-admins` | the site's `OU=groups` | people (and `fabric-admins`) | managing the site's hosts; on a fresh install a member of `<site>-machine-admins`, so the two start as one (2.1.6.38) |
| `<site>-fabric-machines` | the site's `OU=groups` | computer accounts | the machines fabric manages (1.6.11.6): joined into it by default (2.1.6.40) |

The web console's admin group stays its own (2.1.6.41): `webui_admin_group`, `admins` on every install that has it,
**Domain Admins** by default on a new one (the host-access proposal's 2.3.6.4.3). It no longer gets sudo on joined
machines (1.6.11.3); people who need both are put in both. Converged by `src/containers/samba/ensure_groups.py` with the
role groups, ids from the site's block (1.6.3.9). Setup puts the first admin in `fabric-admins` as well as the
console's group (`directory/ensure_admin`): the installer's one person manages the hosts too, and `fabric-admins`
would otherwise start empty. A site's DC that converges before the root's has been upgraded finds no `fabric-admins`
yet and nests it at its next convergence.

## 1.6.11.3 Sudo

The site's sudo role becomes `CN=<site>-fabric-admins,OU=sudoers,<site>`: `%<site>-fabric-admins` (and, at the root,
`%fabric-admins`) may run any command as root on the site's joined Linux machines and those of the sites below, as
the `linux-sudo` role does today (2.1.9.13), which it replaces (2.1.6.37). On upgrade, once: the members of
`<site>-linux-sudo` are added to `<site>-fabric-admins`, the old `sudoRole` removed, and the `linux-sudo` role group
removed from the site's roles (its members already moved), so everyone who had sudo keeps it. The audit records the
move. Other rules in `OU=sudoers` stay the admins' (1.6.11.7).

## 1.6.11.4 Who may sign in: per OU

Who may sign in to a joined machine is the group policy of the OU it sits in (2.1.6.38, 2.1.6.42): each machine OU (the
site's `OU=machines`, its `OU=linux` and `OU=windows`, and any sub-OU an admin makes) can carry fabric's log-on policy,
a GPO `fabric: <site> log-on <ou path>` that names the groups allowed **at the keyboard** (`SeInteractiveLogonRight`) and
**remotely** (`SeRemoteInteractiveLogonRight`: RDP, and SSH through sssd's GPO access control). An OU without its own
policy takes its parent's, as group policy does; Windows enforces it, and Linux through sssd
(`ad_gpo_access_control = enforcing`, set by the join today).

- **A fresh install:** the site's `OU=machines` policy names `<site>-machine-admins` for both.
- **The fabric host's OU** (`OU=fabric-hosts`, 1.6.11.11) always carries a policy of its own: the setting's when it
  names `fabric-hosts`, else by default `fabric-admins` and `<site>-fabric-admins` for both (decision 2.1.6.53, on
  every site, upgrades too). That policy is the one gate on who signs in to the appliance with a domain account: an
  admin changes it like any OU's, and `clear` puts the default back. Anyone else may sign in
  where a fabric admin grants their group, normally by putting machines in OUs and granting the group on the OU.
- **Always kept:** `fabric-break-glass` and the machine's own local Administrators (`BUILTIN\Administrators`) are in
  every fabric log-on policy, so the break-glass ways in (2.1.7.6) cannot be locked out; the editor refuses to drop
  them.
- **Upgrades change nothing** (2.1.6.44): an existing site keeps today's log-on rights (on its site OU: users, admins,
  the role groups, break-glass); the site administrator narrows them in the web console when they choose. Status shows
  the policy of each OU.
- **The editor:** web console, Directory → Machines → *Who may sign in*: per OU, the groups for each right; permission
  `gpo:admin`. CLI: `fabricctl domain logon show [<ou>]`, `fabricctl domain logon set <ou> --console G,... --remote G,...`,
  `fabricctl domain logon clear <ou>` (the OU takes its parent's again). An empty list is refused for the site's
  `OU=machines` (nobody but break-glass could sign in).

How it is built (settled at the build, 2026-10-10):

- **The setting** `logon_policies` (`templates/vars.yaml.j2`, default null): `{<OU path>: {console: [groups], remote:
  [groups]}}`. An OU path is relative to the site's OU, segments of letters, digits, `. - _` joined by `/`, lower
  case, at most 8 levels (`machines`, `machines/linux`); `""` is the site's OU itself (typed `site`). `rights` is
  refused as a path (the legacy GPO's name). Group names must be names AD can hold; whether they exist is checked by the
  editor in the domain, not at render. Checked at every apply (`deploy/check_settings` →
  `samba/check_logon_policies`): the map must name the site's OU or its `OU=machines`, and neither may grant nobody
  (both lists empty); an OU below them may (a locked-down OU: break-glass only).
- **Null** is the log-on rights of before 0.9, unchanged: the one GPO `fabric: <site> log-on rights` on the site's OU
  (`legacy_logon_groups`). A **fresh install** (setup finds no vars file and no provisioned domain,
  `setup/collect_vars`) is given `{machines: {console: [<site>-machine-admins], remote: [<site>-machine-admins]}}`;
  an upgrade or a reinstall is not.
- **The GPOs** (`src/containers/samba/ensure_logon_policies.py`, at every convergence): one per OU of the map,
  `fabric: <site> log-on <OU path or site>`, linked to that OU; its `GptTmpl.inf` grants `SeInteractiveLogonRight` to
  the console groups and `SeRemoteInteractiveLogonRight` to the remote groups, each followed by `fabric-break-glass`
  and `BUILTIN\Administrators`, and carries the `[Group Membership]` part as before (the `windows-admins` of the site
  and the sites above made local Administrators). The site's other fabric log-on GPOs (the legacy one, or an OU's no
  longer named) are removed: unlinked everywhere, the container and its SYSVOL folder deleted (`remove_gpo`). An OU that
  does not exist, or a granted group since removed, is reported in the convergence's output and skipped, never fatal.
- **The editor** saves to the settings and applies (as `dhcp on` does; `--no-apply` only saves), audited
  `LOGON_POLICY_SET` / `LOGON_POLICY_CLEAR`. `set` with one right left out keeps that right as the OU has it. Its
  first edit while the setting is null reads today's list in the domain (directory operation `logon_groups`) and makes
  it the site OU's policy first. `clear` refuses an OU without its own policy and a clear that would leave neither the
  site's OU nor `OU=machines` with one. Agent routes: GET `/v1/domain/logon` (`domain:read`), POST
  `/v1/domain/logon/set` and `/clear` (`gpo:admin`). `fabricctl domain status` shows each OU's policy.
- A site the root prepares for its join (`prepare_site`) is converged there with the legacy GPO until its own DC
  converges with its own setting and replaces it.

## 1.6.11.5 Machines sorted by OS family

As decision 2.1.6.36: each site's `OU=machines` gets `OU=windows` and `OU=linux`; the Linux join places a machine in
`OU=linux` and writes `operatingSystem` / `operatingSystemVersion` from `/etc/os-release` (a join with a site admin's
password: a one-time join signs in as the computer itself, which AD does not let write them, so a pre-created machine's
family is its OU, given by `add-machine --os`, and its version is not recorded; found by the samba suite, 2026-10-10); `add-machine` pre-creates in
the OU for the OS it is given (`--os linux|windows`); a Windows machine that joined itself into `OU=machines` is moved
once by a timer from its `operatingSystem`: `fabric-machines.timer`, hourly, runs `fabricctl domain sort-machines
--scheduled`, and the machines it moved are recorded on the host (`config/machines-sorted.json`), so one an admin
moves back stays where they put it. fabric never moves a machine again, nor touches admins' sub-OUs. Machines joined
before 0.9 stay in `OU=machines` (an admin moves them, or leaves them). The OUs
carry policy for convenience (1.6.11.4); security stays on groups.

## 1.6.11.6 Machines fabric manages

A machine in `<site>-fabric-machines` is one fabric configures beyond the join (2.1.6.40, 2.1.6.43). The Linux join adds
it by default; `--unmanaged` leaves it a plain member (joined, sssd and sudo as today, nothing more). On a managed
Linux machine the join also:

- **SSH:** `GSSAPIAuthentication yes` and `PasswordAuthentication no` (Kerberos only by default, 2.1.6.39; a site may
  turn passwords or certificates on, 1.6.11.9), `UsePAM yes` so the log-on policy (1.6.11.4) applies to SSH; a drop-in
  in `/etc/ssh/sshd_config.d/`, the original kept for the uninstall.
- **DNS:** systemd-resolved pointed at fabric's DNS with the domain as search domain, the machine's other links left as
  they are (2.1.6.31; the manual said so before it was built, 1.6.7.5).
- **Its OU and OS attributes** (1.6.11.5); the break-glass password timer when that is built (1.7.1.3).

**How a machine becomes one** (settled while building it: a one-time join signs in as the computer, which cannot
add itself to a group, and not every admin who joins may change the site's groups): `add-machine` makes the
pre-created account a member, unless `--unmanaged` (then the account's description says so); a machine joined with
an admin's password lands in `OU=linux` and fabric's hourly sort (`fabricctl domain sort-machines`, the timer of
1.6.11.5) makes it a member, once, unless its join said `--unmanaged` (written on its description by the join).
A machine an admin takes out stays out (the sort records what it adopted). The web console adds and removes
machines (`machines:admin`).

**On the machine** the join installs `/usr/local/sbin/fabric-managed` and `fabric-managed.timer` (hourly): signed in
as the machine (its keytab), it asks the DC whether it is in `<site>-fabric-machines`, and applies the configuration
while it is, removes it when it is not. The join applies it at once on a managed join. SSH's drop-in is
`/etc/ssh/sshd_config.d/10-fabric.conf` (first: sshd keeps the first value it reads, and Ubuntu's cloud images turn
passwords on in `50-cloud-init.conf`); passwords are off for every account, local ones too, while keys keep
working. DNS: `/etc/systemd/resolved.conf.d/fabric.conf`, fabric's DNS for the domain's names only (`~<domain>`),
other names as the machine had them. SSH is configured only where an SSH server is installed; the join installs
none.

## 1.6.11.7 Host groups and sudo rules

As decision 2.1.6.31: **host groups** are AD groups `<site>-hosts-<name>` in the site's `OU=groups`, machines as
members, usable in log-on policies (1.6.11.4) and sudo rules; `fabricctl machines groups`, `fabricctl machines group
add|remove <group> <machine>`, the web console; `machines:admin`. **Sudo rules** beyond the site's: who (people or
groups), which commands, as whom, on which host group, stored as `sudoRole` objects in the site's `OU=sudoers` (nested
sites inherit, as the join's search bases already do); `fabricctl sudo list|add|remove`, the web console; permission
`sudo:admin` (new, in the admin bundle). A rule granting ALL commands to everyone is refused.

How it is built (settled at the build, 2026-10-10; manual 4.6.6.8):

- **Host groups** (`src/containers/samba/create_host_group.py` and its neighbours, directory operations run as the
  site's agent): a security group `<site>-hosts-<name>` in the site's `OU=groups`, its description marking it as
  fabric's; `<name>` 1-32 of `a-z 0-9 -` (given short or as the AD name). Only machines of the site's `OU=machines`
  (and the OUs below it) are put in; a host group a sudo rule names cannot be deleted. Its name passes the log-on
  editor's check like any group (1.6.11.4); what log-on rights mean for a group of computers is Windows' business,
  so who may sign in stays per OU. CLI: `fabricctl machines groups`, `fabricctl machines group create|delete
  <group>`, `fabricctl machines group add|remove <group> <machine>`; audited `HOST_GROUP_CREATE`,
  `HOST_GROUP_REMOVE`, `HOST_GROUP_MEMBER_ADD`, `HOST_GROUP_MEMBER_REMOVE`.
- **Sudo rules:** `CN=<name>,OU=sudoers,<site OU>` (`sudoRole`): `sudoUser` (user names, `%group`, `ALL`),
  `sudoCommand` (absolute paths with their arguments, or `ALL`; printable characters only), `sudoRunAsUser`
  (default `root`), and the description `fabric: a sudo rule of fabric's editor (manual 1.6.11.7); hosts: <host
  group or ALL>`, which is what marks a rule as the editor's (the site's default rule carries its own mark, 1.6.11.3;
  anything else is an admin's own). `sudo list` shows all three kinds; `remove` takes only the editor's. Refused at
  the DC: ALL commands for `ALL`, `%Domain Users`, `%users`, `%<site>-users` or a parent site's `%<site>-users`;
  a person or group not in the domain. CLI: `fabricctl sudo list|add|remove`; audited `SUDO_RULE_ADD` (with who,
  commands, as whom, where) and `SUDO_RULE_REMOVE`.
- **How a rule reaches a host group's machines.** sssd's AD sudo provider asks the DC only for the rules whose
  `sudoHost` is `ALL`, one of the machine's own names or addresses, a netgroup (`+name`) or a wildcard, then sudo
  checks the host again; it cannot follow an AD group. Netgroups were set aside: AD holds none unless its schema is
  extended for NIS, and sssd's AD provider would have to resolve them for every machine. So fabric writes the group's
  members into the rule: one `sudoHost` per member computer's short name and its `dNSHostName` (which a pre-created
  account has only once the machine joined: convergence adds it), lower case
  (`refresh_sudo_hosts.py`), rewritten whenever fabric changes a host group's members and at every convergence (a
  member added with RSAT, a machine renamed); a host group with no machines leaves the rule without `sudoHost`, so
  it applies nowhere. A machine sees a change at sssd's next smart refresh (15 minutes by default) or when sssd
  restarts. A rule on ALL hosts is `sudoHost: ALL`, reaching the site's machines and those below, as the default rule.
- **Agent routes:** GET `/v1/host-groups` and `/v1/sudo` (`domain:read`); POST `/v1/host-groups`,
  `/v1/host-groups/<name>/members` (body `action` add|remove, `machine`), `/v1/host-groups/<name>/delete`
  (`machines:admin`); POST `/v1/sudo` (body `name`, `users`, `commands`, `run_as`, `hosts`) and
  `/v1/sudo/<name>/delete` (`sudo:admin`). The web console shows both under Directory → Machines, the forms by
  permission. Nothing is applied: the directory changes at once.

## 1.6.11.8 The fabric host as a member of its domain

As decision 2.1.6.34: setup makes the fabric host a member of its own domain unless the Advanced option leaves it out
(`host_join_domain`, default true; Advanced asks "Make this host a member of its domain [Y/n]", ID
`setup.host_join_domain`). The DC runs on the host under its own name (`dc_name`, 2.1.6.45), and the host signs in
with **its own computer account**, `<HOSTNAME>$` (decision 2.1.6.48, revising 2.1.6.34, which gave the host the
DC's account `<DC_NAME>$` until 0.7.1): a setup step `member`, after `start` (the DC must answer), pre-creates that
account as `fabricctl domain add-machine` makes one (1.6.7.3), then places it in the site's `OU=fabric-hosts` as a
machine fabric manages (decision 2.1.6.49, 1.6.11.11; until then it was left in `OU=machines/OU=linux`, unmanaged),
so the site's Group Policy reaches the host like any other machine; exports its keys to the host's keytab (`samba-tool domain
exportkeytab` for `<HOSTNAME>$` and its `host/` names only, never the DC's nor the whole domain's; mode 0600); writes
`/etc/krb5.conf` and `/etc/sssd/sssd.conf` against the local DC as the Linux join does (1.6.7.3); and enables `sss`
in NSS and PAM (`pam-auth-update`), with sudo from the domain's rules (1.6.11.3).

- **Who may sign in to the host with a domain account:** whom the log-on policy of its OU, `OU=fabric-hosts`,
  allows, and nothing else (decision 2.1.6.53; sssd `access_provider = ad`, `ad_gpo_access_control = enforcing`, as on
  every joined machine). That OU's policy is the fabric admins by default (1.6.11.4), so the appliance stays the
  fabric admins' to manage (2.1.6.41) until an admin grants more in that one place; fabric adds no filter of its own on
  the host. Until 2026-10-12 sssd's `ad_access_filter` also kept domain sign-in to the fabric admins (the access filter
  is gone with 2.1.6.53), and until 0.7.1 the host used the DC's account, which no site's policy reaches, with sssd's
  simple access provider. Break-glass is kept: local accounts, root and local sudo never go through the domain, and
  `fabric-break-glass` is in every fabric log-on policy.
- **A site whose DC is read-only** (decision 2.1.6.52): the host has its own account there too, made at the writable
  DC that site replicates from and cached by the read-only DC (1.6.11.13); with that DC not answering, the step
  refuses. The host never falls back to the DC's account.
- **A DC with the host's name is refused** (decision 2.1.6.51): the host's account would be the DC's. Setup's question
  for the DC's name refuses the host's own (`samba/dc_name_problem` given the host's name, asked again, also for a `dc_name` already in the settings), and the
  `member` step refuses such settings before anything changes, saying to set another `dc_name` before the domain is
  provisioned or `host_join_domain: false`. Until 2026-10-12 the host then kept the DC's account; no install needs that
  (2.3.6.7, the first point), so that path is gone.
- **The account:** named as adcli and Windows name one: the host name's first label, cut to 15 characters (NetBIOS),
  without a dash left at the end, in upper case; a machine fabric manages, in `OU=fabric-hosts` (1.6.11.11); its
  password random, set once and kept nowhere (the keytab is all the host needs), renewed only when
  `host_machine_password_renew_days` asks for it (1.6.11.12). A computer of that name that fabric did not make for this host is refused, named, before anything
  changes. **Upgrades:** an install whose keytab holds the DC's account moves to its own at the next setup (the
  account made, the keytab replaced, sssd restarted; an upgrade asks the `member` consent again, since its plan
  changed; `fabricctl apply` does not run this step). A host renamed (2.1.6.45) gets an account under its new name at
  the next setup, and the one fabric made under the old name is removed.
- **Local accounts, root and local sudo are untouched**, so the host is administered as before with the DC down (and
  a domain account that signed in before keeps signing in from sssd's cache).
- **Consent:** a group of its own, `member` ("This host as a member of its domain", recommended; manual 1.2.9.3):
  the packages (`sssd-ad`, `libnss-sss`, `libpam-sss`, `libsss-sudo`, `krb5-user`) and the files above. Declining it,
  or `host_join_domain: false`, leaves the host as it is; status shows it as a relaxation. An upgrade asks it like any
  new group (an unattended one names it: `--approve member`).
- **Undo:** `fabricctl setup --undo member` and the uninstall remove the host's own account from the domain (first,
  while the DC answers; one the DC will not remove is said, with where to remove it: the web console's Directory →
  Machines), put back each file as it was (`common/keep_original`), remove the keytab and turn `sss` off in NSS and
  PAM; the packages stay (as for the other groups' packages). An export taken by the uninstall keeps the record, so a
  restore takes the account back (or makes it again).
- **Tests:** the sandbox (the host is its systemd container): the keytab holds the host's own account and not the
  DC's, the account is in `OU=machines/OU=linux`; a fabric admin resolves and signs in (PAM), has sudo from the site's
  rule; a person outside the fabric admins is refused; root and a local account keep working with the DC stopped; undo
  puts everything back and removes the account, approving again makes it again; the consent suite plans the group and
  runs the step on a scratch tree (the account made and recorded, a second run changing nothing, a foreign account of
  the host's name refused, the move from the DC's keytab, a rename, the undo's removal, a read-only DC's site:
  1.6.11.13).
- **As built** (2026-10-10; `setup/join_host_domain.py`, `undo/undo_member.py`): the keys are read from the samba
  container (`samba-tool domain exportkeytab --principal=` once for `<DC_NAME>$`, `host/<hostname_dc>` and
  `host/<DC_NAME>`, in the container's `/tmp`, removed at once) and the keytab is replaced only when its keys differ, so
  a second run changes nothing. Two packages beside the design's, as the Linux join installs them: `sssd-tools`
  (`sssctl`) and `libsasl2-modules-gssapi-mit`, without which sssd cannot bind to the DC (found by the sandbox
  suite). sssd talks to this host's DC only (`ad_server = <hostname_dc>`, no dynamic DNS
  updates: the DC keeps its own records). Two host changes the design did not name, both in the consent's list: the
  DC's name goes into `/etc/hosts` when the host cannot resolve it (its own resolver may not know the AD zone), and
  where sudo-rs is the default (Ubuntu 26.04) the classic sudo becomes it, as the Linux join does, since sudo-rs
  cannot read the domain's rules. What was on before (the PAM profiles, sssd enabled, sudo's alternative) is kept in
  `config/member.json` for the undo. `host_join_domain: false` (checked: true or false only) is listed in status's
  relaxed settings; Advanced is now offered on every interactive setup, since this choice exists after the CA does.
- **As built for 2.1.6.48** (2026-10-12; `setup/common/host_identity.py` decides the account,
  `setup/common/find_computer.py` looks it up inside the DC, `directory/add_machine` makes it as fabric-agent,
  `setup/common/export_keytab.py` exports its keys, `undo/remove_host_account.py` removes it): the account's name is
  kept in `config/member.json` (`account`), which is how a later run knows the account is this host's; sssd's
  `ad_hostname` is `<account>.<ad_domain>`, so it finds `<HOSTNAME>$` in the keytab. (The access filter, removed by
  2.1.6.53, was written `DOM:<ad_domain>:(…)`: without the prefix sssd read the nested-membership rule's colons as a
  prefix of its own and denied everyone.)

## 1.6.11.9 SSH certificates (an option)

OpenBao's SSH engine (2.1.7.5, revised by 2.1.6.39) is **off by default**: a site turns it on in the web console. Then
fabric admins get 8-hour user certificates (the user name the one principal), managed machines trust the user CA, and
host certificates reach them through AD (2.3.6.4.1). Built last in 0.9, after everything above; until then a managed
machine signs in with Kerberos only.

## 1.6.11.10 Entry points and tests

New entry points, each with a positive and a negative test (global Rule 9, 3.1.2.5): `fabricctl domain logon
show|set|clear`, `fabricctl machines groups|group`, `fabricctl sudo list|add|remove`, `add-machine --os`, the join's
`--unmanaged`, their agent routes and web forms, the setting `host_join_domain`; with 0.7.1, `fabricctl domain
host-managed [--scheduled]` (1.6.11.11) and the setting `host_machine_password_renew_days` (1.6.11.12). Tests: the samba suite's containers
(groups, the sudo move, log-on policies per OU, OUs by OS, the join's managed configuration on Ubuntu 24.04 and 26.04
and Debian; host groups and sudo rules in `tests/samba/hostgroups.py`, and a rule on a host group applying on its
member and not on another machine in `linux_join.py`), the sandbox (the fabric host as a member), and the Ubuntu 26.04 VM on the lab switch (SSH with Kerberos
from another member, refused for a person outside the OU's groups).

## 1.6.11.11 The fabric host as a machine fabric manages (0.7.1)

As decision 2.1.6.49 (the owner's answer to the fourth point of 2.3.6.7): the fabric host is a machine fabric manages
(1.6.11.6), and its computer account lives in an OU of its own, so Group Policy can be aimed at fabric's hosts apart
from the site's other machines.

- **Its OU:** `OU=fabric-hosts` in the site's OU, beside `OU=machines` (1.6.3.4; made with the site's other OUs at
  every convergence, `ensure_layout`). Beside it, not below it: a GPO an admin links to `OU=machines` (and that OU's
  log-on policy, 1.6.11.4) reaches the appliance only when it is linked to `OU=fabric-hosts` too, and the site's
  `<site>-machine-admins`, who have full control of `OU=machines` (1.6.3.6), have none over the appliance's account
  (the site's OU admins and its agent have, as over all of the site). `OU=fabric-hosts` always has a log-on policy of
  its own (decision 2.1.6.53, 1.6.11.4): the fabric admins by default, written by the DC's convergence on every site
  (`ensure_logon_policies`, `samba/host_logon_default`) whatever `logon_policies` holds, and shown by `fabricctl domain
  logon show` and status; an admin changes it like any OU's (`fabricctl domain logon set fabric-hosts …`, or the web
  console's *Who may sign in*), which takes effect on the host with no change to fabric's files there (sssd reads the
  policy at sign-in); `clear` puts the default back. A right left out of `set` keeps the default's groups. fabric's lookups of a site's machines (the list, disable, remove:
  `find_machine`, `list_machines`) take in `OU=fabric-hosts`, so the host shows in Directory → Machines; host groups
  stay for the machines of `OU=machines` (a sudo rule on all hosts reaches the fabric host).
- **Placed once:** the `member` step, after making the host's account (in `OU=linux`, as `add-machine` does) or
  finding the one it made before, moves it into `OU=fabric-hosts`, makes it a member of `<site>-fabric-machines` and
  writes on its description that it is the site's fabric host (directory operation `place_fabric_host`, as the site's
  agent; audited `MACHINE_PLACE`). `config/member.json` keeps `placed` (the account placed), so it is done once per
  account: an admin who moves the account elsewhere, or takes it out of `<site>-fabric-machines`, keeps it so (as
  for other machines, 1.6.11.5, 1.6.11.6). An account made by 0.7.1's first build (in `OU=machines/OU=linux`, marked
  unmanaged) is moved at the next setup, and a second run changes nothing; a renamed host's new account is placed the
  same way.
- **What fabric configures on the host while its account is in `<site>-fabric-machines`**
  (`setup/common/apply_host_managed`):
  - **SSH**, where an SSH server is installed (`/etc/ssh/sshd_config.d` exists): `/etc/ssh/sshd_config.d/10-fabric.conf`
    with `GSSAPIAuthentication yes`, `GSSAPICleanupCredentials yes`, `GSSAPIStrictAcceptorCheck no` (the keytab holds
    only this host's own keys, and the host's resolver may not give its AD name back) and `UsePAM yes`; and in
    `/etc/pam.d/sshd`, before its `@include common-auth`, `auth requisite pam_localuser.so` under fabric's marker
    line: a password typed over SSH is taken for local accounts only, so a domain account signs in over SSH with its
    Kerberos ticket (or a key it installed), never with its password (2.1.6.39).
  - **Local accounts' SSH sign-in is left as the host had it: the host's own exception to 2.1.6.39** (2.1.6.49).
    fabric never writes `PasswordAuthentication` on the host. Its KDC is the domain controller on this same host: with
    the DC stopped or broken no Kerberos sign-in is possible, and a local account (its password over SSH, or the
    console) is how the DC gets repaired. An owner who wants passwords off for local accounts too sets it in their own
    sshd configuration, which fabric leaves alone. Rejected: turning local passwords off once a Kerberos sign-in has
    been seen to work: it proves Kerberos worked once, not that it works when the DC on the same host fails, which is
    exactly when the local sign-in is needed.
  - **sshd checks it before it is used:** after the drop-in is written, `sshd -t`; when sshd refuses the configuration
    the drop-in is put back as it was, nothing is reloaded and setup stops, saying why. An accepted one is loaded with
    `systemctl try-reload-or-restart ssh` (a reload: open sessions stay).
  - **DNS:** `/etc/systemd/resolved.conf.d/fabric.conf`, `DNS=<host_ip>` and `Domains=~<ad_domain> ~<domain>`: the
    domain's names from fabric's own DNS, every other name as the host had it; only where systemd-resolved runs with
    its stub (`use_host_dns: true`, the default). With `use_host_dns: false` the host's resolver is fabric's
    `resolver` change already (1.2.9) and is left alone.
  - **In step with the group:** `fabric-host-managed.timer` (hourly, 10 minutes after boot) runs `fabricctl domain
    host-managed --scheduled`: it asks the DC (its own database, inside the samba container, as `find_computer` does)
    whether the host's account is in `<site>-fabric-machines`, applies the above while it is and removes it when it
    is not; with the DC not answering, nothing changes. `fabricctl domain host-managed` does the same at once and says
    what it found and changed; on a host that is not a member of its domain it refuses, saying so (the timer then does
    nothing).
- **Never locked out** (each proven by the sandbox suite): with the DC stopped, local accounts sign in at the console
  and over SSH with their passwords, and root and local sudo are untouched; a configuration sshd refuses is never
  loaded; taking the host out of `<site>-fabric-machines`, or undoing `member`, removes everything above.
- **Undo:** `fabricctl setup --undo member` and the uninstall remove both drop-ins and fabric's lines in
  `/etc/pam.d/sshd`, reload sshd and restart systemd-resolved when they changed, then remove the account (1.6.11.8).
- **Consent:** in the `member` group's plan (1.2.9.3); an upgrade asks it again, its plan having changed.

## 1.6.11.12 Renewing the host's machine password (0.7.1)

As decision 2.1.6.50 (the fifth point of 2.3.6.7): the setting `host_machine_password_renew_days`, **0 (off) by
default**: the host's machine password is set once and never changed, which Samba allows (it never expires a machine's
password). A whole number from 1 to 999 turns renewal on: the `member` step installs `adcli` (in the plan when the
setting is on) and writes `ad_maximum_machine_account_password_age = <days>` into the host's `sssd.conf`, so sssd
changes the account's password with adcli, from the host's keytab, once it is that old (checked daily: sssd's default
`ad_machine_account_password_renewal_opts`). 0 writes `ad_maximum_machine_account_password_age = 0`: without it sssd
would try every 30 days, and fail without adcli. Checked at every apply (`deploy/check_samba_settings`): a whole number
from 0 to 999 (Windows' range for the same policy), never `true`/`false`, a fraction or text; anything else is
refused, saying so. After a renewal the `member` step's next run exports the DC's current keys, the same ones, into
the keytab. Turning it back to 0 leaves `adcli` installed, as the step's other packages stay. At a read-only DC's
site the renewal goes to a writable DC (adcli finds one through the domain's DNS) and reaches the read-only DC by
replication, which caches the account (1.6.11.13); not proven end to end (2.3.6.8).

## 1.6.11.13 The fabric host at a read-only DC's site (0.7.1)

As decision 2.1.6.52 (the second point of 2.3.6.7): where a site's DC is read-only (`ad_dc_type: rodc`, 1.9.8), the
host has its own computer account as everywhere else; a read-only DC can neither make an account nor hold one's
password unless it is allowed to cache it, so:

- **The site's join** (once, at the read-only DC's join): setup puts the site's group `<site>-rodc-cache` (made with
  the site's groups at every convergence, `ensure_groups`; not a POSIX group, so it takes no id from the site's block) on the read-only DC's password replication allow list
  (`msDS-RevealOnDemandGroup` on its computer account), at the writable DC it joined through (`ad_join_server`), with
  the join account, before that account is removed (`samba/allow_rodc_cache`, from `setup/start_services`). Already
  there, nothing is written.
- **The `member` step:** first asks whether the writable DC answers (its rootDSE, from inside the site's DC); when it
  does not, the step refuses before anything changes, saying where to look (`fabricctl federation status`) and that
  `host_join_domain: false` leaves the host out. No fallback to the DC's account. Otherwise it makes the account at
  the writable DC (`add_machine` with the server), places it (`place_fabric_host`: in `OU=fabric-hosts`, in
  `<site>-fabric-machines` and in `<site>-rodc-cache`), has the read-only DC cache it now (`samba-tool rodc preload`)
  and exports its keys from the read-only DC. The site's agent writes at the writable DC as it does at its own: its
  rights are on the site's OU, which is the same directory.
- **Idempotent:** a second run with the account found, placed and its keys in the keytab asks nothing of the writable
  DC, so it changes nothing with the link down.
- **Undo** removes the account at the writable DC; its membership of `<site>-rodc-cache`, the read-only DC's only
  entry for it, goes with it. The allow-list entry is the site's, not the host's: it stays while the site has a
  read-only DC (an empty group caches nothing).
- **One choice of the writable DC** *(revised 2026-10-13, code layout only)*: the member step's account, its undo and
  the DHCP options' edits (1.10.4.24.2) all take the DC to write at from one unit, `samba/writable_server`: this site's
  DC, or at a read-only DC's site `ad_join_server` (trimmed); with none known it refuses before anything is written. An
  undo at such a site with no writable DC known says the account was not removed and why, without trying the
  read-only DC (which could not have removed it).
- **A host whose read-only DC cannot cache the account** (a site joined before 0.7.1, whose allow list lacks the
  group): the preload is refused and the step stops, naming the group and the allow list; a setup with the join
  account (a re-join) writes it.
- **Tests:** the consent suite runs the step on a scratch tree (refused with the writable DC down, made and placed
  there and preloaded when it answers, a second run asking nothing of it, the undo at the writable DC); the samba suite
  proves the directory parts against its writable and read-only DCs (the group on the allow list, the account placed
  with the cache group, the preload accepted for it and refused for an account outside the group). Not run: a sandbox
  fabric host whose own DC is read-only (2.3.6.8).
