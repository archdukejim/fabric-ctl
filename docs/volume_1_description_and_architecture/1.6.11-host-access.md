Who may reach which joined machine, and with what rights. The model came from the owner's answers to the host-access
proposal ([2.3.6.4](../volume_2_engineering_decisions/2.3.6-identity-notes.md#2364-proposal-07-host-access-ssh-and-fabrics-own-admin-groups)); decisions 2.1.6.31, 2.1.6.34, 2.1.6.36 to 2.1.6.44. It builds on the directory (1.6.3), the site's
group policy (1.6.5, S6) and the Linux join (1.6.7).

(1.6.11.4), machines sorted by OS (1.6.11.5), managed machines (1.6.11.6), the fabric host as a member (1.6.11.8).

`<site>-machine-admins`), its log-on rights GPO and sudo role (1.6.5, S6), the Linux join (1.6.7). Build order, each
step testable on its own (the samba suite's containers, then the Ubuntu VMs of 2.3.1.9.6): groups and sudo (1.6.11.2,

## 1.6.11.2 Groups

| Group | Where | Members | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fabric-admins` | the organisation's `OU=groups` | people | managing every site's hosts: root through sudo, `fabricctl` on the fabric hosts; member of every `<site>-fabric-admins` |
| `<site>-fabric-admins` | the site's `OU=groups` | people (and `fabric-admins`) | managing the site's hosts; on a fresh install a member of `<site>-machine-admins`, so the two start as one (2.1.6.38) |
| `<site>-fabric-machines` | the site's `OU=groups` | computer accounts | the machines fabric manages (1.6.11.6): joined into it by default (2.1.6.40) |

The web console's admin group stays its own (2.1.6.41): `webui_admin_group`, `admins` on every install that has it,
**Domain Admins** by default on a new one (the host-access proposal's 2.3.6.4.3). It no longer gets sudo on joined
machines (1.6.11.3); people who need both are put in both. Converged by `src/containers/samba/ensure_groups.py` with the
role groups, ids from the site's block (1.6.3.9). Setup puts the first admin in `fabric-admins` as well as the
console's group (`directory/ensure_admin`): the installer's one person manages the hosts too, and `fabric-admins`
would otherwise start empty. A site's DC that converges before the root's has been upgraded finds no `fabric-admins`
yet and nests it at its next convergence.

## 1.6.11.3 Sudo

The site's sudo role becomes `CN=<site>-fabric-admins,OU=sudoers,<site>`: `%<site>-fabric-admins` (and, at the root,
`%fabric-admins`) may run any command as root on the site's joined Linux machines and those of the sites below, as
the `linux-sudo` role does today (2.1.9.13), which it replaces (2.1.6.37). On upgrade, once: the members of
`<site>-linux-sudo` are added to `<site>-fabric-admins`, the old `sudoRole` removed, and the `linux-sudo` role group
removed from the site's roles (its members already moved), so everyone who had sudo keeps it. The audit records the
move. Other rules in `OU=sudoers` stay the admins' (1.6.11.7).

## 1.6.11.4 Who may sign in: per OU

Who may sign in to a joined machine is the group policy of the OU it sits in (2.1.6.38, 2.1.6.42): each machine OU (the
site's `OU=machines`, its `OU=linux` and `OU=windows`, and any sub-OU an admin makes) can carry fabric's log-on policy,
a GPO `fabric: <site> log-on <ou path>` that names the groups allowed **at the keyboard** (`SeInteractiveLogonRight`) and
**remotely** (`SeRemoteInteractiveLogonRight`: RDP, and SSH through sssd's GPO access control). An OU without its own
policy takes its parent's, as group policy does; Windows enforces it, and Linux through sssd
(`ad_gpo_access_control = enforcing`, set by the join today).

- **A fresh install:** the site's `OU=machines` policy names `<site>-machine-admins` for both. Anyone else may sign in
  where a fabric admin grants their group, normally by putting machines in OUs and granting the group on the OU.
- **Always kept:** `fabric-break-glass` and the machine's own local Administrators (`BUILTIN\Administrators`) are in
  every fabric log-on policy, so the break-glass ways in (2.1.7.6) cannot be locked out; the editor refuses to drop
  them.
- **Upgrades change nothing** (2.1.6.44): an existing site keeps today's log-on rights (on its site OU: users, admins,
  the role groups, break-glass); the site administrator narrows them in the web console when they choose. Status shows
  the policy of each OU.
- **The editor:** web console, Directory → Machines → *Who may sign in*: per OU, the groups for each right; permission
  `gpo:admin`. CLI: `fabricctl domain logon show [<ou>]`, `fabricctl domain logon set <ou> --console G,... --remote G,...`,
  `fabricctl domain logon clear <ou>` (the OU takes its parent's again). An empty list is refused for the site's
  `OU=machines` (nobody but break-glass could sign in).

How it is built (settled at the build, 2026-10-10):

- **The setting** `logon_policies` (`templates/vars.yaml.j2`, default null): `{<OU path>: {console: [groups], remote:
  [groups]}}`. An OU path is relative to the site's OU, segments of letters, digits, `. - _` joined by `/`, lower
  case, at most 8 levels (`machines`, `machines/linux`); `""` is the site's OU itself (typed `site`). `rights` is
  refused as a path (the legacy GPO's name). Group names must be names AD can hold; whether they exist is checked by the
  editor in the domain, not at render. Checked at every apply (`deploy/check_settings` →
  `samba/check_logon_policies`): the map must name the site's OU or its `OU=machines`, and neither may grant nobody
  (both lists empty); an OU below them may (a locked-down OU: break-glass only).
- **Null** is the log-on rights of before 0.9, unchanged: the one GPO `fabric: <site> log-on rights` on the site's OU
  (`legacy_logon_groups`). A **fresh install** (setup finds no vars file and no provisioned domain,
  `setup/collect_vars`) is given `{machines: {console: [<site>-machine-admins], remote: [<site>-machine-admins]}}`;
  an upgrade or a reinstall is not.
- **The GPOs** (`src/containers/samba/ensure_logon_policies.py`, at every convergence): one per OU of the map,
  `fabric: <site> log-on <OU path or site>`, linked to that OU; its `GptTmpl.inf` grants `SeInteractiveLogonRight` to
  the console groups and `SeRemoteInteractiveLogonRight` to the remote groups, each followed by `fabric-break-glass`
  and `BUILTIN\Administrators`, and carries the `[Group Membership]` part as before (the `windows-admins` of the site
  and the sites above made local Administrators). The site's other fabric log-on GPOs (the legacy one, or an OU's no
  longer named) are removed: unlinked everywhere, the container and its SYSVOL folder deleted (`remove_gpo`). An OU that
  does not exist, or a granted group since removed, is reported in the convergence's output and skipped, never fatal.
- **The editor** saves to the settings and applies (as `dhcp on` does; `--no-apply` only saves), audited
  `LOGON_POLICY_SET` / `LOGON_POLICY_CLEAR`. `set` with one right left out keeps that right as the OU has it. Its
  first edit while the setting is null reads today's list in the domain (directory operation `logon_groups`) and makes
  it the site OU's policy first. `clear` refuses an OU without its own policy and a clear that would leave neither the
  site's OU nor `OU=machines` with one. Agent routes: GET `/v1/domain/logon` (`domain:read`), POST
  `/v1/domain/logon/set` and `/clear` (`gpo:admin`). `fabricctl domain status` shows each OU's policy.
- A site the root prepares for its join (`prepare_site`) is converged there with the legacy GPO until its own DC
  converges with its own setting and replaces it.

## 1.6.11.5 Machines sorted by OS family

As decision 2.1.6.36: each site's `OU=machines` gets `OU=windows` and `OU=linux`; the Linux join places a machine in
`OU=linux` and writes `operatingSystem` / `operatingSystemVersion` from `/etc/os-release` (a join with a site admin's
password: a one-time join signs in as the computer itself, which AD does not let write them, so a pre-created machine's
family is its OU, given by `add-machine --os`, and its version is not recorded; found by the samba suite, 2026-10-10); `add-machine` pre-creates in
the OU for the OS it is given (`--os linux|windows`); a Windows machine that joined itself into `OU=machines` is moved
once by a timer from its `operatingSystem`: `fabric-machines.timer`, hourly, runs `fabricctl domain sort-machines
--scheduled`, and the machines it moved are recorded on the host (`config/machines-sorted.json`), so one an admin
moves back stays where they put it. fabric never moves a machine again, nor touches admins' sub-OUs. Machines joined
before 0.9 stay in `OU=machines` (an admin moves them, or leaves them). The OUs
carry policy for convenience (1.6.11.4); security stays on groups.

## 1.6.11.6 Machines fabric manages

A machine in `<site>-fabric-machines` is one fabric configures beyond the join (2.1.6.40, 2.1.6.43). The Linux join adds
it by default; `--unmanaged` leaves it a plain member (joined, sssd and sudo as today, nothing more). On a managed
Linux machine the join also:

- **SSH:** `GSSAPIAuthentication yes` and `PasswordAuthentication no` (Kerberos only by default, 2.1.6.39; a site may
  turn passwords or certificates on, 1.6.11.9), `UsePAM yes` so the log-on policy (1.6.11.4) applies to SSH; a drop-in
  in `/etc/ssh/sshd_config.d/`, the original kept for the uninstall.
- **DNS:** systemd-resolved pointed at fabric's DNS with the domain as search domain, the machine's other links left as
  they are (2.1.6.31; the manual said so before it was built, 1.6.7.5).
- **Its OU and OS attributes** (1.6.11.5); the break-glass password timer when that is built (1.7.1.3).

**How a machine becomes one** (settled while building it: a one-time join signs in as the computer, which cannot
add itself to a group, and not every admin who joins may change the site's groups): `add-machine` makes the
pre-created account a member, unless `--unmanaged` (then the account's description says so); a machine joined with
an admin's password lands in `OU=linux` and fabric's hourly sort (`fabricctl domain sort-machines`, the timer of
1.6.11.5) makes it a member, once, unless its join said `--unmanaged` (written on its description by the join).
A machine an admin takes out stays out (the sort records what it adopted). The web console adds and removes
machines (`machines:admin`).

**On the machine** the join installs `/usr/local/sbin/fabric-managed` and `fabric-managed.timer` (hourly): signed in
as the machine (its keytab), it asks the DC whether it is in `<site>-fabric-machines`, and applies the configuration
while it is, removes it when it is not. The join applies it at once on a managed join. SSH's drop-in is
`/etc/ssh/sshd_config.d/10-fabric.conf` (first: sshd keeps the first value it reads, and Ubuntu's cloud images turn
passwords on in `50-cloud-init.conf`); passwords are off for every account, local ones too, while keys keep
working. DNS: `/etc/systemd/resolved.conf.d/fabric.conf`, fabric's DNS for the domain's names only (`~<domain>`),
other names as the machine had them. SSH is configured only where an SSH server is installed; the join installs
none.

## 1.6.11.7 Host groups and sudo rules

As decision 2.1.6.31: **host groups** are AD groups `<site>-hosts-<name>` in the site's `OU=groups`, machines as
members, usable in log-on policies (1.6.11.4) and sudo rules; `fabricctl machines groups`, `fabricctl machines group
add|remove <group> <machine>`, the web console; `machines:admin`. **Sudo rules** beyond the site's: who (people or
groups), which commands, as whom, on which host group, stored as `sudoRole` objects in the site's `OU=sudoers` (nested
sites inherit, as the join's search bases already do); `fabricctl sudo list|add|remove`, the web console; permission
`sudo:admin` (new, in the admin bundle). A rule granting ALL commands to everyone is refused.

## 1.6.11.8 The fabric host as a member of its domain

As decision 2.1.6.34: setup makes the fabric host a member of its own domain unless the Advanced option leaves it out
(`host_join_domain`, default true; Advanced asks "Make this host a member of its domain [Y/n]", ID
`setup.host_join_domain`). The DC runs on the host under the host's name, so the host signs in with the DC's own
machine account: a setup step `member`, after `start` (the DC must answer), exports that account's keys to the host's
keytab (`samba-tool domain exportkeytab` for `<HOST>$` and its `host/` names only, never the whole domain's; mode
0600), writes `/etc/krb5.conf` and `/etc/sssd/sssd.conf` against the local DC as the Linux join does (1.6.7.3), and
enables `sss` in NSS and PAM (`pam-auth-update`), with sudo from the domain's rules (1.6.11.3).

- **Who may sign in to the host with a domain account:** the fabric admins only (`fabric-admins` and the site's
  `<site>-fabric-admins`), set on the host itself (sssd `access_provider = simple`). The host's computer account is the
  DC's, in AD's `OU=Domain Controllers`, so the sites' per-OU policies (1.6.11.4) never reach it; and the appliance is
  the fabric admins' to manage (2.1.6.41).
- **Local accounts, root and local sudo are untouched**, so the host is administered as before with the DC down (and
  a domain account that signed in before keeps signing in from sssd's cache).
- **Consent:** a group of its own, `member` ("This host as a member of its domain", recommended; manual 1.2.9.3):
  the packages (`sssd-ad`, `libnss-sss`, `libpam-sss`, `libsss-sudo`, `krb5-user`) and the files above. Declining it,
  or `host_join_domain: false`, leaves the host as it is; status shows it as a relaxation. An upgrade asks it like any
  new group (an unattended one names it: `--approve member`).
- **Undo:** `fabricctl setup --undo member` and the uninstall put back each file as it was (`common/keep_original`),
  remove the keytab and turn `sss` off in NSS and PAM; the packages stay (as for the other groups' packages).
- **Tests:** the sandbox (the host is its systemd container): a fabric admin resolves and signs in (PAM), has sudo from
  the site's rule; a person outside the fabric admins is refused; root and a local account keep working with the DC
  stopped; undo puts everything back; the consent suite plans the group.
- **As built** (2026-10-10; `setup/join_host_domain.py`, `undo/undo_member.py`): the keys are read from the samba
  container (`samba-tool domain exportkeytab --principal=` once for `<HOST>$`, `host/<hostname_dc>` and
  `host/<HOST>`, in the container's `/tmp`, removed at once) and the keytab is replaced only when its keys differ, so
  a second run changes nothing. Two packages beside the design's, as the Linux join installs them: `sssd-tools`
  (`sssctl`) and `libsasl2-modules-gssapi-mit`, without which sssd cannot bind to the DC (found by the sandbox
  suite). sssd talks to this host's DC only (`ad_server = <hostname_dc>`, no dynamic DNS
  updates: the DC keeps its own records). Two host changes the design did not name, both in the consent's list: the
  DC's name goes into `/etc/hosts` when the host cannot resolve it (its own resolver may not know the AD zone), and
  where sudo-rs is the default (Ubuntu 26.04) the classic sudo becomes it, as the Linux join does, since sudo-rs
  cannot read the domain's rules. What was on before (the PAM profiles, sssd enabled, sudo's alternative) is kept in
  `config/member.json` for the undo. `host_join_domain: false` (checked: true or false only) is listed in status's
  relaxed settings; Advanced is now offered on every interactive setup, since this choice exists after the CA does.

## 1.6.11.9 SSH certificates (an option)

OpenBao's SSH engine (2.1.7.5, revised by 2.1.6.39) is **off by default**: a site turns it on in the web console. Then
fabric admins get 8-hour user certificates (the user name the one principal), managed machines trust the user CA, and
host certificates reach them through AD (2.3.6.4.1). Built last in 0.9, after everything above; until then a managed
machine signs in with Kerberos only.

## 1.6.11.10 Entry points and tests

New entry points, each with a positive and a negative test (global Rule 9, 3.1.2.5): `fabricctl domain logon
show|set|clear`, `fabricctl machines groups|group`, `fabricctl sudo list|add|remove`, `add-machine --os`, the join's
`--unmanaged`, their agent routes and web forms, the setting `host_join_domain`. Tests: the samba suite's containers
(groups, the sudo move, log-on policies per OU, OUs by OS, the join's managed configuration on Ubuntu 24.04 and 26.04
and Debian), the sandbox (the fabric host as a member), and the Ubuntu 26.04 VM on the lab switch (SSH with Kerberos
from another member, refused for a person outside the OU's groups).
