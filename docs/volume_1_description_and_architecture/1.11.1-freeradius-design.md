## 1.11.1.1 802.1X (FreeRADIUS)

> **Built (roadmap 4, first pass):** FreeRADIUS 3.2 (Debian's packages on
> the pinned Debian base; 4.4 is upstream's supported line, 2.1.1.5) in a
> non-root container without capabilities. **EAP-TLS** against the fabric
> CA and **MAB**, each decided per request by fabric's policy (2.1.11.2): the
> device linked to the certificate's SHA-256 fingerprint, or owning the MAC,
> must be enabled and hold `network:eap-tls` / `network:mab`; the reply
> carries its role's VLAN. Fail closed when the directory cannot be asked.
> RADIUS clients in `vars.yaml` with secrets in OpenBao, Message-Authenticator
> required per client (BlastRADIUS), `fabricctl radius`, the FreeRADIUS tab
> (clients, recent decisions), turned on after the install (2.1.2.16).
>
> **Built (second pass): people by password.** EAP-TTLS/PAP inside a TLS
> tunnel to `radius.<domain>`; the password is checked by binding to the
> site's domain controller (AD) as the person (the domain's lockout counts
> network logins; S3.2, 1.6.5.17). Only
> members of groups mapped in `radius_people` may join, each mapping with
> an optional VLAN and a priority (lowest wins), managed with `fabricctl
> radius map-group` and on the FreeRADIUS tab. No TTLS at all while no group
> is mapped. Network logins have no second factor.
>
> **Long-term targets (later passes, all planned):** RadSec (RADIUS over TLS, TCP 2083) with Step-CA
> certificates; CoA / Disconnect from the UI when a device is disabled;
> SCEP enrolment; per-VLAN DHCP subnets and DDNS subzones; MAB keyed off
> Kea reservations. Revocation today is unlinking the certificate or
> disabling the device (effective at the next authentication, no session
> resumption); CRL/OCSP later.
>
> **Built (0.9, untested):** vendor profiles for UniFi and Cisco (1.11.1.2), RadSec for Cisco (1.11.1.3), sessions
> from accounting with Disconnect and CoA for Cisco (1.11.1.4); the lab suite written (1.11.1.5).

- **FreeRADIUS 3.2** container; RADIUS clients = switches/APs listed in
  `fabric.yaml` (`radius_clients:` with per-client shared secrets in
  `fabric-secrets.yml`). RadSec (RADIUS over TLS, Step-CA certs) where the
  switch supports it.
- **Primary method: EAP-TLS** with device/user certificates from Step-CA.
  - Enrolment: Step-CA **SCEP provisioner** for MDM/network gear, ACME for
    Linux hosts, `fabricctl --client-cert` / web UI for manual issuance.
  - SCEP requires an RSA intermediate — fabric's default
    (`cert_intermediate_key_type: rsa`) already satisfies this.
  - Revocation: short-lived certs (Step-CA passive revocation) plus an
    OCSP/CRL check in FreeRADIUS.
- **Username/password: EAP-TTLS/PAP and PEAP-MSCHAPv2 against the site's
  DC.** PEAP (what Windows offers by default) for people and domain
  machines: FreeRADIUS hands the MS-CHAPv2 answer to `ntlm_auth`, which asks
  the DC through its winbind (the DC's sockets mounted read-only), so
  FreeRADIUS never holds a password or an NT hash. People and machines join
  only through a mapped group (machines: an enabled computer in the site's
  `OU=machines`, 2.1.11.3); both methods are off while no group is mapped
  (S3.3, 1.6.5.17).
- **MAB** (MAC auth bypass) for printers/IoT, keyed off Kea reservations —
  a device reserved in DHCP can be admitted to its VLAN by MAC.
- **Dynamic VLANs and access (built: device RBAC, in AD since S3):** devices
  live in the site's `OU=devices` and name roles in the site's or the
  organisation's `OU=device-roles` (1.6.3.4), read with the site's
  `fabric-radius-<site>` account over LDAPS to the host's address. A role
  grants permissions (`network:eap-tls`, `network:mab`, …) and optionally a
  VLAN (lowest priority number wins). FreeRADIUS looks the device up by
  certificate fingerprint (EAP-TLS) or MAC (MAB), refuses disabled devices
  and devices without the permission, and returns the role's VLAN as
  `Tunnel-Private-Group-Id`. People stay in Keycloak. CoA/Disconnect for
  quarantine from the UI.

## 1.11.1.2 Switches and access points by vendor (NAS profiles)

Designed and built 2026-10-12 for 0.9 (decisions 2.1.11.1, 2.1.11.4); **built, untested** (2.3.11.5). Every RADIUS
client (a NAS: a switch, an access point, a controller) names its **vendor**: `unifi`, `cisco` or `generic` (the
default, and what a client added before 0.9 becomes). The vendor picks a **profile**
(`fabriclib/radius/nas/profiles.py`, the one place these facts live) that drives what FreeRADIUS returns, how a MAB
request is read, whether RadSec and CoA are offered, and which setup page the console shows.

| | `unifi` (UniFi Network: its switches and APs) | `cisco` (Catalyst, IOS-XE) | `generic` |
|---|---|---|---|
| VLAN in the Access-Accept | `Tunnel-Type` = VLAN (13), `Tunnel-Medium-Type` = IEEE-802 (6), `Tunnel-Private-Group-Id` = the VLAN id (RFC 3580) | the same | the same |
| A MAB request is | `User-Name` = the MAC, `User-Password` = the same MAC | `Service-Type` = Call-Check (10), `User-Name` = the MAC (`aabbccddeeff` by default), `Calling-Station-Id` = the MAC (`AA-BB-CC-DD-EE-FF` by default) | `User-Name` = the MAC |
| MAB is refused when | `User-Name` is not a MAC; a `User-Password` that is not the same MAC; a `Calling-Station-Id` naming another MAC | it is not Call-Check; `User-Name` is not a MAC; a `Calling-Station-Id` naming another MAC | `User-Name` is not a MAC; a `Calling-Station-Id` naming another MAC |
| RadSec (1.11.1.3) | not offered: Ubiquiti's RadSec is documented for UniFi Network 8.4+ Wi-Fi profiles only (2.3.11.5) | offered | not offered |
| CoA / Disconnect (1.11.1.4) | refused, with why: Ubiquiti documents neither | Disconnect-Request, and CoA-Request with `Cisco-AVPair` `subscriber:command=reauthenticate` or `bounce-host-port`, to UDP 3799 | refused, with why |
| Setup page (4.11.1) | where each setting is in UniFi Network | an IOS-XE configuration generated for that switch, without its secret | the generic table |

Every MAC spelling (`aa:bb:..`, `AA-BB-..`, `aabb.ccdd.eeff`, `AABBCCDDEEFF`) is read as the same MAC, so a switch set
to another MAC format still works; what a profile adds is the shape of a genuine MAB request. How the policy knows
the vendor: `fabric-radius.json` carries, per client name, its profile's policy part (`vendor`, `mab`, `vlan`),
rendered from the profiles; the sites hand the policy the client's `shortname` (`Tmp-String-3`). A client the
policy does not find there is treated as `generic`.

A client's vendor and transport are set when it is added (`fabricctl radius add-client … --vendor cisco`, the console's
form) and changed later (`fabricctl radius set-client`, the console's row). A vendor that is not one of the three is
refused with the list; so is RadSec for a vendor whose profile does not offer it.

## 1.11.1.3 RadSec: RADIUS over TLS

Designed and built 2026-10-12 for 0.9 (decision 2.1.11.5); **built, untested** (2.3.11.5). RADIUS over UDP protects
the shared secret and the passwords inside it with MD5 only; RadSec (RFC 6614) carries the same RADIUS inside TLS
over TCP 2083, with both ends proving themselves by certificate.

- **Per client:** `transport: udp | radsec` (default `udp`); `radsec` only for a profile that offers it (Cisco).
- **FreeRADIUS** listens on TCP 2083 at the host's address while at least one client uses RadSec (the
  `fabric-radsec` virtual server; its port is published, and let through the firewall for the RADIUS clients, only
  then). TLS 1.2, its certificate the server's (`radius.<domain>`, which the switch must match), and a **client
  certificate required**: chained to the fabric CA, revocation checked against the CRL in `ca.pem` (as EAP-TLS,
  2.1.5.10), from the client's address, and with the common name **`radsec-<client name>`**; any other certificate
  is refused before any request is decided (logged `REJECT method=radsec reason=certificate_not_this_client`). The
  RadSec clients are their own client list (`clients radsec`), so a RadSec client's address is never answered over
  UDP.
- **The shared secret** inside TLS is the fixed `radsec` (RFC 6614 §2.3). The client still has its own secret
  (generated, in OpenBao): it signs the CoA and Disconnect packets fabric sends it over UDP (1.11.1.4).
- **The switch's certificate:** the switch makes its own key and a request (CN `radsec-<name>`); it is signed on the
  Step-CA tab (Sign a CSR), so the key never leaves the switch. 4.11.1.3 has the IOS-XE steps.
- **Before the policy runs**, the NAS certificate's `TLS-Client-Cert-*` attributes are taken off the request, so
  EAP-TLS in the same request sees only the device's own certificate.
- **A client on UDP is a relaxation** (global Rule 10): `fabricctl status` and the console's Overview list each one
  (`radius_clients: <name> on RADIUS/UDP`) with its effect: its shared secret and the RADIUS inside are protected by
  MD5 only, so it belongs on a trusted LAN. UniFi clients stay on UDP until UniFi's switches are proven to speak
  RadSec (2.3.11.5).

## 1.11.1.4 Sessions, Disconnect and CoA

Designed and built 2026-10-12 for 0.9 (decisions 2.1.11.6, 2.1.11.8); **built, untested** (2.3.11.5).

- **Sessions from accounting.** Accounting is no longer only acknowledged: fabric's policy logs every
  Accounting-Request as one `fabric: ACCT …` line in FreeRADIUS's journal (status, session id, MAC, client, NAS
  address, port, user; values URL-quoted), nothing else stored. `fabriclib/radius/list_radius_sessions` reads the
  last 26 hours of them: a session is the latest record per client and `Acct-Session-Id`, open until a Stop; who it
  is and its VLAN come from the latest `ACCEPT` for its MAC. A switch that sends no accounting has no sessions to
  show (the setup pages turn accounting on, with interim updates).
- **The console's Sessions view** (FreeRADIUS tab, `radius:read`) lists the open sessions: device or person, MAC,
  VLAN, client and port, last seen; and per session, for a client whose profile supports it, **Disconnect**,
  **Re-authenticate** (the device authenticates again, so a changed role or VLAN applies) and **Move VLAN** (bounce
  the port: the device also asks DHCP again, on its new VLAN). These need the new permission **`radius:coa`**
  (Admin and Equipment operator bundles). The CLI: `fabricctl radius sessions`, `fabricctl radius disconnect`,
  `fabricctl radius reauthenticate [--bounce]`.
- **Sending.** fabric-agent sends the packet itself, from the host, over UDP to the session's NAS address (it must
  lie inside the client's address) on the profile's CoA port, signed with the client's shared secret (RFC 5176's
  Request Authenticator) and carrying a Message-Authenticator; the session is named by `Acct-Session-Id`,
  `Calling-Station-Id` and `NAS-IP-Address`. Three tries, three seconds each. The answer is checked (Response
  Authenticator, and its Message-Authenticator when present) and shown: ACK, or NAK with its `Error-Cause`, or no
  answer with what to check on the switch.
- **Refused, with why, and nothing sent:** a vendor whose profile has no CoA (UniFi: Ubiquiti documents none; the
  answer says what to do instead), Move VLAN for a vendor without a port bounce, a session that is closed or unknown,
  a NAS address outside the client's address, a client without a secret, 802.1X off, and (403) a person without
  `radius:coa`. Every attempt that reaches a switch is audited (`RADIUS_COA`, with its answer).
- **Revocation stays at the next authentication** for vendors without CoA; for Cisco, disabling a device and then
  Re-authenticate (or Disconnect) applies it at once. CoA sent by itself when a device is disabled is not built
  (2.3.11.5, an open point).

## 1.11.1.5 The 802.1X lab suite and the Windows checks

Designed and written 2026-10-12 for 0.9 (decision 2.1.11.7); **written, not run** (2.3.11.5). The end-to-end tests
of 2.3.11.3, on the lab of 2.3.11.4 (the Incus cluster, 2.1.1.50):

- **`tests/lab/dot1x.sh`**, run on the fabric host: drives the authenticator (`hostapd` with its `wired` driver,
  dynamic VLANs) and the supplicants over SSH (`wpa_supplicant` with its `wired` driver), and checks each method
  accepted with its role's VLAN (the port's traffic tagged towards fabric, a DHCP lease from that VLAN), refusals
  (a disabled device, a wrong password, no mapped group), a port with no supplicant (nothing, or MAB by permission),
  an accounting session listed, and CoA refused for the `generic` authenticator.
- **`tests/lab/windows_checks.ps1`**, run on a Windows member as Administrator: W1–W4 and W6–W9 of 2.3.6.1.19, each
  `PASS`/`FAIL` with what it saw; W5 is checked on the fabric host (`dot1x.sh` with `WINDOWS_MACHINE` set reads the
  decisions).

## 1.11.1.7 MAB from a DHCP reservation

Designed and built 2026-10-12 for 0.9, after dev/0.8.0's address manager merged (2.3.11.6; its decision waits for a
register number, 2.3.11.6.1); **built, untested**. A device that has a reservation in the address plan (1.10.4.3: a
MAC's fixed address) can be admitted by MAB without a device entry of its own:

- **The reservation names a device role** (`fabricctl ipam address add|set <ip> --role <role>`; a reservation only).
  The role is kept on the reservation's entry as `fabricRoleName`, with AD's existing auxiliary class
  `fabricDeviceRoles` (1.6.3.4), so the schema does not change. Setting it is refused for a role the directory does
  not have; a role that does not grant `network:mab`, or whose VLAN is not the reservation's, is warned about.
- **The decision** (fabric's policy, 2.1.11.2): a MAB request whose MAC no device has is looked up among the site's
  reservations (`OU=networks`, `fabricAddressKind=reservation`, `fabricMac`). It is accepted when the reservation's
  role grants `network:mab`, on **the reservation's VLAN** (its subnet's: the address only works there), or the role's
  VLAN when the subnet has none. A MAC that a device has is decided by the device alone (a disabled device is
  refused, never let in through its reservation). Logged `ACCEPT method=mab device=<reservation's name or address>
  source=reservation`; refused with why (`reservation has no role`, `no role grants network:mab`).
- Removing the reservation, or its role, takes effect at the device's next authentication (and at once with CoA on
  Cisco, 1.11.1.4).

## 1.11.1.8 A role's VLAN is a VLAN of the plan

Designed and built 2026-10-12 for 0.9 (2.3.11.6; the decision waits for a register number); **built, untested**.
1.10.4.5 says a device role's VLAN names a VLAN of the address plan:

- **Saving a role with a VLAN** (the Directory tab, `POST /v1/roles`) checks it against this site's plan, read from
  its DC: when the plan has VLANs, the role's must be one of them, or it is refused naming the plan's VLANs and how
  to add one (`fabricctl ipam vlan add`). A site whose plan has no VLAN yet (not using the address manager) keeps
  bare numbers, as before 0.8. The DC not answering refuses the save (fail loudly), as the save itself would.
- **The console shows the VLAN as the plan names it**: "20 (iot, 10.1.20.0/24)" on the Roles table, read from the plan
  the site last kept (1.10.4.14), so the page asks no DC.
- Roles saved before the plan existed keep their numbers; a VLAN that later leaves the plan shows without a label.
- The groups mapped for password logins (`radius_people`, 1.11.1.1) still take a bare VLAN number (2.3.11.6, an
  open point).
