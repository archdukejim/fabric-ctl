## 2.1.12.1 Is the DNS filter part of an install, and what does it start with

- **Decision:** **On by default** (`dns_filter: adguard`; `none` turns it off), shown on setup's plan and asked in Advanced. A first deploy sends internet lookups to **Cloudflare** over DNS-over-HTTPS (by address) and turns on the **AdGuard DNS filter** list ([1.12.1.3](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.12.1-adguard.md#11213-settings)).
- **Shapes:** 1.12, 3.3
- **Made:** 2026-10-07, by the owner, in 0.6.0. Replaces the 2026-10-01 choice of local DNS only.
- **Why:** the owner expected the filter in the stack; [2.3.6.1.27](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#236127-before-060-uninstall-reinstall).
- **Status:** In force; **revised by 2.1.12.3 on 2026-10-08**: the filter is BIND's resolver, not AdGuard; internet names go to Cloudflare over DoT, not DoH; lists are converted to RPZ from AdGuard's catalogue, with the AdGuard DNS filter still the default. Still on by default.

## 2.1.12.2 DNS-over-TLS on 853, answered by AdGuard

- **Decision:** AdGuard answers DoT with the DNS name's certificate (or the public wildcard), so encrypted queries are filtered too; 853 joins the ports only with the filter on; doctor checks DoH's `/dns-query`.
- **Shapes:** 1.12, 1.4
- **Made:** 2026-10-08, by the owner, for 0.6.3.
- **Why:** 2.3.1.11.6.
- **Status:** In force; the DoH check **built 2026-10-08** (doctor, `verify_install`: a DNS query over `https://<dns name>/dns-query` must answer the host's address). **Revised by 2.1.12.3 on 2026-10-08**: the BIND resolver answers DoT, not AdGuard, in 0.7 (not 0.6.3); built after the spike ([2.3.12.1](2.3.12-dns-filter-notes.md#23121-proposal-07--bind-filters-dns-managed-in-fabrics-web-console-and-adguard-home-goes)). **Built 2026-10-10** (1.12.2.16, 2.3.12.1.24): DoT on 853 and DoH through nginx, both answered by the resolver with the DNS name's certificate; doctor checks DoT too.

## 2.1.12.3 Which engine filters DNS, and where it is managed

- **Decision:** A second BIND (`bind9-resolver`) answers clients and filters with response policy zones; lists, rules, per-client groups, the query log and statistics are managed in fabric's web console; AdGuard Home is removed, its settings imported on upgrade (anything that cannot move is reported). Internet names go to Cloudflare over DoT by address; the lists are **AdGuard's catalogue** (its HostlistsRegistry, read live), any added on demand and converted to RPZ (rules RPZ cannot express are skipped and counted), with the **AdGuard DNS filter** on by default; client groups are addresses and subnets (devices, roles and DHCP scopes with Kea in 0.8), each a view chained to the main one, so the lists are loaded once and a group only adds — safe search, its own lists and rules, names it allows — and never switches a shared list off (owner 2026-10-09, after the spike); the resolver's memory limit is 384 MiB, each list's cost shown before it is added; a blocked name answers NXDOMAIN (no block page: it cannot work for HTTPS); statistics are kept 90 days; safe search per group, off by default, comes in the same release; no services are blocked as such and there are no schedules (2.3.12.1.14). A spike on the test Pi comes first ([2.3.12.1](2.3.12-dns-filter-notes.md#23121-proposal-07--bind-filters-dns-managed-in-fabrics-web-console-and-adguard-home-goes)).
- **Shapes:** 1.12, 1.4, 1.8, 3.3, 4.12
- **Made:** 2026-10-08, by the owner ("bind9 replaces adguard with our ui handling"), first for 0.6.3, then **0.7, a release of its own** (the owner, the same day: "a new feature and a major milestone"; the later releases moved up one, 1.1.4.6); the questions of 2.3.12.1.11 answered the same day.
- **Why:** one DNS engine and one UI; per-client policy from fabric's own device and network data; 2.3.12.1.10.
- **Status:** In force; being built for 0.7 — the spike done (2.3.12.1.16–17), step 2 (the resolver), step 3 (the move from AdGuard Home, which is gone), step 4 (the query log), step 5 (the console's tab) and step 6 (client groups, safe search, DoT and DoH for clients) built (2.3.12.1.18–21, 2.3.12.1.23–24): the design is built; the sandbox run and the upgrade test on the Pi come before 0.7 is cut. Revises 2.1.12.1 and 2.1.12.2. **Revised by 2.1.12.6 on 2026-10-11:** the lists move from
  BIND's response policy zones to AdGuard Home behind BIND (0.7.0); the rest stands.

## 2.1.12.4 How long the DNS query log is kept, and who reads it

- **Decision:** Query-log entries are kept **7 days** and read by **admins only** (a permission of their own); statistics are kept as counts without client addresses.
- **Shapes:** 1.12, 1.8
- **Made:** 2026-10-08, by the owner, for the filter proposed in 2.1.12.3.
- **Why:** enough to trace a block reported days later; the log names who looked up what, so it is personal data and kept short and narrow.
- **Status:** In force; built in 0.7, step 4 (2.3.12.1.20): queries purged after 7 days, statistics after 90; `dns:querylog` in the admin bundle only.

## 2.1.12.5 Where the query log is kept, and how it is collected

- **Decision:** In fabric's **Postgres**, as the design proposed (a `dnslog` database of its own), and read from the
  resolver's log files by a **timer every 5 minutes and before every read** (so what is shown is current), not by an
  always-running service ([1.12.2.13](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.12.2-bind-resolver.md#112213-the-query-log-and-statistics)).
- **Shapes:** 1.12, 1.3, 1.8
- **Made:** 2026-10-09, by the owner, asked when step 4 was built: a SQLite file on the host was offered (no new
  dependency, independent of Keycloak) and declined for Postgres; the timer was the proposed default.
- **Why:** Postgres as designed; the timer keeps one unit fewer running, and reading on demand removes its delay.
- **Status:** In force; built in 0.7, step 4 (2.3.12.1.20).

## 2.1.12.6 The lists are matched by AdGuard Home, behind BIND

- **Decision:** BIND's resolver keeps answering clients (plain DNS, DoT, DoH), fabric's zones (never filtered), the
  owner's allow and block rules and safe search (small response policy zones, as built), the groups (views), and the
  query log and statistics (fabric's, from BIND, with dnstap for the answers). Names it does not answer itself it
  forwards to **AdGuard Home**, which only matches the lists: AdGuard's catalogue as checkboxes, refreshed daily, held
  in its own compact matcher; a blocked name answers NXDOMAIN (`blocking_mode: nxdomain`). Each group's view forwards
  from an address of its own, which AdGuard Home knows as a client, so a group's own lists and rules apply there.
  AdGuard Home is driven only through its API by fabric-agent; its web UI is never published (its port on
  `fabric_net` only, its account a generated password in fabric's secrets) and its own query log and statistics are
  off. It runs as a non-root user, every capability dropped but NET_BIND_SERVICE kept in the bounding set,
  `no-new-privileges`, port 53 through the network namespace's `ip_unprivileged_port_start`; fabric renders its
  `AdGuardHome.yaml`, so it never has a first launch (which demands root). Upstream: Cloudflare over DoT, as before.
- **Shapes:** 1.12, 1.4, 1.8, 1.2, 3.3, 4.12
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner, after the spike ([2.3.1.18](2.3.1-product-notes.md#23118-proposal-070-dns-made-right-and-adguard-home-reconsidered)): "go with adguard behind bind"; earlier the same day
  "consider returning adguardhome using api instead of it's default web ui", "can we use bind 9 and point at adguard
  as upstream?" and "why don't we take the query feature built, keep it, and then disable it for adguard, keep those
  system resources".
- **Why:** measured on the owner's 15 lists (2.77 million rules, 2.3.1.18): AdGuard Home holds them in about 350 MiB
  (345 MiB with a 16th list added, after a restart) where BIND needs 2.3 GiB (4.6 million records even merged:
  deduplication saved 8.6%); a list is enabled in under a second instead of a 29-second merge and reload; a restart
  blocks again in 3.5 s instead of 15 s; a blocked answer costs about half a millisecond more. BIND in front stays
  at 45 MiB and keeps everything already built that does not need the lists.
- **Impact:** the resolver's list zones, their converter's use at runtime and the lists job's conversion are replaced
  by AdGuard Home's lists; a new container and image (pinned and published like the others, 2.1.14); the console's
  DNS filter page reads and writes lists through AdGuard's API; the query log takes the answers from dnstap and asks
  AdGuard's `check_host` (cached, per name) whether an NXDOMAIN was a block and which list's (a blocked answer carries
  no marker of its own through BIND). No released install ran BIND's lists (0.7 was never released); test hosts on a
  0.8.0 candidate have their lists moved at the upgrade.
- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-11 for 0.7.0 (2.1.1.49). Revises 2.1.12.3. As designed and built (1.12.3, its
  differences listed in 1.12.3.15): a group reaches AdGuard over DoT with its name as ClientID, not from an address
  of its own; the answers come from BIND's `responselog`, not dnstap; groups have no lists of their own (the owner,
  2026-10-11); AdGuard reaches the upstreams by address.

## 2.1.12.7 Where DNSSEC is validated

- **Decision:** **option A**: the resolver validates (`dnssec-validation auto`), fabric's own zones excepted
  (`validate-except`: they are never signed), and AdGuard Home passes every DS query (fabric's own first rule,
  `@@||*^$dnstype=DS,important`, never the owner's to remove). Background: 1.12.3.2 had the resolver not validate (`dnssec-validation no`) and AdGuard Home validate
  (`enable_dnssec`). Measured on the test Pi (2.3.1.19.3): nothing validates. BIND, when it does not validate, sets
  the CD ("checking disabled") flag on every query it forwards (`lib/ns/query.c`, `lib/dns/resolver.c`, BIND 9.20),
  so AdGuard Home and Cloudflare return even bogus data: `dnssec-failed.org` resolves. The options, each tried on the
  test Pi:
  - **A, recommended:** BIND validates (`dnssec-validation auto`) and AdGuard Home always passes DS queries (a
    fabric-owned allow rule, `@@||*^$dnstype=DS`), so BIND can prove a blocked name's zone unsigned. Bogus names
    SERVFAIL, signed answers carry AD, a blocked name in an unsigned zone (`ads.doubleclick.net`) still answers
    NXDOMAIN; a blocked zone's own apex (`doubleclick.net`) and any blocked name in a signed zone answer SERVFAIL
    (still blocked, but a client with a second resolver may retry there).
  - **B:** BIND validates, no DS rule: every blocked name answers SERVFAIL.
  - **C:** keep 1.12.3.2: no validation at all (as 0.7.0's candidate is).
- **Shapes:** 1.12
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner ("1 A"), after the host suite on the test Pi found it (0.7.0's candidate).
- **Why:** 1.12.3.2's reason rested on AdGuard Home validating, which BIND's CD flag defeats.
- **Status:** In force; built for 0.7.0 (2.1.1.49). `tests/resolver/run.py` and `tests/host/run.sh` check that a
  broken signature is refused. Revises 1.12.3.2's choice of where to validate.

## 2.1.12.8 AdGuard Home joins the memory budget; its rebuilds may briefly pass it, with a warning

- **Decision:** AdGuard Home's memory is planned with the others (1.2.4.3.5): the budget counts its lists' steady
  size (floor 384 MB, weight 1); its container's limit is 2.5 times its share, so the minute it compiles its lists
  fits. What that briefly takes past the budget is allowed and warned about (setup's plan, `fabricctl status`, the
  DNS filter's own procedure), never refused. A steady need past the budget is refused: the floors' check names the
  parts to turn off, and a list ticked past AdGuard Home's share is refused, saying how to make room.
- **Shapes:** 1.2, 1.12, 1.1
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner ("Is it just for temporary spikes when loading or constant memory load? If
  constant, I'd say no and inform users the selected items exceed memory budget. If just temporarily spikes, I find
  it acceptable, but we should warn users"); the spike is temporary (1.12.3.9: 751 MiB at most on the Pi while
  compiling, 380 MiB steady).
- **Why:** a floor holding the spike did not fit beside Keycloak on a 4 GB host (2.3.1.20.5); counting the steady
  size fits (3488 MB of floors with every part on, against 3584 MB) and keeps the owner's 16 lists (980 MiB limit
  at 4 GB).
- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-12 for 0.7.1. Settles 2.3.1.20.5.
