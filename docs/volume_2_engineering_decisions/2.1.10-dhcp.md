## 2.1.10.4 What a DHCP subnet's clients may reach

- **Decision:** a subnet fabric serves gets fabric's services by default: its clients may use the DNS (fabric's BIND
  and the filtering resolver), NTP and fabric's pages (the landing and certificate pages, the console's sign-in),
  exactly as the host's own LAN does: handing out addresses implies serving them. A subnet marked **guest** (for
  example the VLAN 802.1X puts unknown devices on) gets DNS through the filter and NTP only. SSH to the host stays
  for the host's LAN and `security.firewall_allow`, whatever DHCP serves.
- **Shapes:** 1.10, 1.4, 1.12, 1.2 (the firewall)
- **Made:** 2026-10-10, by the owner ("By default adds them, however, if subnets are created for guest subnets like
  with 802.1x those may be restricted").
- **Why:** a client that gets an address but cannot resolve names is broken; guests should not see management pages.
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8. **Revised 2026-10-12 (status only): built** (1.10.3.3: served subnets in
  BIND's, the resolver's, the firewall's and chrony's lists, `access: guest` DNS and NTP only;
  `dhcp/client_networks.py`); the lab suite `tests/lab/dhcp.sh` 25 passed, 0 failed, and the sandbox 183 passed, 0 failed, on `dev/0.8`
  ([2.3.10.2](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#23102-08-on-the-lab-what-the-baseline-showed-and-the-results)).

## 2.1.10.5 The DNS and NTP servers DHCP hands out

- **Decision:** by default a subnet's clients are told to use fabric's own address **on the interface that serves
  that subnet** for DNS and NTP (not `host_ip`, which they may not be able to reach). `dhcp.dns` and `dhcp.ntp`, and a
  subnet's own options, still override it.
- **Shapes:** 1.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-10, by the owner.
- **Why:** on a second interface, `host_ip` is on another network the clients have no route to.
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8. **Revised 2026-10-12 (status only): built** (1.10.3.2: the address on the
  serving interface, option 121 for `host_ip`, in `templates/kea/kea-dhcp4.conf.j2`); the lab suite `tests/lab/dhcp.sh` 25 passed, 0 failed, and the sandbox 183 passed, 0 failed, on `dev/0.8`
  ([2.3.10.2](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#23102-08-on-the-lab-what-the-baseline-showed-and-the-results)).

## 2.1.10.6 Changes applied by restarting Kea, through its control socket

- **Decision:** as built: a change renders Kea's configuration, checks it (`kea-dhcp4 -t`) and restarts Kea (a
  second or two; leases kept in memfile), and fabric reads leases through Kea's local control socket. The design's
  first plan, `config-set` over Kea's HTTPS API with client certificates, is not taken: nothing needs it while one
  host runs one Kea. It returns with HA (2.1.10.2).
- **Shapes:** 1.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-10, recorded when 0.8 was scoped (the build since roadmap 3; 1.10.1.1 corrected).
- **Why:** simpler and as safe: the check runs before the restart, and a restart loses no lease.
- **Status:** In force; built.

## 2.1.10.7 DHCP's PTR records live in fabric's reverse zones, kept across applies

- **Decision:** a reverse zone that covers DHCP's pools or reservations is dynamic: Kea's key may write PTR and DHCID
  records there (and nothing else, nowhere else). On every apply fabric freezes the zone, reads what BIND holds, keeps
  Kea's PTR and DHCID records for every address it has no static record of, and swaps in its regenerated file with
  them. Fabric's own PTRs win: a static record never lies in a pool (normalize_dhcp refuses it), and a reservation
  whose address also has a static record keeps the static PTR. A DHCP subnet whose reverse zone has no static record
  still gets the zone.
- **Shapes:** 1.10, 1.4
- **Made:** 2026-10-10, by the agent, while building 0.8's reverse DNS (2.1.10.3), the owner having asked for work
  without waiting for approval; flagged to the owner.
- **Why:** a pool and fabric's static records share their /24 reverse zone (`7.168.192.in-addr.arpa`), so a zone of
  DHCP's own (as `dhcp.<domain>` is for names, 2.1.4.1) is possible only when a pool fills a zone alone. Freezing
  writes BIND's copy, Kea's records included, so keeping them costs one read per apply.
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8. Narrows 2.1.4.1 for reverse zones: forward names stay in `dhcp.<domain>`.
  **Revised 2026-10-12 (status only): built** (1.10.3.4; the freeze and keep in the apply's zone install,
  `dns/install_zone_file.py`); the Kea suite registers a PTR with real Kea and BIND and the key is refused anything
  but PTR and DHCID; the lab suite `tests/lab/dhcp.sh` 25 passed, 0 failed, and the sandbox 183 passed, 0 failed, on `dev/0.8`
  ([2.3.10.2](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#23102-08-on-the-lab-what-the-baseline-showed-and-the-results)).

## 2.1.10.8 VLAN subnets are relayed to fabric by the router, which routes and enforces policy

- **Decision:** each VLAN has its own subnet, routed by the site's gateway (at the owner's house a UniFi gateway),
  which also enforces the policy between VLANs. fabric does not route, firewall between VLANs, or configure the
  switches and gateway. The gateway relays each VLAN's DHCP to fabric (a DHCP relay, "DHCP helper", per network),
  and Kea picks the subnet by the relay's address (1.10.3.2). fabric's procedure for the relay is the operator's
  step (Volume 4).
- **Shapes:** 1.10, 1.2, 4.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner: "the vlans will have a subnet assigned and it will route through a gateway, a
  dhcp helper rule will be needed on my router to assist"; who routes and enforces: "unifi".
- **Why:** the owner's network is UniFi with 802.1X and VLAN segmentation (2.3.1.10); its gateway already routes.
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8.0 ([2.3.10.3](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#23103-proposal-080-an-address-manager-for-vlan-segmented-sites)).

## 2.1.10.9 One /24 per VLAN, the VLAN ID its third octet

- **Decision:** "micro segmentation" is many small subnets, one per VLAN, not client classes inside one subnet. The
  convention fabric suggests: a /24 per VLAN whose third octet is the VLAN ID (VLAN 10, management:
  `10.<site>.10.0/24`; VLAN 255, storage: `10.<site>.255.0/24`). A suggestion, never forced: any subnet may be
  entered.
- **Shapes:** 1.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner: "many subnets e.g. mgmt is 10, 255 is storage (3rd octet with a /24)".
- **Why:** an address names its VLAN at a glance; four houses stay apart by their second octet (2.1.10.12).
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8.0 ([2.3.10.3](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#23103-proposal-080-an-address-manager-for-vlan-segmented-sites)).

## 2.1.10.10 0.8.0 is an address manager (IPAM), every address tracked, delivered whole

- **Decision:** fabric's DHCP becomes an address manager: every site's VLANs and subnets, their ranges (the gateway,
  static ranges, DHCP pools, reserved), and **every address**: static ones (with owner, MAC, name, notes, optionally
  published in DNS), reservations and live leases, shown together; an address claimed twice is refused. Subnets
  and the address manager are not split across releases: 0.8.0 carries all of it.
- **Shapes:** 1.10, 1.4, 1.8, 1.9
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner: "the subnet should not spit between releases, also this should look kind of
  like an IPAM solution so I can track all my IPs including static".
- **Why:** with dozens of VLANs over four houses, a spreadsheet beside fabric would drift.
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8.0 ([2.3.10.3](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#23103-proposal-080-an-address-manager-for-vlan-segmented-sites); all its points answered 2026-10-11); written into Volume 1 as [1.10.4](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.10.4-address-manager.md) on 2026-10-12.

## 2.1.10.11 Where the address plan is kept

- **Decision:** **in AD** (option A of [2.3.10.3](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#23103-proposal-080-an-address-manager-for-vlan-segmented-sites)): each site's VLANs, subnets, ranges, static addresses and DHCP options
  are objects in its own `OU=networks`; every DC holds the whole plan. An edit from the top is written on its DC and
  replicates to the house; each site's fabric watches its own OU (its DC's highest USN, every 30 s) and renders Kea,
  its DNS records and the access lists when it changes. A house cut off keeps serving from its own copy. Each
  site's `vars.yaml` keeps only what is the host's own (the interfaces Kea listens on). Rejected: each site's vars
  with edits sent through the federation endpoint (an offline house cannot be edited); fabric's database (one
  writer per row: an edit from the top would not be the house's).
- **Shapes:** 1.10, 1.9, 1.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner ("3 A").
- **Why:** the owner edits four houses from the top (2.1.9.19); the plan is already in AD (1.10.2.8) and AD already
  replicates it.
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8.0.
  **Revised 2026-10-23 by 2.1.10.35:** with no site OUs (2.1.6.72), the plan's entries live in the domain's one
  `OU=networks`, each named with its site; the rest stands.

## 2.1.10.12 Each site's address block, and the default split of a /24

- **Decision:** fabric **suggests** addresses from a class A plan: the second octet is the site (a number per site,
  unique, chosen at install or join), the third the VLAN (2.1.10.9): `10.<site>.<VLAN>.0/24`. Inside a /24 the
  suggested split is gateway `.1`, static `.2-.99`, DHCP `.100-.249`, reserved `.250-.254`, editable per VLAN.
  **Suggestions only:** a site's admins (and the owner above every site) may enter any subnet, split and routing,
  NAT or another path included; fabric checks only what it must (overlaps across the organisation where networks
  are routed together, `--allow-overlap` as today; one claim per address).
- **Shapes:** 1.10, 1.9
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner: "default, class A with second octet being site, but site admins (or god admin
  [aka me]) can choose to do anything, including NAT and route traffic some other way"; the split: "yes".
- **Why:** a readable default for four houses that never forces a network into it.
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8.0.
  **Revised 2026-10-23 by 2.1.10.34 and 2.1.10.36:** the site's number lives on its record in `CN=fabric,CN=System`,
  not on its OU; "a site's admins (and the owner above every site)" are whoever a grant gives `plan` at the site, and
  the Directory Administrators. The suggestion and the split stand.

## 2.1.10.13 Finding addresses in use that nobody recorded

- **Decision:** Kea pings an address before handing it out and skips one that answers (Kea 3.0's ping-check hook),
  and an admin can **scan a subnet on request** (ping and ARP from fabric's host; a routed VLAN answers what its
  gateway lets through). Neither ever blocks: an address found in use but unrecorded is **shown** ("in use,
  unrecorded", with the MAC when ARP saw it) and the console **asks whether to make it a reservation** for the device
  at that address (or a static record); the admin may also ignore it or register something else there.
- **Shapes:** 1.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner: "B, with the caveat that the it doesn't prevent you from registering, just
  informs and asks if you want to set a reservation for the existing device at that IP".
- **Why:** finding hand-set devices is the point of an IPAM, but the admin decides.
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8.0. Built 2026-10-12 on `dev/0.8.0-scan`, its tests written and not run
  (the owner's direction of 2026-10-12): [1.10.4.22](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.10.4-address-manager.md#110422-ping-check-and-the-scan-of-a-subnet-as-built);
  Kea 3.0's ping_check hook confirmed open source ([2.3.10.6](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#23106-ping-check-and-the-scan-built-untested)); how the scan runs: 2.1.10.26.

## 2.1.10.14 DHCP options are part of the plan, with netboot presets

- **Decision:** 0.8.0's address manager carries DHCP options at every level (organisation, site, subnet, client
  class, address; the most specific wins), any option by name or code with custom definitions, and the options a
  subnet or address ends up with shown with where each came from. Netboot presets (PXE for BIOS and UEFI by client
  architecture, iPXE chain-loading, UEFI HTTP boot) can be switched on per VLAN or address, pointing at a boot
  server ([2.3.10.3.10](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#2310310-dhcp-options-and-netboot)).
- **Shapes:** 1.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner: "a critical component to dhcp..... Dhcp options, I will likely want to include
  options for netboot".
- **Why:** options are what make DHCP useful beyond addresses; netboot needs per-architecture answers.
- **Shown by their standard names:** every option appears under its RFC name with its code (option 67
  "boot file name", 66 "TFTP server name", 6 "DNS servers", 42 "NTP servers"), never only a number. Almost all are
  unset. The options fabric itself sets because it provides the service (DNS, NTP, and the domain and search list)
  carry a **"provided by fabric"** marker: fabric runs that service and keeps the option's value with the stack;
  an admin may still override it (the marker then says so). Owner, 2026-10-11: "the options necessary should have
  their default names (e.g. option 67 being bootfile)... we know dns, and ntp will be there and while users can
  change that, we can put a nice little pill or some other indicator that fabric provides that service and manages
  it as a part of the stack".
- **Status:** In force; to build in 0.8.0 with the rest of 2.1.10.10.

## 2.1.10.16 Where the site's number is asked: at setup and by a command

- **Decision:** both (option C of [2.3.10.4.1](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#231041-where-the-sites-number-is-asked)): setup
  asks the site's number once, at its start step after the domain controller runs and before the upgrade moves 0.7's
  subnets (one short line, question `setup.site_number`, the suggestion its default: the site's order in the

  shows or changes it later (`--site` for a site below). A number is 1-255 and unique in the organisation; changing
  it changes only what fabric suggests from then on, never a subnet already in the plan. A site with a read-only DC
  is not asked: it is numbered from the site above (`fabricctl ipam block <n> --site <site>`). This is a question
  setup asks although the answer can be changed later, an exception to 2.1.2.16 the owner chose.
- **Shapes:** 1.10, 3.3, 4.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner ("both").
- **Why:** the number is chosen at install as 2.1.10.12 says, and a house renumbered later needs no reinstall.
- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-12 on `dev/0.8.0-ipam` ([1.10.4.18](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.10.4-address-manager.md#110418-the-owners-answers-of-2026-10-12-as-built)).
  **Revised 2026-10-23 by 2.1.10.34, 2.1.10.36 and 2.1.10.37:** the number is kept on the site's record; `--site`
  names any site, and changing a site's number needs `plan` there (a grant, or the Directory Administrators); "a site
  above" no longer exists. Setup's question stands.

## 2.1.10.17 A reservation's name may be published in the site's zone too

- **Decision:** yes (option B of [2.3.10.4.2](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#231042-a-reservations-name-in-dns)): a
  reservation with DNS on also gets an A record (and its PTR) in the site's zone, beside the name DHCP registers for
  it in `dhcp.<domain>`. The rules of a published name hold: a name, one DNS label, unique among the site's published
  names and not already in the settings' zone. A reservation inside a DHCP range may carry its A record (Kea gives
  that address only to its MAC).
- **Shapes:** 1.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner ("yes").
- **Why:** a device with a fixed address is reached by the site's own name, as a static one is.
- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-12 on `dev/0.8.0-ipam`. Which PTR the address keeps when Kea registers its
  own is [2.3.10.4.6](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#231046-a-published-reservations-ptr) (open). Revised by 2.1.10.20 on
  2026-10-12: a published reservation's names are only the site zone's (its A and PTR); DHCP registers nothing for
  it, so there is no second name in `dhcp.<domain>`.

## 2.1.10.18 More than one subnet per VLAN

- **Decision:** allowed (option B of [2.3.10.4.3](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#231043-more-than-one-subnet-per-vlan)):
  `fabricctl ipam subnet add <vlan> <cidr>|set <subnet>|remove <subnet>` gives a VLAN further subnets (a secondary
  network, e.g. while renumbering), or a first one to a VLAN kept as a record. Every rule still holds: one claim per
  address, no overlap anywhere in the organisation unless `--allow-overlap` gives a reason, ranges inside their
  subnet. `vlan add` still makes a VLAN with one subnet; `vlan set` changes the subnet of a VLAN that has one, and
  refuses its subnet settings on a VLAN with several (naming them). The suggestion of 2.1.10.9 stays one /24 per VLAN.
- **Shapes:** 1.10, 4.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner ("allow more").
- **Why:** renumbering a VLAN, or a network that outgrew its /24, needs two subnets on one VLAN for a while.
- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-12 on `dev/0.8.0-ipam`.

## 2.1.10.19 The upgrade at a site with a read-only DC waits for the parent's write path

- **Decision:** wait (option C of [2.3.10.4.4](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#231044-the-upgrade-at-a-site-with-a-read-only-dc)):
  at a site whose DC is read-only, the 0.7 subnets stay in its `vars.yaml` and Kea keeps serving them from there, as
  phase 1 built it; each apply says so. They move into the plan when the parent's write path (S8, 1.10.2.8) exists.
- **Shapes:** 1.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner ("wait").
- **Why:** nothing breaks while it waits, and a hand procedure would be thrown away once S8 is built.
- **Status:** Replaced by 2.1.10.43 on 2026-10-23: the move goes through the writable DC that 0.8.0 already writes
  through.

## 2.1.10.20 A DNS record set by hand outranks DHCP's updates

- **Decision:** a DNS record set by hand is authoritative: one in fabric's zones (the settings' `dns:`), or a static,
  infrastructure or reservation address of the plan with DNS on. DHCP's dynamic updates (Kea's DDNS) never overwrite or
  replace it, forward or reverse. Settles [2.3.10.4.6](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#231046-a-published-reservations-ptr)

## 2.1.10.21 A zone change between applies renders BIND from the apply's checked settings

- **Decision:** `fabricctl dns add-zone` and `remove-zone` (and the console's and the menu's zone forms) render BIND's

  vars change is rolled back. Settles [2.3.10.4.10](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#2310410-fabricctl-dns-add-zone-renders-bind-without-dhcps-reverse-zones)

- **Why:** between applies, a zone change otherwise dropped the reverse zones that hold only DHCP's PTRs and Kea's

- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-12 on `dev/0.8.0-ipam`. **Extends 2.1.4.7** (the same decision made for

## 2.1.10.22 A DHCP name left behind when an address's names become hand-set is deleted, at apply and hourly

- **Decision:** once an address is named by a record set by hand (2.1.10.20), fabric deletes the name DHCP registered
  for it in `dhcp.<domain>` (its A or AAAA record for that address, and its DHCID once no address is left at the name)
  with an RFC 2136 update signed with Kea's key, at every apply and on a schedule. The schedule is a timer that runs
  only this cleanup (`fabric-dhcp-names.timer`, hourly), not a full apply (the owner's confirmation): a full apply
  would restart, every time, each service whose configuration changed, and would meet an apply's every refusal
  unattended; the cleanup alone does what the schedule is for. Only names whose address a record set by hand covers
  are deleted; records set by hand are never touched; each removal is audited; BIND refusing fails loudly; a run with
  nothing to delete sends nothing. The interval is fixed in the unit, as for fabric's other timers. Kea's PTR needs no
  update: the apply's regenerated reverse zone already drops it (2.1.10.7). Settles
  [2.3.10.4.9](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#231049-a-dhcp-name-left-behind-when-a-reservations-names-become-hand-set) with its
  option B.
- **Shapes:** 1.10 ([1.10.4.20](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.10.4-address-manager.md#110420-a-dhcp-name-left-behind-is-deleted-at-every-apply-and-hourly)),
  4.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner: "B delete it and apply on a schedule to ensure it clears"; the same day the

- **Why:** what an admin names by hand is what DNS answers (2.1.10.20); a second, stale name for the same address in

## 2.1.10.23 The organisation's and each site's DHCP options are kept in AD, edited by any site the top allows

- **Decision:** the organisation's DHCP options, option definitions and client classes are one entry at the
  organisation's level of the plan in AD (`CN=dhcp-options,OU=organisation` in the top site's OU); each site's are one
  entry under its own OU (`CN=dhcp-options,OU=<site>`), both of a new structural class `fabricOptionSet` with two new
  attributes (`fabricDhcpOptionDef`, `fabricDhcpClass`) beside phase 1's `fabricDhcpOption`; phase 1's objects are not
  changed (an additive schema extension). **Who edits:** a site's own set by that site's admins and by any site above
  it (AD's per-site access, as for the rest of the plan); the organisation's set by any site whose fabric-agent the top
  has put in the group `fabric-dhcp-org-editors` (the top's own agent always may), and there only by a person holding
  the new permission `dhcp:org-options`. The admin bundle carries `dhcp:org-options` at every site, so granting a site
  is one step at the top (`fabricctl ipam option-editors add <site>`); by default only the top site can. Two layers,
  because a house's admins control their own Keycloak but never the top's group: a house cannot grant itself.
  **Writes** go to a writable DC: this site's, or at a site whose DC is read-only the writable DC it replicates from
  (`ad_join_server`, as 2.1.6.52 does for the host's account on 0.7.1); with none known, the edit is refused.
  **Every edit** is audited with the person, this site and the scope. Each site's Kea applies a change at its next
  apply or within 30 s through the plan poll (2.1.10.24). **The upgrade** moves a site's `dhcp.options`,
  `dhcp.option_defs` and `dhcp.client_classes` from `vars.yaml` into the site's set once, idempotent (an option, a
  definition or a class the set already holds keeps the set's and is listed as not carried); it runs where the
  site's subnets already live in the plan (the same point as 1.10.4.15), so a site with a read-only DC keeps them in
  `vars.yaml` with its subnets (2.1.10.19). **`vars.yaml` does not override afterwards:** what is left there before
  the move is served above the site's set (so nothing a running site serves changes), and once moved the settings no
  longer carry them; the 0.7 commands that edited them refuse ("change them with fabricctl ipam option"). This is the
  safer reading: one source the top can see, so the options a house serves are the ones its plan shows.
- **Shapes:** 1.10 ([1.10.4.24](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.10.4-address-manager.md#110424-dhcp-options-in-the-plan-as-built)),
  1.6 (1.6.3: the organisation's OU holds the entry and the group), 1.8 (1.8.2: the permission), 4.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner: "1 A, but can't we make it so they can be edited by any site? ... And then we
  can do permissions handling on this"; the storage, the group, the permission's default and the override reading
  were settled by the build as the safest reading of that answer.
- **Why:** the plan is already in AD and replicates to every house (2.1.10.11); options at the organisation's level
  belong with it. A permission alone would let any house's own admins grant it to themselves; the top's group keeps
  the grant at the top. A local override in `vars.yaml` would make a house serve what the top cannot see.
- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-12 on `dev/0.8.0-options`, tests written, not run (2.3.10.5).
  **Revised 2026-10-23 by 2.1.10.34 and 2.1.10.36:** the organisation's set is the domain's,
  `CN=dhcp-options,CN=fabric,CN=System`, and each site's options are on the site's record; who edits either is a
  grant's `options` right (the domain's at `all`) or the Directory Administrators' tier. `fabric-dhcp-org-editors`, the
  top's convergence's access entry and `dhcp:org-options` are not made. Writes still go to a writable DC; the upgrade

## 2.1.10.24 The 30-second plan poll is a timer that re-renders Kea alone

- **Decision:** 2.1.10.11's watch of the DC's highest USN every 30 s is run by a systemd timer,
  `fabric-plan-watch.timer` (30 s after boot, then every 30 s), starting a oneshot unit that runs
  `fabricctl ipam watch --scheduled`. Each run reads the DC's `highestCommittedUSN` from its root DSE (anonymously,
  inside the DC's container: no secret is loaded while nothing changed); when it moved, it reads the site's plan and
  option sets, and when what Kea is rendered from differs from what it last rendered, it renders Kea's configuration
  from the settings and the plan, has Kea check it (`kea-dhcp4 -t`) and restarts Kea (2.1.10.6), then brings the host
  firewall in line when the served subnets changed. **Never a full apply.** A change that also needs DNS (a name
  published or withdrawn, a reverse zone for a new subnet) is reported (`fabricctl ipam watch` shows it, the run's
  journal says it) and waits for the next apply. It skips a round while an apply holds the settings' lock. Installed
  while Kea is, removed with it, like `fabric-dhcp-names.timer`.
- **Shapes:** 1.10 ([1.10.4.26](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.10.4-address-manager.md#110426-the-30-second-plan-poll)), 3.10, 4.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner through the coordinating agent ("the most robust runner ... never a full apply").
- **Why:** a timer survives a crashed run (the next one starts clean), needs no long-lived process holding the agent's
  password, shows in `systemctl list-timers`, and is the pattern fabric's other schedules use (2.1.10.22). A daemon
  inside fabric-agent would tie DHCP to the console's process; a cron job has no unit status. A full apply every time
  the plan changes would restart every service whose configuration changed and meet an apply's every refusal
  unattended. Reading the USN first keeps the run cheap and keeps OpenBao out of it while nothing changed.
- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-12 on `dev/0.8.0-options`, tests written, not run (2.3.10.5). Revises 1.10.4.8
  (which also re-rendered DNS records and the access lists): DNS waits for the next apply (open point 2.3.10.5.2).
  **Revised 2026-10-13 by 2.1.10.29:** the poll keeps reporting a change DNS needs (2.3.10.5.2 settled with A), and
  Sync now publishes BIND's records on demand.

## 2.1.10.25 Netboot presets are kept as a named option on the subnet or address

- **Decision:** a preset is one entry in the subnet's or address's `fabricDhcpOption` values, a JSON object named
  `netboot` (`{"netboot": {"server", "bios", "uefi", "arm64", "ipxe", "http"}}`) beside its DHCP options; fabric reads
  it apart from the options and writes the two together, so no class changes. A VLAN's preset is set on each of its
  subnets. At an apply (and at the poll) fabric renders it as Kea client classes: for a subnet, classes evaluated only
  for that subnet (Kea 3.0's `only-in-additional-list` classes named in the subnet's `evaluate-additional-classes`);
  for an address, a marker class the reservation carries and classes that test it after the host lookup
  (`member('KNOWN') and member('<marker>')`). The classes: PXE BIOS (option 93 = 0), PXE UEFI x64 (7 or 9), PXE UEFI
  arm64 (11), each with its boot file as the packet's `file` field and option 67, the boot server as `next-server`
  (an address) and option 66; iPXE (user class `iPXE`, option 77) gets the script's URL instead, so it does not loop;
  UEFI HTTP boot (vendor class `HTTPClient`) gets the `http://` URL with option 60 `HTTPClient` sent back. A preset at
  an address outranks its subnet's.
- **Shapes:** 1.10 ([1.10.4.25](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.10.4-address-manager.md#110425-netboot-presets-as-built)), 4.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner through the coordinating agent ("as named options on the subnet/class objects is
  likely simplest"), with the presets of 2.1.10.14 and 1.10.4.6.3.
- **Why:** the preset belongs to the object it boots and replicates with it; one more value in an attribute the
  subnet and address already carry needs no new class on phase 1's objects. Classes evaluated per subnet keep one
  VLAN's boot server from answering another VLAN's clients.
- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-12 on `dev/0.8.0-options`, tests written, not run (2.3.10.5). Kea's documentation
  does not say whether an additional class's `file` and `next-server` fields are applied; fabric sets options 67 and
  66 too, and the `kea` suite's netboot checks are what prove it (2.3.10.5).

## 2.1.10.26 How a scan runs: in Kea's container, by fixed commands, only the site's served subnets, rate-limited, audited

- **Decision:** the on-request scan of 2.1.10.13 runs **inside Kea's own container** (`kea-dhcp4`: host network,
  with the NET_RAW capability it already holds for DHCP), by `docker exec` with **fixed argument lists** (`fping` with
  fixed flags and the checked network, then `cat /proc/net/arp`), never a shell string; Kea's image adds Debian's
  `fping`. No container or service gains a capability; fabric-agent keeps its sandbox. A scan covers **only a subnet
  this site's Kea serves**, named by its name or exact network, at most a /22; anything else is refused. It is
  **rate-limited**: one probe every 10 ms, one retry, 500 ms timeout; one scan at a time on the host; the same
  subnet once a minute. From the console it needs **`dhcp:write`** (listing what may be scanned, `dhcp:read`); from
  the command line, root. Each scan is **audited** (`IPAM_SCAN`); a refused one probes and audits nothing; the
  result is shown, not kept. Ping-check is the hook's global parameters only, on by default, `dhcp.ping_check: false`
  to leave it out.
- **Shapes:** 1.10 ([1.10.4.22](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.10.4-address-manager.md#110422-ping-check-and-the-scan-of-a-subnet-as-built)),
  1.14 (Kea's image), 1.8 (the console's scan page), 1.2 (security: no new capability), 4.10
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the builder of `dev/0.8.0-scan` on the coordinating agent's brief (run it in the Kea
  container, a fixed command, rate-limited, only subnets fabric serves, audited) — the safest reading of 2.1.10.13;
  the choices it leaves to the owner are open points in [2.3.10.6](2.3.10-dhcp-notes.md#23106-ping-check-and-the-scan-built-untested).
- **Why:** ping and ARP need raw sockets. fabric-agent is sandboxed and may not reach the LAN; giving it NET_RAW,
  or a container of its own with it, would add a privilege, while Kea's container already has exactly that one on
  the host network. A fixed command can only ping the network fabric checked; the limits keep a scan from flooding a
  network or being turned on someone else's; `dhcp:write` matches who may act on what it finds.
- **Status:** In force; built 2026-10-12 on `dev/0.8.0-scan`, its tests written and not run (the owner's direction
  of 2026-10-12). **Revised 2026-10-13 by 2.1.10.28:** "only a subnet this site's Kea serves" became every subnet of
  this site's plan, DHCP on or off, and a scan runs only where the scan policy allows it; the rest stands.
  **Revised 2026-10-23 by 2.1.10.38:** who may scan is the `scan` right at the subnet's site (with `dhcp:write` at the
  console); the rest stands.

## 2.1.10.28 A scan may cover every subnet of the site's plan, under a scan policy kept in AD like a group policy

## 2.1.10.30 Scans run top down: a site above triggers a branch's scan through AD, and decides whether its admins may scan

- **Decision:**
  - **A scan always runs at the site that owns the subnet**, on its own Kea, only on its plan's subnets, under
    2.1.10.26's and 2.1.10.28's limits unchanged. No site probes another's network.
  - **A site above a branch may trigger a scan there** ("above": the Networks tab's scope, `visible_sites` /
    `site_scope`, 2.1.10.27): it writes a **scan request** into AD below the subnet's entry in the branch's plan
    (class `fabricScanRequest`: the network, the asking site, the person; when, from AD's own `whenCreated`); the
    branch's 30-second plan poll runs it under every limit, as `<person>@<site>`, and writes the result back on the
    request (state `done`, the result as JSON of at most 48 000 characters, rows beyond it counted as truncated), or
    `refused` with why. The asking console shows it when it arrives; `fabricctl ipam scan <subnet> --site <branch>`
    waits for it. Audited at both ends (`IPAM_SCAN_REQUEST` at the asking site; `IPAM_SCAN` with `requested_by=` at the
    branch, `IPAM_SCAN_REQUEST_REFUSED` for one it refused). At most one pending request per subnet and four per
    branch; one run per poll run; a request still pending after an hour is answered `expired`, not run; every request
    is removed by the branch's poll 24 hours after it was made.
  - **A sibling or a site below can never request.** fabric-agent refuses before AD is asked (`site_scope`); AD
    refuses too: a site's agent writes only below its own OU (`ensure_site_acl`'s inherited full control of its own
    OU, 1.6.3.6), so it has no access in a sibling's or its parent's OU. The branch's own agent and OU admins are
    denied creating a request in their own `OU=networks`, and the branch's poll runs only a request whose owner in AD
    (its creator) is the agent of the site the request names, a site above the branch in AD.
  - **Local scans are set from above.** Whether a branch's own admins may start scans there (the console's Scan now,
    `fabricctl ipam scan` at the branch) is `fabricLocalScan` on the branch's OU (with `fabricLocalScanBy`: the setting
    site, the person, when), writable by the sites above through their inherited full control and denied, by explicit
    access entries on that OU (not inherited), to the branch's own agent and `<site>-ou-admins`, who hold no right to
    change permissions. **Default: off** (the central-IT model: local admins do little by default); a scan triggered
    from above runs whatever it says; **the top site's admins may always scan the top's subnets.** Set with
    `dhcp:write` from a site above (the Networks tab's card, `fabricctl ipam local-scan allow|deny --site <s>`),
    audited `IPAM_LOCAL_SCAN_SET`; a scan whose setting cannot be read (the DC not answering, no kept copy) is
    refused.
  - **Schema** (additive): attributes 39 `fabricLocalScan`, 40 `fabricLocalScanBy`, 41 `fabricScanRequestedBy`,
    42 `fabricScanActor`, 43 `fabricScanState`, 44 `fabricScanResult`, 45 `fabricScanDoneAt`; classes 15
    `fabricLocalScanSet` (auxiliary, on a site's OU) and 16 `fabricScanRequest` (structural, below a subnet's

  - **2.1.10.28's scan policy is removed:** its organisation and site levels, the enforced switch, the routes

- **Shapes:** 1.10 ([1.10.4.29](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.10.4-address-manager.md#110429-scans-from-the-top-down)),
