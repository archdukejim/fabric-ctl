## 2.1.18.1 The guides apply to every part, as far as they apply, as securely as fabric can

- **Decision:** DISA's Application Security and Development STIG, the API SRG, the Application Server SRG and the DNS
  SRG (with the BIND 9.x STIG for BIND) are applied to every part of fabric they fit, fabric's CA included (step-ca and
  OpenBao, held to the general guides since DISA has no CA guide), "as secure as possible". Which part answers to which
  is 1.18.1.4; each part's answers are the checklist (1.18.2).
- **Shapes:** 1.18 (all of it), every part's chapter as features reach it (1.1.6.5).
- **Made:** 2026-10-22, by the owner: "I want to apply the AS&D , API, CA, DNS and Application Server STIGs as
  applicable to all the items we have. and try to make them as secure as possible"; "CA" is fabric's own certificate
  authority (step-ca and OpenBao), covered by DISA's general guides (the owner confirmed).
- **Why:** 2.1.1.67.
- **Status:** In force from 2026-10-22. The guides beyond these five are 2.1.18.4.

## 2.1.18.2 Requirements of the DoD as an organisation are exempt

- **Decision:** a requirement, or the part of one, that applies only to the DoD (DoW) as an organisation is answered
  **exempt-dod**: its texts (the DoD banner), its PKI, its identity cards and enterprise identity (CAC, PIV, FICAM,
  ICAM), its roles and processes (ISSO, ISSM, AO, PPSM, IAVM), its figures (DoW-defined event lists, intervals), its
  networks and services, and classification marking (the seven kinds of 1.18.1.5.1). The technical requirement under a
  DoD wrapper is not exempt (1.18.1.5.2).
- **Shapes:** 1.18.1.5, the checklist's `exempt-dod` rows, 1.18.3.3.2 and 1.18.3.4.3 (what fabric takes out of members'
  baselines).
- **Made:** 2026-10-22, by the owner: "with the exception that we are NOT a dod / dow organization any specific
  organizational ideas should be strictly exempt".
- **Why:** fabric's users are not the DoD; DoD texts, roots and processes would be wrong or have no one to carry them.
- **Status:** In force from 2026-10-22. How strictly a mixed requirement is read is 2.1.18.5.

## 2.1.18.3 Members' STIGs are applied by Group Policy, opt-in

- **Decision:** fabric makes it easy to apply the domain members' own OS STIGs, Windows and Linux, as Group Policy: an
  admin picks a STIG for a set of machines and fabric makes the GPO (1.18.3). Opt-in: nothing is linked on a fresh
  install. It fits 0.8.0's grants and machine sets without changing them (1.18.3.2.3).
- **Shapes:** 1.18.3, 1.6.13 (beside the grants, unchanged), 1.6.11.6 (`fabric-managed` on Linux members).
- **Made:** 2026-10-22, by the owner: "we should make it easy to apply them as a part of a gpo for Windows or Linux
  systems using their stigs via Group Policy". Opt-in and the fit to the grants model are the design's (the caller's
  brief), for the owner to review with 1.18.3.
- **Why:** members are where most of a domain's STIG work lies; Group Policy is how a domain already sets machines.
- **Status:** In force from 2026-10-22 as the owner's request. The design's open points: 2.1.18.11 to 2.1.18.14 and
  2.1.18.18.

## 2.1.18.4 Further guides beyond the five (Open)

- **Question:** some parts have DISA guides of their own outside the five (1.18.1.3.3): the AD Domain and Forest STIGs
  (Samba DC), the Database SRG and the Crunchy Data PostgreSQL STIG (Postgres), the Web Server SRG and the F5 NGINX STIG
  (nginx), the AAA Services SRG (FreeRADIUS, Keycloak), the Container Platform SRG (Docker, the images), the Central Log
  Server SRG (Fluent Bit's side). Do they join the checklist?
- **Options:** (A) yes, all of them, as a second pass in 0.11.0; (B) only the AD STIGs and the Web Server SRG, the parts
  most exposed; (C) no, the five only.
- **Recommendation:** (A): the owner asked for "as secure as possible", and Postgres, nginx and FreeRADIUS otherwise
  answer to no guide at all.
- **Shapes:** 1.18.1.3.3, 1.18.1.4, the checklist.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.5 How strictly the exemption is read, and who stands in for the ISSO (Open)

- **Question:** many rules mix a DoD part with a technical one ("notify the ISSO when auditing fails", "the ISSM selects
  the audited events", "back up at DoW-required intervals"). Is the whole rule exempt, or only its DoD part? And if
  only the DoD part, who stands in for the ISSO, ISSM and SA, and what accepts an exception such as fabric-agent
  running as root (ASD V-222430) where DoD would ask the AO?
- **Options:** (A) only the DoD part is exempt; the Directory Administrators and the Enterprise Admin stand in for the
  ISSO and SA (told in the console's top bar, Overview and `fabricctl doctor`, and by mail if one is set), and an
  exception is a recorded decision in this register; (B) the whole rule is exempt whenever any part of it is DoD's.
- **Recommendation:** (A): "strictly exempt" then covers every organisational idea, and the security property under it
  stays, as the owner's "as secure as possible" asks.
- **Shapes:** 1.18.1.5.2, the checklist's ASD-03, ASD-04b, ASD-09, AS-03 rows.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.6 STIG values by default, or a STIG profile (Open)

- **Question:** the STIGs' values are stricter than several recorded decisions: passwords of 15 characters (2.1.6.35
  says 12), lockout after 3 failures (10), MFA for administrators (2.1.1.34: a password first), a 10-minute idle
  sign-out for admins (the console's 15). Changing those decisions is a design modification (5.5). How do installs get
  the STIG's values?
- **Options:** (A) a setting `security_profile: standard | stig`, chosen in setup and the wizard, `standard` by default
  (today's values), `stig` setting every STIG value at once; the console shows which checklist rows `standard` leaves
  unmet; (B) the STIG's values become the defaults for new installs (revising 2.1.6.35 and retiring the deviation
  2.1.1.34), each lowerable as a relaxation shown in status (2.1.2.9); (C) no profile: each value is set on its own.
- **Recommendation:** (A), with `stig` the recommended answer in setup's question: it keeps the owner's simple first
  standup (2.1.1.34's reason) and makes the whole STIG one choice; (B) if the owner now prefers secure by default
  outright.
- **Shapes:** 1.18.2.7, 1.6.12.10 (password policies), 1.8 (sessions), 1.3.2 (setup), 2.1.6.35, 2.1.1.34.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.7 MFA for privileged accounts (Open)

- **Question:** the ASD STIG's multifactor rules (among V-222522 to V-222531; their CAC and Alt. Token wording is exempt) and the Application Server SRG's V-204746 and V-204747 (CAT I) ask for MFA on privileged
  accounts. fabric starts at a password (2.1.1.34). Is MFA required for the console's administrators (the Directory
  Administrators, delegated admins, `fabric-admin` in the console)?
- **Options:** (A) required under the `stig` profile (2.1.18.6) for every account holding an admin permission: a passkey,
  TOTP or per-user certificate, enrolled at the first sign-in; (B) required always, for admins, revising 2.1.1.34 for
  them; (C) not required, the checklist row stays to-build.
- **Recommendation:** (A); with (B) if 2.1.18.6 goes to (B). `fabric-admin` on the host's console and `local-admin`
  stay password break glass (2.1.6.69), recorded as the exception.
- **Shapes:** 1.6.2, 1.8.2, 2.1.1.34, the checklist's ASD-10 and AS-08 rows.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.8 A notice of the organisation's own (Open)

- **Question:** the DoD banner's text is exempt (2.1.18.2). Does fabric offer an organisation's own notice in its place,
  shown before sign-in and kept until acknowledged, on the console and Keycloak's sign-in page, and given to members'
  baselines (Windows' legal notice, Linux `/etc/issue` and the SSH banner)?
- **Options:** (A) yes: one setting `login_notice` (title and text, empty by default; empty means none anywhere); (B)
  no banner at all.
- **Recommendation:** (A): it is the security purpose of the rule, organisations often have their own, and empty by
  default changes nothing for the rest.
- **Shapes:** 1.8 (sign-in), 1.6.2 (Keycloak's theme), 1.18.3.3.2 b, 1.18.3.4.3.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.9 FIPS 140-validated cryptography (Open)

- **Question:** many rules ask for FIPS 140-2 or 140-3 validated modules (ASD V-222570, V-222571; AS V-204758, CAT I;
  API V-274603, V-274606, V-274783; BIND V-272436). Validation is a federal requirement, not a DoD one. fabric's images
  are Debian-based and not validated; on Ubuntu, validated OpenSSL and kernel come only with Ubuntu Pro's FIPS packages.
- **Options:** (A) approved algorithms only (TLS 1.2 and 1.3 with AEAD ciphers, SHA-2, RSA 3072 or P-256 and up),
  without validated builds: the rows stay `to-build` with "approved, not validated" in their evidence; (B) also a FIPS
  option for the host on Ubuntu Pro (the owner's subscription), images unchanged; (C) FIPS-validated images too (UBI or
  Chainguard FIPS bases), a large change to 1.14; (D) add FIPS validation to the exemption as a federal requirement.
- **Recommendation:** (A) now, (B) as an option later: (C) would rebuild every image on a paid base, against 2.1.1.5.
- **Shapes:** 1.2.6, 1.14, the host (1.18.3.5), every row marked 2.1.18.9.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.10 Password lifetime, unused and temporary accounts (Open)

- **Question:** the STIGs ask for a 180-day maximum password age (local accounts on Windows: 60), unused accounts and
  machines disabled after 35 days, and temporary accounts that expire within 72 hours. NIST SP 800-63B advises against
  forced rotation; 2.1.6.35 has passwords never expire.
- **Options:** (A) under the `stig` profile: 180 days, the 35-day disable (a daily check, the admins told, an exception
  list for service accounts and break glass), and temporary accounts as an account option; (B) the 35-day disable and
  temporary accounts only, no forced rotation, the rotation row recorded as a deliberate deviation following NIST; (C)
  none.
- **Recommendation:** (B): rotation adds little against fabric's lockout and MFA, and NIST is the better guide for
  non-DoD organisations; the unused-account disable closes a real gap.
- **Shapes:** 1.6.12 (people, machines), 2.1.6.35, the checklist's ASD-03, ASD-12, ASD-13, AS-16 rows.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.11 Where DISA's GPO package comes from (Open)

- **Question:** fabric needs DISA's GPO package to make Windows baselines (1.18.3.3.1). Does fabric download it, or does
  the admin?
- **Options:** (A) the admin downloads it from DISA and uploads it; fabric shows its SHA-256 to compare with DISA's or
  NIST's listing; (B) fabric downloads the current quarter's package from `dl.dod.cyber.mil` on request, with (A) for
  offline installs; (C) fabric publishes a stripped copy in its own repository.
- **Recommendation:** (A): no download path to maintain (1.1.6.2's offline rule holds by itself), the package's
  Microsoft templates are not redistributed (1.6.5.9's rule), and the admin chooses the quarter; (B) can follow.
- **Shapes:** 1.18.3.3.1.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.12 The STIG's deny rights on local accounts and `local-admin` (Open)

- **Question:** the Windows STIGs deny "Local account" network sign-in and Remote Desktop on domain members
  (`SeDenyNetworkLogonRight`, `SeDenyRemoteInteractiveLogonRight`). That also shuts out the machine's `local-admin`,
  which 1.6.13.4 keeps as a way in on every machine.
- **Options:** (A) keep the STIG's deny: `local-admin` is used at the keyboard or the hypervisor's console only; the
  baseline's page says so; (B) override it in every baseline: `local-admin` keeps remote sign-in, the checklist row
  recorded as the exception; (C) the admin chooses per baseline, (A) by default.
- **Recommendation:** (C) with (A) as the default: the STIG's reason (local credentials reused across machines) is
  answered by OpenBao's per-machine passwords, but remote use of a local admin is still the lateral path the rule
  closes.
- **Shapes:** 1.18.3.3.2 e, 1.6.13.4, 2.1.7.6.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.13 How a Linux member applies its baseline (Open)

- **Question:** what applies a Linux baseline's settings on the member (1.18.3.4)?
- **Options:** (A) fabric's own step in `fabric-managed`, applying the OS STIG's profile with OpenSCAP and ComplianceAsCode
  content pinned in fabric's apt repository, settings carried in the GPO as a tailoring file; (B) Samba's
  `samba-gpupdate` with its extensions, the rest delivered as cron scripts (needs `adcli --add-samba-data` at the join
  and a timer in place of `oddjob-gpupdate`, which Debian and Ubuntu lack); (C) Ubuntu Pro's USG (`usg fix disa_stig`)
  driven by `fabric-managed` on members with Pro attached.
- **Recommendation:** (A): nothing new on members beyond OpenSCAP, the whole profile rather than a few settings, a scan
  result to prove it, and the same machine sign-in `fabric-managed` already uses. (C) as an option where Pro is
  attached.
- **Shapes:** 1.18.3.4.2, 1.6.11.6, 1.1.7 (ComplianceAsCode's licence), 2.1.1.2 (the apt repository).
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.14 Members with no DISA STIG, and the RHEL family (Open)

- **Question:** DISA has no STIG for Ubuntu 26.04 yet, none for Debian 12 or 13 or Proxmox VE, and fabric's Linux join
  does not support RHEL-family members (1.6.7). What do their machines get?
- **Options:** (A) Ubuntu 26.04: the 24.04 profile marked "preview", its failures shown, until DISA publishes;
  Debian and Proxmox: none; RHEL 9 and 10: when the join supports them; (B) nothing until DISA publishes; (C) a GPOS
  SRG-derived profile for every system.
- **Recommendation:** (A): 26.04 is the members' newest Ubuntu (2.1.1.63) and is close to 24.04; hardening a Proxmox
  host by a profile written for something else risks its VMs.
- **Shapes:** 1.18.1.3.4, 1.18.3.4.4, 1.6.7.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.15 The fabric host's own OS STIG (Open)

- **Question:** the fabric host is Ubuntu and is reached by no GPO (1.18.3.5). Does fabric harden it to the Ubuntu STIG?
- **Options:** (A) a setup step `stig`, opt-in (or on under the `stig` profile), applying the same engine as members
  with a tailoring that keeps fabric working (its firewall instead of ufw's defaults, Docker, the DC's host network,
  the audit rules' cost on a 4 GB Pi), consented to as host changes (1.2.9) and undone by uninstall (Rule 12); (B) the
  checklist only, the host's hardening left to the owner; (C) on by default.
- **Recommendation:** (A): the host holds the CA's keys and the domain's secrets, so it matters most; opt-in because the
  profile changes much of the system, and Rule 12 asks for consent.
- **Shapes:** 1.3.2 (setup), 1.2.9 (consent), 1.2.6, the checklist's `host` rows.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.16 How "met" is proven on real machines: scans and a lab campaign (Open)

- **Question:** the suites prove fabric's own rows (1.18.2.5); members' baselines need real machines and a scan.
- **Options:** (A) a `stig` campaign on gaming-23's Hyper-V lab (3.1.5): Windows Server 2025, Windows 11 and Ubuntu 24.04
  and 26.04 members, each baseline applied, then scanned (OpenSCAP with DISA's or ComplianceAsCode's content on Linux;
  DISA's SCC on Windows, if its licence lets it be used there), results recorded in 2.3.18; (B) Linux only (OpenSCAP),
  Windows checked by `gpresult` and spot checks.
- **Recommendation:** (A), with (B)'s fallback on Windows if SCC cannot be used.
- **Shapes:** 1.18.2.5, 1.18.3.7, 3.1.5, 1.1.4.6 (0.11.0's "tested on").
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.17 The per-rule pass of the checklist (Open)

- **Question:** the first pass is by requirement group (1.18.2.4). When is every rule answered on its own row?
- **Options:** (A) before 0.11.0 ships, generated from DISA's XCCDF files (a script that reads each guide's XCCDF from a
  download kept outside the repository and adds a row per rule, keeping the groups' answers as the start); (B) only as
  features reach each rule (1.1.6.5); (C) never: groups are enough.
- **Recommendation:** (A): 0.11.0 is the STIG release, and the rules the mirrors' truncated pages hid (1.18.2.4) are
  only seen that way.
- **Shapes:** 1.18.2, `scripts/docs/` (the generator).
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.18 Who manages STIG baselines (Open)

- **Question:** who may load packages and add, change or remove baselines (1.18.3.6)?
- **Options:** (A) the Directory Administrators, with a new permission `stig:admin` in their bundle, as `groups:admin`
  for grants; (B) the Enterprise Admin only (`fabric-admin`); (C) (A) for baselines, (B) for loading packages.
- **Recommendation:** (A): a baseline links at `OU=machines`, where the Directory Administrators already link GPOs
  (1.6.13.10.4), and it cannot touch the grants' rights (1.18.3.3.2 a).
- **Shapes:** 1.6.12.11, 1.8.2, 1.18.3.6.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.19 Log offload under the STIG (Open)

- **Question:** the Application Server SRG's V-204833 (CAT I) and the OS STIGs ask for logs sent off the system; Fluent
  Bit forwarding is optional and off by default (1.15). Members' `audisp-remote` needs a target too.
- **Options:** (A) under the `stig` profile, setup asks for a forwarding target and the console warns until one is set;
  members' baselines send to it; (B) the rows answered "met when configured", nothing asked; (C) fabric runs a log
  store of its own.
- **Recommendation:** (A): a log kept only on the machine it describes is lost with it.
- **Shapes:** 1.15, 1.18.3.4.3, the checklist's ASD-09, AS-02 rows.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.20 DNSSEC signing of fabric's own zones (Open)

- **Question:** the BIND STIG and the DNS SRG ask for signed zones with the KSK kept offline (V-272372, V-272373,
  V-272384 to V-272391, V-205176 to V-205178). fabric's zones are unsigned; the resolver forwards them without
  validation (2.1.12.7). The AD zones take dynamic updates, so they would be signed inline with the ZSK on the server.
- **Options:** (A) sign fabric's zones and the AD zones with BIND's `dnssec-policy` (inline signing, NSEC3, RRSIG
  validity within 2 to 7 days), the KSK's private key in OpenBao and given to BIND only for a key rollover, the
  resolver validating them with fabric's trust anchor; (B) sign only the zones without dynamic updates; (C) leave them
  unsigned and record why (internal zones, TSIG-protected transfers and updates).
- **Recommendation:** (A), built with the DNS work it touches: it closes the BIND STIG's largest group, and the resolver
  already validates everything else.
- **Shapes:** 1.4, 1.12.2, 2.1.12.7, 1.7 (the KSK's key).
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).

## 2.1.18.21 nginx as the API gateway: rate limits and a WAF (Open)

- **Question:** the API SRG asks for one gateway, throttling, per-client rate limits and a web application firewall
  (V-274612, V-274682, V-274697, V-274707, V-274769).
- **Options:** (A) nginx is the gateway (every API already passes it, the agent's is a unix socket): `limit_req` per
  client on sign-in, the console's POSTs and the federation endpoint; no WAF, recorded as not applicable to an
  appliance with no public API; (B) (A) plus ModSecurity with the OWASP Core Rule Set in the nginx image; (C) nothing.
- **Recommendation:** (A): rate limits cost little; a WAF in front of fabric's own small, validated routes adds a large
  dependency for little gain.
- **Shapes:** 1.2.4 (nginx), 1.9.4.2, the checklist's API-06 and API-11 rows.
- **Status:** Open (2026-10-22).
