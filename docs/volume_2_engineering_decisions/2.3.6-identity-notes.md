## 2.3.6.1 Proposal: Samba AD replaces 389-DS as fabric's directory

### 2.3.6.1.1 Status and what the owner decided

2.3.6.1.4 are rewritten (2026-10-04: 1.6.3, 1.9.8, 1.6.5, 1.6.7, 1.2.7 and new topics in 1.2.1, 1.4.1, 1.3.3,
1.10.2; decisions 2.1.6.15, 2.1.6.16, 2.1.9.11, 2.1.9.12, 2.1.6.17, 2.1.6.18, 2.1.6.19, 2.1.13.1, 2.1.1.31 in 2.1.6) and were **approved on 2026-10-04** (2.3.6.1.11); the build starts at S1. (Earlier the same day: direction approved, gated by
the S0 spike, 2.3.6.1.5.) Nothing is rebuilt
until the spike has answered every question in 2.3.6.1.5 and the owner has approved the rewritten designs (global
Rules 4 and 5.5). It changes the Samba design (1.6.4), which kept 389-DS as the source of truth beside an
optional Samba.

The owner's answers to the Samba decisions (2.1.6), 2026-10-04:

- **2.1.6.6, where passwords and people live:** "If we can replace 389-DS with Samba I think we should and simplify
  the container stack." Samba AD becomes fabric's one directory; 389-DS goes.
- **2.1.6.7, site controllers:** the owner asked for "federated levels": a site's admins change only their site,
  while the configuration replicates everywhere. AD's answer within one domain: an organisational unit (OU) per
  site with rights delegated to that site's admins; schema, configuration and Group Policy replicate to every
  controller; read-only controllers (RODCs) where the spike proves them. A forest of several domains (true
  per-site domains) is, as far as is known, not supported by Samba; the spike confirms it.
- **2.1.6.8, the controller's address:** the host's address. Ports 389/636 are free once 389-DS is gone; BIND
  keeps 53 and serves the AD zone through Samba's DLZ plugin.
- **2.1.6.9, Linux joins:** follows S1 (there is only AD to join); the owner confirms it with the rewritten
  design.
- 2.1.6.10 and 2.1.5.3: not yet answered.

### 2.3.6.1.2 What changes

Today 389-DS is fabric's directory: people, groups, POSIX ids, devices and their roles, the address plan, split
into an organisation part and site parts and replicated between sites (M1, M1b, M5). Samba AD would take all of
that over and add what only AD gives: Windows logons and Group Policy, Kerberos single sign-on for Linux,
PEAP-MSCHAPv2 for 802.1X, one password store. The stack loses a container (`dirsrv`) and a replication system
(fabric's own agreements and `fabric-directory-sync`), and the hardest part of the old Samba design — keeping
passwords equal in two directories — disappears.

### 2.3.6.1.3 What replaces what

| Today (389-DS) | With Samba AD |
|---|---|
| `dirsrv` container, `ldap_base_dn`, organisation suffix + `ou=<site>` sub-suffixes (M1, M1b) | `samba` container (a DC), realm `AD.<DOMAIN>`; the organisation's objects in the domain, one OU per site |
| M5 replication: agreements, read-only organisation at sites, `fabric-directory-sync.timer` | AD replication between the site DCs; AD sites built from the address plan; RODCs at sites if proven |
| fabric's schema (`05-schema.ldif`: 15 attributes and classes for devices, device roles, networks, sites) | The same attributes added to AD's schema (permanent once added) or kept as fabric data elsewhere — decided by the spike |
| ACIs and fabric's service accounts (`30-aci.ldif`, `20-accounts.ldif`) | AD ACLs on OUs (`samba-tool dsacl`), delegated per site; service accounts as AD users |
| Keycloak federates to 389-DS (writable) | Keycloak federates to AD (its LDAP provider in AD mode; passwords checked by AD) |
| FreeRADIUS looks devices and people up in 389-DS; EAP-TTLS checks passwords by LDAP bind | FreeRADIUS looks up in AD; PEAP-MSCHAPv2 through `ntlm_auth` (winbind); EAP-TLS unchanged |
| Linux clients: SSSD against 389-DS (M10 steps 1–3) | Linux clients join AD (`realm join`, Kerberos); sudo rules in AD (the sudo schema) |
| POSIX ids from fabric (`ensure_posix_identities`) | RFC 2307 attributes in AD (`--use-rfc2307`), assigned by fabric as today |
| The web UI's directory pages and fabric-agent through `fabriclib/ldap` | The same pages and routes through a directory layer that speaks to AD |
| Address plan stored in the directory (M8) | In AD (schema) or in fabric's own data, replicated with it — decided by the spike |
| BIND only; AD DNS behind it (old design, macvlan) | BIND serves fabric's zones and, through DLZ, the AD zone; Windows registers its names by signed dynamic updates |

### 2.3.6.1.4 Impact assessment

**Architecture.** The directory moves from 389-DS to Samba AD everywhere. 1.2 (topology), 1.3.3 (agent API), 1.9
(federation: M1, M1b, M5), 1.10.2.6 (address plan), 1.6.6 (Linux join), 1.6.4 (Samba) and the container
inventory (1.2.6, 2.1.2.8) are rewritten. Keycloak, FreeRADIUS, BIND, Step-CA and OpenBao stay; their links to the
directory change. The core-services rule (1.2.8: DNS, DHCP, LDAP, SSO and nginx never depend on OpenBao to start)
applies to the DC as it did to 389-DS.

**Breaking changes.**

- Every existing install migrates once: people, groups, POSIX ids, devices, device roles and the address plan
  move from 389-DS to AD; **passwords cannot move** (389-DS keeps only one-way hashes), so every person sets a new
  password at the first sign-in after the upgrade (Keycloak's required action). Installs are few (the owner's
  two houses and test machines), so a guided migration plus that one reset is proposed.
- Federation: sites join the AD domain instead of a 389-DS replica; the invitation carries what a DC needs to join.
  Existing sites re-join.
- The fabric-agent routes for people, devices and roles keep their shape; their backend changes.
- The memory budget at 4 GB changes (a DC instead of 389-DS; measured in the spike).
- The host publishes the DC's ports (88, 135, 389, 445, 464, 636, 3268/3269, the RPC range): a host change that
  goes through consent (1.2.9).

**Scope creep risk: high.** It touches most of fabric. Contained by: the spike first (2.3.6.1.5) — if a question
fails, the replacement stops and the old design (Samba beside 389-DS) is the fallback; then one rewritten design
approved before any code; then the build in steps, each tested, with 389-DS kept running until the AD side passes
the same suites (dirsrv, keycloak, freeradius, federation, sandbox).

### 2.3.6.1.9 Outcome of the spike

**Every question passes.** Samba AD can replace 389-DS as fabric's directory, built from Debian's packages on
fabric's base, hardened like the other containers. What the rewritten design must carry (each from a result above):

- The DC runs with CHOWN, DAC_OVERRIDE, FOWNER, SETUID and SETGID — no others (SETGID and SETUID alone serve
  sign-ins, but Group Policy writes ACLs on SYSVOL; Q16),
  read-only, its full host name, its sockets on `/run`; provisioning output discarded (it prints a temporary
  password); the administrator's password set from a file.
- BIND gains Samba's DLZ module (`samba-libs`, `samba-dsdb-modules`), the DC's `smb.conf` read-only, the DC's
  `bind-dns` folder and a writable `/var/tmp`; its limit goes to 128 MiB.
- fabric's schema moves to an AD-valid OID arc derived from its UUID, loaded in two sessions; structural classes
  carry a default security descriptor.
- Sites are OUs in one domain with delegated rights (Samba has no child domains); RODCs work (Kerberos forwarded,
  cached sign-ins with the root DC down, writes refused); NTLM forwarding at an RODC is tested again in S1.
- Keycloak federates to AD (writable, LDAPS); fabric decides the old-password window (60 minutes by default).
- FreeRADIUS uses the DC's winbind for PEAP (`ntlm auth = mschapv2-and-ntlmv2-only` in `smb.conf`, the
  privileged folder in its group); EAP-TLS unchanged.
- Linux machines join with adcli into their site's OU; SSSD gives fabric's ids, access by group and sudo from AD
  (sudo's schema with its own OIDs).
- Migration moves everything but passwords and 389-DS's service accounts; people set a new password once.

The owner now approves or rejects the replacement (2.3.6.1.6); on approval the designs of 2.3.6.1.4 are rewritten.

### 2.3.6.1.10 Owner decisions after the first runs, and Q12–Q16

On 2026-10-04, after Q1–Q11, the owner answered S5 and S6, revised S2 and added 2.1.6.11, 2.1.6.12, 2.1.6.13, 2.1.6.14 (register: 2.1.6): the AD
domain is chosen at setup (`ad.<domain>` recommended for federation, fabric's own domain acceptable for one
location, permanent); everything fabric manages lives under `OU=sites`; Group Policy pushes only the trusts fabric
needs, the rest through an ADMX-driven editor; the DC's certificates come from Step-CA; the password policy is the
admin's, with no defaults; each site chooses and can change its DC type, site DCs are independent and resync, each
writes only its own site by default; access is authorized per site. The owner asked for Q12–Q16 (2.3.6.1.5) before
these go into the rewritten design; the replacement itself still waits for approval on the results.

| # | Result | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| Q12 | **Pass** | A writable site DC joins and is cut off from the root while its site's LAN still reaches it. While cut off: the site admin adds a person to its own site at the site DC, and is still refused in another site (ACLs are checked by every DC, offline too); the root adds a person elsewhere. After reconnecting, both DCs have both sides' changes; the same attribute changed on both sides converged to the later write on both; the same new name created on both sides kept both objects, one renamed (`CNF:`), nothing lost. A site DC must register its names in AD's DNS (replication finds partners by them) — the real build does it at join. **Finding for "each DC writes only its own site":** AD limits *who* writes, not *which DC*: a site's admins are limited to their OU on every DC, but a domain admin signed in at a site DC can still write anywhere. ### 2.3.6.1.12 Findings while building S1

  (WSL's Windows drive hid it); the Dockerfile sets it.
- **A joined machine's local Administrators** must stay in the site's log-on policy, which replaces Windows'
  local lists.
- **ACL entries must be compared parsed**: Samba rewrites SDDL text, so a text comparison would add an entry again on

### 2.3.6.1.16 S3: FreeRADIUS on AD

Findings while moving FreeRADIUS to AD (S3.2, 2026-10-05), all covered by the `freeradius` suite against a real DC:

- **AD has its own groups with common names** (`Guests`, `Users`, `Administrators`, in `CN=Builtin`), and group
  names are unique across the domain: a fabric group called `guests` cannot be made, and a `radius_people` mapping
  of `guests` would have matched AD's own group. The person check therefore only takes groups under `OU=sites`
  (fabric's), plus Domain Users (`users`) from the primary group, which `memberOf` never lists.
- **AD answers every refused bind alike** (`invalidCredentials`, with the reason only in a diagnostic code): a wrong
  password, a locked, disabled or expired account and a one-time password not yet changed are all "wrong password or
  account locked" in the auth log. A person on their one-time password sets their own at the web sign-in first.
- **Roles are read only from the site's own `OU=device-roles` and the organisation's**, by FreeRADIUS and by the
  web UI alike: another site's role of the same name never grants anything here.
- FreeRADIUS no longer needs `install_ldap`: the DC is part of every install.

**2.1.11.3 (owner, 2026-10-05): PEAP machine sign-in uses the group mappings.** Asked before S3.3 because the design
named PEAP for machines without saying which machines get in. Options put to the owner: the `radius_people`
mappings for machines too (chosen); every enabled machine of the site on the port's default VLAN (no control, no
off switch); or a fabric device per machine whose roles grant a new permission (most control, but a device record
for every joined machine and a new permission in the vocabulary). The mappings reuse one mechanism and one screen,
give machines a VLAN, and admit no machine until a group is mapped.

S3.3 (PEAP, 2026-10-05), built as the spike found (Q8): FreeRADIUS asks the DC through its own winbind, with no
member join. Findings:

- The DC's winbind sockets go through a host folder (`<deploy_base>/samba/winbindd`, mounted over the DC's `/run`
  tmpfs), and its privileged pipe lives in its data folder; converge puts that folder in FreeRADIUS's group (root,
  0750) after every start. FreeRADIUS mounts the DC's `etc/` folder rather than `smb.conf` alone: the DC's entrypoint
  rewrites the file (a new inode), which a single-file mount would not follow.
- `%{mschap:User-Name}` already turns a machine's `host/ws1.<domain>` into `ws1$` (and `DOMAIN\alice` into
  `alice`): the spike's own rewrite is not needed.
- The policy decides PEAP in the inner tunnel's post-auth, once the domain has accepted the answer: the account
  must then be in a mapped group (2.1.11.3); a machine also in the site's `OU=machines`.
- FreeRADIUS's `%{%{…}:-…}` expansions are Jinja tags to the renderer: `mods/mschap` is rendered raw.

### 2.3.6.1.20 S8: sites joining the root's domain

Built 2026-10-05 (1.6.5.22 S8.1–S8.3); `tests/samba/site_join.py` joins a writable and a read-only site DC to a root DC
with BIND serving the AD zone. Findings:

- **A joining DC resolves through the root.** Its own host's BIND has no AD zone before it joins, so its resolver

- **Replication back to the root needs a site link.** A new AD site in no site link left the root with an inbound

### 2.3.6.1.21 The owner's S8 answers

2026-10-05, on the questions S8.1–S8.5 left open:

- **Overrides and grants (2.1.9.14).** The owner prefers a database to files, for fabric's scale later, and Postgres.
  The owner then asked for it to be at every site and kept in sync. Postgres's own logical replication with one
  writer per row was chosen over pulling copies through the endpoint (slower, and code of our own) and over

### 2.3.6.1.23 S8.8: sites nest in the directory

The first part of S8.8 (2.1.6.20, 1.9.8.14), built 2026-10-05: a site's OU is made in its parent's and marked as a site OU
(`fabricSiteInfo`, the class that also carries its id block), and found by that mark and its name everywhere
(`paths.site_dn`), never built from `OU=sites`. Tested in `tests/samba/dc.py` (a parent's agent writes in the site
below it; a nested site's agent and admin are refused in the parent, on the nested site's service accounts, and
changing their OU's permissions, against a control where the domain's Administrator makes the same change),
`site_join.py` (both site DCs nested under the root, joined and replicating), `linux_join.py` (the installer finds
its site's OU with Kerberos and LDAP), and the federation, FreeRADIUS and Keycloak suites. Findings:

- **Rights to change permissions were removed** from the site's admins and agent (`WD`, `WO`): with them a nested
  site could protect its OU and shut its parents out. Nothing fabric does needs them as a person or the agent;
  convergence sets ACLs as the system.
- **A parent's principals are denied on a nested site's service accounts** explicitly (an explicit deny is
  evaluated before an inherited allow).
- **Which site is the root** is the one whose OU holds `OU=organisation`, as for S8.4.
- Hosts keep the site's place as `ad_site_ou` and `ad_org_ou` (from the join); this also fixed Keycloak's group
  mapper at a site that is not the root, which looked for the organisation's groups in the site's own OU.
- The Linux installer reads its site's place from the DC before it joins (`kinit` as the joining account, then a

### 2.3.6.1.25 S8.6 spike: Postgres between sites through nginx

`spikes/postgres-nginx/run.sh` (2026-10-05), Postgres 18.6 and nginx 1.30.5 from the lock, a test CA, two Postgres
servers (A publishes, B subscribes) and nginx in front of A, nothing published on a host:

- **Works:** Postgres 17+'s direct TLS (`sslnegotiation=direct`, ALPN `postgresql`) is plain TLS to nginx's stream
  module (`ssl_alpn postgresql`), which requires a client certificate of the organisation's CA and maps its subject
  to where it may go; logical replication with a row filter (`WHERE owner <> 'lab'`) runs through it, a new row
  arriving within seconds; the replication password stays in a passfile, never in `pg_subscription`.
- **Refused before Postgres sees anything:** no client certificate, another CA's certificate, a valid certificate
  of a site not linked (sent to a closed port), and Postgres's own negotiation (its SSLRequest is not TLS).
- **TLS on the inner hop is not possible with nginx:** its stream proxy has no `proxy_ssl_alpn` (nginx refuses the
  configuration), and Postgres's direct TLS refuses a connection without that ALPN; its classic negotiation is not
  TLS from the first byte, so nginx cannot open it either. **A Unix socket** in a volume nginx and Postgres share
  works instead: the inner hop never touches a network. Chosen (2.1.9.14, revised the same day; the owner had asked for
  TLS inside "if it can be done, not a requirement").

## 2.3.6.2 Proposal: password-free sign-in on the domain

### 2.3.6.2.1 What the owner asked

2026-10-07, after setting up the web UI's client certificate on the test Pi: the certificate should belong to people
**on the domain**, as part of their user profile, and give them single sign-on **without a password or MFA**. The
client certificate and the authenticator stay available for those who want both ("for paranoid people").

Today (2.1.6.2) every web UI sign-in needs a client certificate from fabric's CA *and* Keycloak's password and TOTP;
apps connected with `fabricctl sso` ask for the password and TOTP.

Two ways for a domain member to prove who they are without typing anything:

- **Kerberos (SPNEGO).** The domain logon already gives the person a Kerberos ticket from the site's DC. Keycloak
  accepts it with its Kerberos authenticator, beside the LDAP federation it already has. Nothing to issue, renew or
  revoke: disabling the account or leaving the domain ends it. Browsers must be told to trust `sso.<domain>` for
  Negotiate: fabric's GPO for Edge and Chrome on Windows, the Linux join script for Linux.
- **A per-user certificate** in the person's profile, which Keycloak accepts with its X.509 authenticator. Windows
  auto-enrols only from Microsoft's certificate service (MS-WCCE), which Samba does not provide, so fabric needs its
  own enrolment on Windows and Linux. Suits smart cards and TPM-bound keys.

Devices off the domain (phones, guests) keep the password and TOTP.

### 2.3.6.2.2 The owner's answers

2026-10-07, recorded in the register as 2.1.6.21, 2.1.8.1, 2.1.8.2, 2.1.6.22 (revised the same day: 2.3.6.2.5)
([2.1.6](2.1.6-identity.md)):

- **Both methods** (2.1.6.21): Kerberos by default, user certificates available too.
- **The web UI from a domain device** (2.1.8.1): signed in by the domain, but asks for TOTP by default; setup asks,
  and it can be changed later.
- **The web UI's client certificate** (2.1.8.2): an option, off by default, easy to turn on after the first standup —
  one of the layers an install can add as it hardens.
- **When** (2.1.6.22): after 0.6.0, tested on the owner's Proxmox lab (two Windows and two Ubuntu Server VMs).

### 2.3.6.2.3 Impact assessment

- **Architecture.** Keycloak gains a Kerberos keytab (a service principal `HTTP/sso.<domain>` in the site's DC) and
  browser flows that try Kerberos, then a user certificate, then password and TOTP. The web UI's gate moves from
  nginx (`ssl_verify_client on`) to a setting: with the certificate on, nginx requires it as now; with it off,
  nginx asks for none and the web UI relies on Keycloak, still binding the session to what signed in. User
  certificate enrolment is a new part of the agent and the join scripts.
- **Breaking changes.** None for 0.6.0 installs that keep the certificate: 2.1.8.2's default (off) applies to new
  installs, and an upgrade keeps an install's current gate until the owner changes it. 2.1.6.2 is replaced for the web
  UI part only; Keycloak with TOTP stays.
- **Security.** Off the certificate, the admin console is reachable by any LAN device's browser up to the Keycloak
  sign-in, so password guessing and web bugs are no longer stopped at nginx. TOTP for the console (2.1.8.1's default)
  and Keycloak's brute-force lockout remain. Status output and the web UI show the active layers (global Rule 10).
- **Scope creep.** User certificate enrolment for Windows is the large part; Kerberos alone covers the owner's
  domain machines. Building Kerberos first and enrolment second keeps each step testable.

### 2.3.6.2.4 Still to design before building

The chapters these change (Keycloak 1.6.2, the web UI's security model, the GPOs, the Linux join), the flows in
Keycloak, how a user certificate is enrolled and revoked on each OS, and the settings and their status lines.

### 2.3.6.2.5 Revised: a password by default, raised from the console

2026-10-07, the same day, after the owner asked whether a certificate could be unlocked with a Windows Hello PIN
so sign-in is not instant. The answer given: a **passkey** (WebAuthn) does that — Keycloak supports it natively,
the key is made in the PC's TPM and used only after the Hello PIN, fingerprint or face, it cannot be copied off
the device or used on a look-alike site, and phones and security keys work the same way. A certificate the user
imports is not reliably bound to Windows Hello outside Microsoft's own enrolment, which Samba lacks. Logging on
to Windows itself with Windows Hello for Business against Samba is not assumed; it is checked when this is
designed.

The owner then set the model:

- **2.1.6.23:** a new install asks everyone, admins included, for a username and password; Kerberos from the domain
  logon is on by default. This relaxes global Rule 10 (secure by default); the deviation is recorded in 2.1.1.34.
  2.1.8.1's TOTP default is revised away.
- **2.1.6.24:** every stronger layer is raised from the web console; lowering one is possible only at the command line
  on the fabric host, as root, with the layer's name typed to confirm, an audit entry and a warning that stays in
  doctor and the web UI. No floor: the command line can lower anything. Passkeys join the layers 2.1.6.21 offers.

Impact beyond 2.3.6.2.3: until this is built, 0.6.0 keeps 2.1.6.2 (client certificate, password and TOTP for the web
UI). When built, an install upgraded from 0.6.0 keeps the layers it has; only new installs start at a password.

### 2.3.6.2.6 The 0.6.1 design

Proposed 2026-10-07 for the owner's approval, from 2.1.6.21, 2.1.8.1, 2.1.8.2, 2.1.6.22, 2.1.6.23, 2.1.6.24 and 2.1.6.25, 2.1.6.26, 2.1.6.27, 2.1.6.28. When approved, each part moves into
its home chapter (1.8.2 the web UI's gates, 1.6.2 Keycloak, 1.6.5 the GPOs, 1.6.7 the Linux join, 1.1.8 the
settings, Volume 4 the procedures) in the commit that builds it.

#### 2.3.6.2.6.1 The layers and their settings

| Setting (fabric.yaml) | Values | New install | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `signin_kerberos` | true / false | false (2.1.6.32; true before 0.6.4, 2.1.6.23) | Keycloak accepts the domain logon's Kerberos ticket (SPNEGO) before asking for a password |
| `signin_admin_second_factor` | none / totp / passkey / any | none (2.1.6.23) | A second factor for the **admin tools**: the web console, OpenBao's UI, AdGuard's page (2.1.6.25) |
| `signin_everyone_second_factor` | none / totp / passkey / any | none (2.1.6.23) | A second factor for **every sign-in**: the admin tools and every app added with `fabricctl sso` (2.1.6.25) |
| `webui_client_cert` | true / false | false (2.1.8.2) | nginx requires a client certificate from fabric's CA for the web console |

The levels are ordered, weakest first: none < any < totp < passkey (revised while building, 2.3.6.2.8: `any`
accepts either factor, so it is weaker than requiring one; a passkey cannot be phished). `any` means a person with a
passkey uses it and anyone else uses TOTP, enrolling it at their next sign-in if they have none; they may add a
passkey in Keycloak's account page. The admin tools always get at least the everyone setting: their effective level
is the higher of the two. A passkey is a second factor here (after the password or Kerberos); signing in with a
passkey alone, without a password, is for a later release.

#### 2.3.6.2.6.2 Keycloak

- **Two flows** replace `fabric-webui-mfa`: `fabric-admin-signin` (bound to `fabric-webui`, `fabric-openbao` and
  AdGuard's client) and `fabric-signin`, the realm's browser flow (every other client, including the `app-*`
  clients of `fabricctl sso`, which no longer bind a flow of their own). Each is a copy of the stock browser flow:
  cookie, then Kerberos (ALTERNATIVE when `signin_kerberos`, else DISABLED), then the username and password form,
  then the second-factor sub-flow: DISABLED for none; REQUIRED with OTP REQUIRED for totp; REQUIRED with WebAuthn
  REQUIRED for passkey; REQUIRED with OTP and WebAuthn ALTERNATIVE for any. Converged on every apply, like today.
- **Kerberos:** a service account per site, `fabric-sso-<site>` in the site's `OU=service-accounts`, holds the SPNs
  `HTTP/sso.<domain>` and `HTTP/<hostname>.<domain>` (browsers may ask for the name a CNAME points to). Its keytab
  is exported by the samba container (`samba-tool domain exportkeytab`) into Keycloak's config folder (0400,
  Keycloak's user); its password is generated and kept like the other `ad_*` secrets. The LDAP provider gets
  `allowKerberosAuthentication`, the AD realm, the server principal and the keytab; a `krb5.conf` maps fabric's
  domain to the AD realm inside the container. Keycloak's own password check stays LDAP (not Kerberos).
- **WebAuthn policy** for passkeys: relying party `sso.<domain>`, user verification **required** (the Windows Hello
  PIN, fingerprint or face: the owner's point in 2.3.6.2.5), any authenticator attachment, resident keys preferred.

#### 2.3.6.2.6.3 The web console

- With `webui_client_cert` false, nginx asks for no client certificate (`ssl_verify_client off`) and the web app
  skips the gates that read it (1.8.2 gates 1, 2 and 5); the session binds to the session cookie and the sign-in's
  ID token instead of the certificate fingerprint (gate 7). With it true, everything is as in 0.6.0.
- `prompt=login` stays: each console sign-in runs the admin flow again (silent with Kerberos and no second factor).
- Setup's first admin kit holds no `.p12` while the certificate is off; `fabricctl client-cert` still makes one,
  ready for when it is turned on.

#### 2.3.6.2.6.4 Raising and lowering (2.1.6.24, 2.1.6.27)

- **Raise:** a **Security** page in the web console (admins only, 2.1.6.27) shows every layer, its state and what
  raising it does; *Raise* asks for a fresh sign-in (the last 5 minutes, like OpenBao's unlock methods), then
  fabric-agent writes the setting to `fabric.yaml`, converges Keycloak (and nginx for the certificate) and writes
  the audit log. The page offers only raises. Turning on the client certificate warns that each admin needs their
  `.p12` first (`fabricctl client-cert`), and is refused while the signed-in admin's browser presented none.
- **Lower:** only `sudo fabricctl security lower <layer> [<level>]` on the fabric host. It states what is lost, asks
  for the layer's name typed back (`--yes` does not skip it), writes the setting, converges, and writes the audit
  log with the root user and the sudo user.
- **Status:** `sudo fabricctl security` lists the layers. A layer lowered below a level it once had is marked in
  `/opt/fabric/config/security-lowered.json` until raised again; `fabricctl doctor` and the web console's Overview
  show a warning for it. Setup (`--file` or a re-run) refuses to lower a layer of an existing install and points to
  the command; a fresh install takes any values.

#### 2.3.6.2.6.5 Browsers (2.1.6.28)

- **Windows:** the site's Windows baseline GPO (1.6.5.20) adds the Negotiate allowlist for `sso.<domain>`: Edge and
  Chrome (`AuthServerAllowlist`) and Firefox (`Authentication` → `SPNEGO`).
- **Linux:** the join script writes the same for Firefox (`/etc/firefox/policies/policies.json`) and for Chromium
  and Chrome (their managed policy folders), and maps fabric's domain to the AD realm in `krb5.conf`
  (`[domain_realm]`).
- Without them (a browser that is not configured, or a device off the domain), Keycloak shows the password form.

#### 2.3.6.2.6.6 Not in 0.6.1

Per-user certificates (2.1.6.26), signing in with a passkey alone, and logging on to Windows with Windows Hello for
Business.

### 2.3.6.2.8 Spike K1 and step 2: what building changed

**Spike K1** (`spikes/kerberos-sso/`, 2026-10-07, against the keycloak suite's real DC and Keycloak 26.7):

- A keytab exported by `samba-tool domain exportkeytab` for a service account with two HTTP SPNs lets Keycloak accept
  a domain person's ticket with server principal `*`, both for `sso.<domain>` and for the host name a client
  canonicalises a CNAME to. The AD vendor's principal attribute (`userPrincipalName`) matches Samba's.
- **The account must be AES-only** (`msDS-SupportedEncryptionTypes` 24): without it Samba issues RC4 tickets and
  Java refuses them ("Encryption type RC4 with HMAC is not supported").
- Without a ticket Keycloak answers `401 Negotiate` with a page that continues to the password form (a browser
  submits it at once).
- A person made by fabric must change the one-time password before the KDC gives them a ticket (at their first
  Windows log-on in practice).

**Step 2** (Keycloak's flows and settings), changes to 2.3.6.2.6:

- Keycloak's stock browser flow puts Kerberos beside the password form at the top, so a Kerberos sign-in would skip
  the second factor. fabric builds its flows itself: the cookie (realm flow only), then a first factor (Kerberos or
  password), then the second factor. The admin flow takes no session cookie, which also closes a 0.6.0 gap (a
  sign-in through Keycloak's account page carried into OpenBao's UI without TOTP).
- With TOTP and a passkey both ALTERNATIVE, Keycloak sends someone who has neither to the passkey sign-in page, where
  they cannot go on. `any` is therefore a passkey sub-flow (only for someone with one) followed by a TOTP sub-flow
  that runs when the passkey one did not (Keycloak's "sub-flow executed" condition).
- The levels' order became none < any < totp < passkey (2.3.6.2.6.1).
- The keycloak suite proves it with a real DC and Keycloak (44 checks): the flows and their bindings, the default
  (a password only), raising the admin tools to TOTP (the console then asks for it; other sign-ins do not; the
  console ignores an app's session), Kerberos from a domain client (no password; still the console's TOTP; through
  the CNAME's name; the fallback; `signin_kerberos` off), passkeys with a software authenticator that reports user
  verification (registration, sign-in, a wrong key refused), and `any` (a passkey used; TOTP enrolled by someone with
  neither).

## 2.3.6.4 Proposal (0.7): host access, SSH and fabric's own admin groups

Written 2026-10-08, when the SSH certificate authority (2.1.7.5) was being built for 0.6.3. The owner's answers turned
it into a model of who may reach which host, so it moved to 0.7 with the Linux join's leftovers (2.1.6.31,
2.1.1.39). **Settled 2026-10-10** (2.1.6.34, 2.1.6.37 to 2.1.6.40): the points of 2.3.6.4.4 are answered; built in 0.9.

### 2.3.6.4.1 What the owner said

- SSH works between devices for whoever is authorised by role; everyone else is refused. By default nothing is
  authorised to SSH: fabric has its own groups, **`fabric-admins`** (people) and **`fabric-machine`** (machines).
  Machines (Linux desktops, possibly a group) and an admin can be authorised; a user cannot.
- Local accounts are untouched unless a GPO or setting restricts them; they keep their own rules, which fabric and AD do
  not manage.
- `fabric-admins` is for managing the host: root, and `fabricctl` on the command line. The web console's admins are
  separate; their default initial group is **Domain Admins**.
- The groups exist **per site and for the whole organisation**: only the top level's admins add or remove sites.
- A joined machine not in `fabric-machine` is **open by default**; once domain accounts exist, the person is
  **prompted to restrict** it.
- On a `fabric-machine` host, `fabric-admins` sign in by whichever methods are configured: an SSH certificate (from
  OpenBao), a Kerberos ticket, or a password.
- Host certificates reach joined machines **through AD**: the machine writes its SSH host key to its own computer object
  (a fabric schema attribute only it or an admin may write), a fabric timer signs it with OpenBao's host CA (principals
  from `dNSHostName`) and writes the certificate back, and the machine's timer reads it with its keytab.

### 2.3.6.4.2 The model as understood

| Who | Where | May |
|---|---|---|
| `<site>-fabric-admins`, organisation `fabric-admins` | the site's (or every) `fabric-machine` hosts and fabric hosts | SSH by the configured methods, root, `fabricctl`; the organisation's group alone adds and removes sites |
| web console admins (default: Domain Admins) | the web console | as today's admin bundle |
| anyone else in the domain | `fabric-machine` hosts | no SSH |
| anyone in the domain | other joined hosts | as the host's own rules: open until the owner restricts it when prompted |
| local accounts | everywhere | untouched |

SSH user certificates: 8 hours, issued only to `fabric-admins` members, the user name as the one principal (with
`TrustedUserCAKeys`, sshd takes every principal as a login name, so groups as principals could open other accounts).

### 2.3.6.4.3 What it changes

- **2.1.7.5:** certificates for `fabric-admins` only, the user name as principal (not the person's groups).
- **The web console's admin group** (`webui_admin_group`, default `admins`): its default becomes Domain Admins.
- **Sudo on joined Linux machines:** today `<site>-linux-sudo` grants it; `fabric-admins` takes or joins that role
  (with the sudo-rule editor and host groups of 2.1.6.31).
- **Federation:** adding and removing sites limited to the organisation's `fabric-admins`.
- **`fabricctl` for `fabric-admins`:** today it is root through sudo on the fabric host, which is not itself a member
  of its domain; giving the group that access means joining the fabric host to its own domain (new).
- **A new question:** restricting SSH on joined hosts once domain accounts exist (setup and the web console).

### 2.3.6.4.4 Open points

- Does the fabric host join its own domain (for `fabric-admins` to reach it), and how is it administered if the DC is
  down? **Answered 2026-10-09 (2.1.6.34):** it joins by default (an Advanced option leaves it out); local accounts
  stay untouched, so the host is administered as before with the DC down.
- How `fabric-admins` relates to `<site>-linux-sudo` and to the sudo-rule editor (replace, or one rule among others).
  **Answered 2026-10-10 (2.1.6.37):** it replaces it.
- What the restrict prompt offers (only `fabric-admins`; a host group; leave open) and where it is asked again.
  **Answered 2026-10-10 (2.1.6.38):** no prompt: group policy, `<site>-machine-admins` by default.
- Which login methods are on by default on a `fabric-machine` host. **Answered 2026-10-10 (2.1.6.39):** Kerberos only.
- How a machine joins `fabric-machine` (at join, an option; later from the web console). **Answered 2026-10-10
  (2.1.6.40):** at join by default, opted out there; later from the web console.

### 2.3.6.4.5 Found while scoping it

- OpenBao 2.7 has no Kerberos auth method (its auth plugins: AppRole, client certificates, JWT, Kubernetes, OIDC,
  userpass), so a machine cannot sign in to OpenBao with its keytab: hence host certificates through AD.

### 2.3.6.9.2 Does Samba honour nested groups on the allow list

Yes. Checked 2026-10-13 in Samba's own source (master):

- `source4/rpc_server/drsuapi/getncchanges.c`, `getncchanges_repl_secret()`: the replicated account is read with the
  attribute `tokenGroups`, and the read-only DC's account with `msDS-RevealOnDemandGroup` and `msDS-NeverRevealGroup`;
  the decision is `samdb_confirm_rodc_allowed_to_repl_to()`.
- `source4/dsdb/common/rodc_helper.c`, `samdb_confirm_rodc_allowed_to_repl_to()` and
  `samdb_confirm_rodc_allowed_to_repl_to_sid_list()`: the account's SID and its `tokenGroups` SIDs are compared with
  the two lists: on the denied list, refused; else on the allow list, allowed; else refused. The denied list is
  checked first, so it wins. krbtgt accounts and inter-domain trusts are refused outright.
- `tokenGroups` is the list of SIDs from a transitive group membership expansion (Microsoft Learn, *Token-Groups
  attribute*, learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/adschema/a-tokengroups): a member of a group nested in
  `<site>-rodc-cache` is allowed.
- `samba-tool rodc preload` (`python/samba/netcmd/rodc.py`) takes several accounts, `--file=-` (stdin) and
  `--ignore-errors` (a refused account is listed as "Error replicating DN …" and the rest go on); with no credentials
  it signs in as the DC's own machine account.

So people go in by nesting (`<site>-users`, `<site>-admins`); machines have no group of their own, so each is a direct
member, kept in step. Not proven on a running pair yet: the samba suite's cases below are written, not run.

The denied list fabric checks is what Samba's join writes for a read-only DC (the Denied RODC Password Replication
Group and the built-in Administrators, Server, Backup and Account Operators) and the denied group's default members
(Domain Admins, Enterprise Admins, Schema Admins, krbtgt among them). That these are exactly what Samba writes is from
its join and provisioning code as remembered, not re-read this round: the samba suite's run will show it (a run that
finds a list different on a fresh join is a fault in the check, not in the DC).

### 2.3.6.9.4 Known limits and open points

1. **A person taken out of `<site>-users` but kept.** The allow list no longer reaches them, so the writable DC refuses
   a new preload, but the password the read-only DC already holds stays there until it changes (AD's behaviour:
   nothing un-caches a secret). A person *removed* (deleted, as fabric removes people) is gone from the read-only DC
   after replication. Options: (A) say so (built: 4.6.5.6 tells the operator to reset the password too); (B) fabric
   resets the person's password when it sees them leave the group while cached (the read-only DC's
   `msDS-RevealedList` names who it holds). Recommendation: A; B only if sites take people out of `<site>-users`
   without removing them.
2. **People's directory writes at a read-only DC's site.** Found while building: adding, changing and removing people
   (`directory/create_person`, `remove_person`, `set_person_*`) go to the site's own DC, which is read-only and refuses
   them; only machines and the host's account are written at the writable DC (`samba/writable_server`). Caching does
   not need it (the site's people can be managed at the parent's console, and nesting follows them), but the console
   at a read-only DC's site cannot manage its people. Options: (A) leave it (built); (B) route every directory write
   at such a site through `writable_server`, as 2.1.6.52 did for machines. Recommendation: B, as its own change.
   **Settled by the coordinator 2026-10-13: (B), built** on the same branch, recorded as a revision of 2.1.6.52 (it
   completes that decision's intent; no new number). One routing point, `directory/run_op`: every operation not in
   its `READS` list goes to `writable_server`'s DC; reads stay local; refused with no writable DC known (before
   anything is asked) or with it not answering (the container's `directory_op` now reports a failed sign-in at
   another DC as `unreachable`, said plainly, nothing changed). Group Policy edits cannot be routed (the editor works
   on the DC's own database and SYSVOL), so `gpo_request` refuses `load`, `set` and `clear` at such a site.
   Tests: `tests/samba/directory_routing_test.py` (**run**: pure, 65 passed: every write op routed, every read local,
   READS checked against the DC's operations and their code, the refusals, GPO); `tests/samba/dc.py` (written,
   **not run**: a person made, disabled and removed at the writable DC from the RODC's site; a read at the RODC;
   refused with no writable DC known, one not answering, and the writable DC stopped). A run needs the `samba` suite.
3. **Accounts of another site nested by hand** are allowed by the read-only DC's policy while they are in the group
   (fabric cannot put them on the denied list: only a Domain Admin may write the DC's account). fabric never preloads
   them and refuses loudly each run (setup, apply, the hourly timer) until they are taken out. Recommendation: keep.
4. **Who may sign in per OU at a read-only DC's site** (found while building point 2). `fabricctl domain logon
   set|clear` and the console save the setting and apply; the apply's convergence at a read-only DC does only
   winbind's pipe, so the site's log-on GPOs are not rewritten and the change has no effect, without saying so.
   Options: (A) leave it (as built); (B) refuse it at such a site, as Group Policy edits are; (C) write the site's
   log-on GPOs from the site above. Recommendation: B now, C if read-only sites need their own log-on policy.
5. **A change and the read-only DC's own reads.** A write made at the writable DC shows in the site's lists (read at
   the read-only DC) only after the next replication, a few minutes. Options: (A) leave it (built; 4.6.5.6 says so);
   (B) after each write, have the read-only DC replicate the changed object at once (`samba-tool drs replicate
   --local --single-object`). Recommendation: A unless the console's delay confuses its users.
