## 2.1.6.1 Who may do what (people)

- **Decision:** RBAC: per-area permissions as Keycloak realm roles, bundles (Admin, Network operator without device management, Equipment operator for 802.1X + 389-DS hardware, PKI operator, Helpdesk, Auditor); the agent verifies the user's signed token on every call; OpenBao policies follow the same roles ([1.6.8.1](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.8-rbac.md#1681-access-control-for-people-rbac-across-the-stack-decided-2161-built)).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.3, 1.8
- **Made:** 2026-09-27 to 2026-10-01, while fabric was designed, by the owner.
- **Why:** people get only the areas they work in, and every tool checks the same roles.
- **Status:** In force; built.

## 2.1.6.2 Identity (checklist E2)

- **Decision:** Keycloak (OIDC, TOTP enforced) on 389-DS; a client certificate from fabric's CA for the web UI.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.8
- **Made:** 2026-10-03 (the checklist), by the owner.
- **Why:** 2.3.1.8.5.
- **Status:** Revised: the directory is Samba AD since 2026-10-04 (2.1.6.6); since 2026-10-07 sign-in starts at a password with TOTP raised from the web console (2.1.6.23, 2.1.6.24), and the web UI's client certificate is an option, off by default (2.1.8.2); built in 0.6.1.

## 2.1.6.6 Where passwords live (the directory)

- **Decision:** Samba AD replaces 389-DS as fabric's one directory (people, groups, ids, devices, passwords), if the S0 spike proves it.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.2, 1.9, 1.11
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner. Proposed then: 389-DS stays the source of truth and passwords reach Samba when set; alternatives were AD as the source with Keycloak federating to it, or 389-DS's Windows-sync plugin.
- **Why:** 2.3.6.1.1.
- **Status:** In force; the spike proved it (2.3.6.1.9), and it is built.

## 2.1.6.7 Site DCs

- **Decision:** One domain; an OU per site with rights delegated to its admins; configuration, schema and Group Policy replicate everywhere. Each site chooses its DC type — writable, read-only or none — and can change it later (demote and re-join); site DCs are independent and resync after being offline; each DC may write only its own site's OU by default, its limits listed with overrides in the Federation tab; the root DC holds the domain-wide roles.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner (proposed: read-only DCs if S0 proves them, else full DCs); revised the same day.
- **Why:** 2.3.6.1.10.
- **Status:** In force, revised 2026-10-04 (each site chooses its DC type).

## 2.1.6.10 Default GPOs

- **Decision:** The trusts fabric's services need (its root CA) are pushed by default; every other policy is optional, set through an ADMX-driven editor in fabric's menus (or the standard tools). fabric ships its own ADMX templates and a blank starter template, and links to Microsoft's templates, which are not shipped (licensing).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner (proposed: time, the 802.1X profile and the root CA).
- **Why:** 2.3.6.1.1.
- **Status:** In force.

## 2.1.6.11 The AD domain name

- **Decision:** The AD domain name is chosen at setup, with no default, and cannot be changed later. Sites get the domain from their invitation. The AD domain is never fabric's own domain (nor a parent of it). The suggestion is a protected sibling at the top of the organisation's name, apart from the sites' names: `ad.` plus the first node's domain without its first label (`lan.j-j.family` → `ad.j-j.family`); a sub-domain such as `ad.lan.j-j.family` stays allowed.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 3.3
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner, after the spike's first runs; revised the same day.
- **Why:** 2.3.6.1.10, 2.3.6.1.13.
- **Status:** In force, revised 2026-10-04: same-domain mode is dropped. It replaced 2.1.9.8 (the organisation base DN).

## 2.1.6.12 Layout in the directory

- **Decision:** Everything fabric manages lives under `OU=sites`, one OU per site; the root site's OU also holds the organisation-wide items (address plan, shared roles and groups). AD's own containers stay as they are; its default containers for new users and machines point to site OUs.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner, after the spike's first runs.
- **Why:** 2.3.6.1.10.
- **Status:** In force; nesting refined by 2.1.6.20.

## 2.1.6.13 Passwords

- **Decision:** The admin sets the whole password policy at setup — no fabric defaults, nothing preselected; an unattended setup refuses without it; changeable later in the menus. fabric's one-time passwords are generated to satisfy the policy.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 3.3
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner, after the spike's first runs.
- **Why:** 2.3.6.1.10.
- **Status:** In force. Revised by 2.1.6.35 on 2026-10-09: a new domain starts from fabric's default policy, no longer asked at setup; changeable any time.

## 2.1.6.14 Access per site

- **Decision:** People and machines are tracked across the whole domain, but access is authorized per site: by default a site's people may log on only to that site's machines (logon rights per site OU, enforced on Windows and, through SSSD, on Linux), and the same per-site groups govern 802.1X and web sign-in. Access to another site's equipment is granted explicitly, or by a blanket policy set as an override in the Federation tab.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9, 1.11
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner, after the spike's first runs.
- **Why:** 2.3.6.1.10.
- **Status:** In force.

## 2.1.6.15 A previous password after a change (Q7)

- **Decision:** AD accepts a previous password for network sign-ins for 60 minutes after a change; fabric turns that off (0 minutes): a changed password stops working at once; settable (`ad_old_password_minutes`).
- **Shapes:** 1.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner, approving the rewritten directory design.
- **Why:** 2.3.6.1.11.
- **Status:** In force.

## 2.1.6.16 SYSVOL between DCs

- **Decision:** Samba replicates GPO objects, not their files. Each GPO is owned by the site it is linked to (the root for domain GPOs); every DC pulls the GPO folders it does not own from their owner's DC **over SMB**, signed in as its own machine account (445 is already open between DCs), on the condition that SYSVOL and NETLOGON are **never advertised**: not browseable, no NetBIOS, no mDNS, no WS-Discovery ([1.9.8.15](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.9.8-federation-on-ad.md#19815-group-policy-between-sites-over-smb)).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner, approving the rewritten directory design; revised 2026-10-05 (owner).
- **Why:** [2.3.6.1.21](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#236121-the-owners-s8-answers).
- **Status:** In force, revised 2026-10-05: over SMB (was: over the federation endpoint, mutual TLS).

## 2.1.6.17 uid/gid numbers when writable site DCs create people while cut off

- **Decision:** Each site gets its own id block from its parent at join (`posix_id_block`, default 100 000): the next block after every one the domain holds, so a nested parent never repeats another branch's; the root's block keeps today's numbers.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9
- **Made:** 2026-10-04, by the owner, approving the rewritten directory design; revised 2026-10-05.
- **Why:** 2.3.6.1.11.
- **Status:** In force, revised 2026-10-05 with nesting (2.1.6.20): the block comes from the parent (was: from the root). Replaced 2.1.6.5.
  **Revised by 2.1.6.46 on 2026-10-11:** blocks of 1 000 000 (was: 100 000), the root's from 1000000 (was:
  5001), fixed ranges inside each, at most 999 sites; for new installs from 0.7.0.

## 2.1.6.20 Nesting in the directory

- **Decision:** A site's OU is placed **in its parent's OU** (the root's OU is the top, under `OU=sites`) and **inherits by default**: Group Policy through AD's GPO inheritance and permissions through AD's ACL inheritance, from the domain down. A parent links a GPO **enforced** for a control its children may not change; anything else a child may override with its own GPO or by blocking inheritance. A parent's roles (2.1.9.13) and agent reach every site below it. A site with no DC reads and writes through its nearest ancestor that has one. Re-parenting moves the OU ([1.9.8.14](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.9.8-federation-on-ad.md#19814-nesting-and-inheritance)).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9
- **Made:** 2026-10-05, by the owner (S8.8). Replaces the flat layout of 1.9.8.6.
- **Why:** [2.3.6.1.21](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#236121-the-owners-s8-answers).
- **Status:** In force.

## 2.1.6.21 How people on a domain device sign in without a password or MFA

- **Decision:** **Both:** Kerberos from the domain logon (Keycloak's Kerberos authenticator) by default, and a per-user certificate in the person's profile (Keycloak's X.509 authenticator); off the domain, password and TOTP.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.8
- **Made:** 2026-10-07, by the owner.
- **Why:** password-free sign-in for people on the domain, with the client certificate and the authenticator kept as layers to add (2.3.6.2).
- **Status:** In force, narrowed 2026-10-07 by 2.1.6.26 (per-user certificates later); revised the same day by 2.1.6.23 (a password by default).

## 2.1.6.23 What a new install asks at sign-in

- **Decision:** **A username and password, for everyone, admins included**; Kerberos from the domain logon on by default. A deviation from global Rule 10 (2.1.1.34).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.8
- **Made:** 2026-10-07, by the owner.
- **Why:** 0.6.0's sign-in was too strict to be usable; a first standup should be simple, and everything can be raised afterwards (2.3.6.2.5).
- **Status:** In force; built in 0.6.1. Revised 2.1.8.1. Revised by 2.1.6.32 on 2026-10-09: Kerberos is off by default and asked at setup.

## 2.1.6.24 Raising and lowering sign-in security

- **Decision:** **Raised from the web console** (TOTP, a passkey such as Windows Hello, a per-user certificate, the web UI's client certificate); **lowered only at the command line** on the fabric host, as root, with the layer's name typed to confirm (`--yes` does not skip it), an audit entry, and a warning in `fabricctl doctor` and the web UI until it is raised again. **No floor:** the command line can lower anything.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.3, 1.8, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-07, by the owner.
- **Why:** going up should be easy; going down should take deliberate handling (2.3.6.2.5).
- **Status:** In force; built in 0.6.1. Revised by 2.1.8.4 on 2026-10-09: from the release after 0.7 (2.1.1.42), lowering is also possible from the web console's settings page, behind the admin's password.

Added while building: nobody disables or removes themselves, the admin group never loses its last member who can sign in, and only an admin changes a fabric group's members.

## 2.1.6.32 Kerberos sign-in off by default, asked at setup

- **Decision:** `signin_kerberos` is off on a new install, and setup asks for it (default no), explaining that only
  computers joined to the domain hold the ticket it uses. It can be turned on or off later as now (the Security page,
  `fabricctl security kerberos on|off`). An install that already has it keeps its setting on upgrade.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.8, 3.3
- **Made:** 2026-10-09, by the owner, for 0.6.4, after the test install of 0.6.3-rc.1: Keycloak's Kerberos challenge
  (`401 Negotiate`) made the browser of a computer outside the domain show its own Windows sign-in box before fabric's
  page, which looked like a password fabric never gave.
- **Why:** the first computer to sign in is almost never a domain member, and an unexplained browser box is the
  first thing a new admin meets ([2.3.6.5](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#2365-the-first-sign-in-and-changing-setups-choices-later-2026-10-09)).
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-09** for 0.6.4 (2.1.1.41). Revises 2.1.6.23. Revised by 2.1.2.16 on 2026-10-09: not asked at setup; off, turned on later.

## 2.1.6.33 The first admin: fabric-admin, with the password chosen in setup

- **Decision:** setup asks the first admin's name, recommending **`fabric-admin`**, and their password, typed twice
  and checked against the password policy just set. It is their real password: no `initial-password.txt`, no forced
  change at the first sign-in. `fabric-admin` is the one `fabric-` name a person may have (the prefix stays reserved
  for fabric's host service accounts, none of which uses it). Keycloak's admin role, also called `fabric-admin`
  today (`webui_admin_role`), is renamed **`fabric-console-admin`**, and every other fabric name that would read the
  same is kept apart from it. This admin's password is what the settings page asks for (2.1.8.4).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.8, 3.3, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-09, by the owner ("set a first user, with a recommended user name of fabric-admin, and have them
  set the first password in the installer"; a person in the domain; "rename keycloak, and if there is a group that
  conflicts lets deconflict that too").
- **Why:** the owner signed in without knowing which password was asked, or where it was: a password chosen in setup
  is known; one name for the first admin across the console and, from the release after 0.7, the host (2.1.6.34, 2.1.1.42).
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-09** for 0.6.4 (2.1.1.41). An unattended setup takes the password from the secrets
  delivery (Rule 10: never on a command line), never generates one silently.

## 2.1.6.34 The fabric host joins its own domain by default

- **Decision:** setup makes the fabric host a member of its own domain, recommended and on by default, with an
  Advanced option to leave it out. Domain sign-in is added beside the host's local accounts, which stay untouched
  (root and local sudo work with the domain controller down). The host's domain identity is the domain controller's
  own machine account (the DC runs on this host under its name, so a second member join under the same name is not
  possible). Permissions start low and are raised later (2.3.6.4: `fabric-admins`).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.2, 3.3
- **Made:** 2026-10-09, by the owner ("by default the system that you are logging into (aka the host for fabric)
  becomes the first domain member … in the advanced options they can stop it"; local accounts always work).
- **Why:** better trust from the start, and almost every install wants it; answers the first open point of
  [2.3.6.4.4](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#23644-open-points).
- **Status:** In force; to build with the host-access proposal (2.3.6.4), in the release after 0.7 (moved by 2.1.1.42 on 2026-10-10; it was set for 0.7). **Placed in 0.9 by 2.1.1.45 on 2026-10-10.** **Moved to 0.7.1 by 2.1.1.51 on 2026-10-12.** **In force, revised by 2.1.6.48 on 2026-10-12 (the host's identity: its own computer account, not the DC's).**

## 2.1.6.35 The domain's default password policy

- **Decision:** a new domain starts with: at least **12 characters**, **complexity** on (three of upper case, lower
  case, digits and other characters, and not the user name), **5** previous passwords remembered, a new password may
  be changed again at once, passwords **never expire**, and an account is **locked after 10 failed sign-ins in 15
  minutes, for 15 minutes**. Changed any time (`fabricctl domain password-policy`, the vars editor; the settings page
  after 0.7, 2.1.8.4, 2.1.1.42); a vars file may set any of it at install.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 3.3, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-09, by the owner (the lighter of three: Microsoft's baseline and NIST 800-63B were the others).
- **Why:** setup no longer asks it (2.1.2.16), so a domain needs a starting policy; a home network's, easy to raise.
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-09** for 0.6.4 (2.1.1.41). Revises 2.1.6.13 (nothing preselected).

## 2.1.6.36 Machines sorted into OUs by OS family

- **Decision:** each site's `OU=machines` gets **`OU=windows`** and **`OU=linux`**, and a machine is placed by its
  operating system **once**: at join (the Linux join script joins into `OU=linux`; `fabricctl domain add-machine`
  pre-creates the account in the OU of the OS it is given), and a Windows machine that joined itself into
  `OU=machines` is sorted once by a fabric timer from its `operatingSystem`. After that fabric never moves it: an
  admin's move wins, and admins may make sub-OUs of their own (labs, kiosks), which fabric leaves alone. The Linux join
  writes `operatingSystem` and `operatingSystemVersion` from `/etc/os-release` (Windows writes its own). The OS is
  the machine's own claim, so the OUs are for convenience policy (browsers, desktop settings, update rings); what is
  security (who may sign in, sudo, SSH) stays on groups an admin controls (2.3.6.4, 2.1.6.31). Fabric's own lookups
  already search all of `OU=machines`.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-09, by the owner ("can we automatically put it in sub ous to make gpos easier?"; OS family plus
  admin-made OUs; placed at join, then kept; after 0.7, 2.1.1.42).
- **Why:** Group Policy is linked to OUs, so machines of one kind in one OU make GPOs easy to aim; placing once keeps
  a machine's own claim from moving it later, and leaves the admin in charge of the tree.
- **Status:** In force; to build with the host-access work (2.3.6.4) and the Linux join's leftovers (2.1.6.31), in the release after 0.7 (moved by 2.1.1.42 on 2026-10-10; it was set for 0.7). **Placed in 0.9 by 2.1.1.45 on 2026-10-10.**

## 2.1.6.46 Site id blocks of a million, from 1000000, with fixed ranges inside each

- **Decision:** site *S*'s uid/gid block is *S* × 1 000 000 to *S* × 1 000 000 + 999 999 (the root: 1000000-1999999),
  its first id for the site's first admin, and inside every block fixed ranges by use: admin accounts, service
  accounts, fabric's built-in groups, admins' groups, people, guests, reserved
  ([1.6.3.17](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.3-samba-directory.md#16317-the-ranges-inside-a-sites-id-block-070)).
  At most 999 sites, so every id stays below 1 000 000 000; the 1000th join is refused, with no setting to raise
  it. fabric's organisation-wide groups move from 1100-1121
  and 5000 into the root's built-in range. The Linux join moves local subordinate ids above AD's range and warns
  about overlaps. The block's start and size are permanent; the ranges are fabric's, not settings. Existing installs
  keep their numbers.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9, 1.3
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner: "should site 1 ... start at 100001-199999, I think those numbers make most sense
  with 100000 reserved for the default admin"; "breakdown every 100000 block into default ranges, reserving sections
  for each use case ... I leave it to you to suggest appropriately sized ranges"; the ranges proposed approved ("yes
  this looks good"); then, the same day: "lets try to narrow that down to 2147, so we will actually give a block of a
  milliion to each site, and make sure to scale everything appropriate to that" (blocks of 100 000 became 1 000 000,
  every range ×10; the 2147th site, which would get a short block, refused); "we are also going to soft cap this
  at 1000 (we won't even be able to test it)", then "can we do 999 sites, I think that gives us a clean end right?"
  (999 the one limit: ids below 1 000 000 000, no setting).
- **Why:** the old layout (people from 5001, a 100 000 block, fixed group ids 1100-1121 and 5000) put fabric's ids
  among Linux's own: local users and groups, systemd's dynamic users, `nobody`, rootless containers' subordinate ids;
  the users OU's `uid_range` end (50000) was never read; nothing refused a block past AD's 32-bit limit. A million
  per site starts clear of all of them and gives every use room; 999 sites is far past the 20 fabric is designed for
  ([1.9.1.4](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.9.1-sites.md#1914-design-and-test-scope-up-to-20-sites)).
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-11** for 0.7.0 (2.1.1.49; 1.6.3.17 lists what, and the one choice made
  while building: the built-in range's first hundred kept for fabric's fixed gids). Revises 2.1.6.17. **Revised
  2026-10-13 by 2.1.6.51** (no install from before 0.7.0 exists): "existing installs keep their numbers" no longer
  applies to the settings; their upgrade (`keep_old_install`) is deleted (S10). What remains for an older block (the
  DC's one-run handing out, the template's tolerance of an older vars file) is registered as S11 for the owner.
  **Revised again 2026-10-13 (S11 done):** the one-run handing out is deleted; a block that is not a million from a
  multiple of a million is refused, in the settings (`posix_id_start`, `posix_id_block`) and by the DC's converge.

## 2.1.6.47 Ids lent between sites when one fills: deferred indefinitely

- **Decision:** not built now, and not ruled out. The owner asked whether federation could give people uids from
  another site's block, to balance use, when a quorum of DCs agrees. If it is ever needed, it uses the DCs' own
  mechanism, never a quorum fabric builds: AD's single-master role, as the RID master hands out RID pools. The
  role's holder (the root's DC) grants an overflow pool (for example 100 000 ids, from the space past the last site's
  block) to a site whose people range passes about 90% full, only while it is reachable, and the grant is written to
  AD, where it replicates; a site cut off keeps using what it holds. A quorum would need a consensus layer beside AD
  (its replication is multi-master and eventually consistent, with no voting): not built, and the scaling scope
  1.9.1.4 keeps out.
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9
- **Made:** 2026-10-11, by the owner: "we don't need it, but this is an engineering decision that should be more
  considered as deferred indefinitely as opposed to a hard no"; "no we would NOT build a qurom we would use dc's internal
  machanism to be clear".
- **Why:** each site's block holds 799 999 people (2.1.6.46), far past what one site of fabric's scale needs, so no
  site is expected to fill; the per-site blocks are what keep cut-off DCs from giving the same id twice. Ids already
  cross sites when a person moves (their uid stays), so a lent pool would not change what an id means: where its
  person was created.
- **Status:** Deferred indefinitely. Revisit if a site's people range ever passes 50% full, or if fabric's site
  scope (1.9.1.4) grows.

## 2.1.6.48 The fabric host joins as its own computer account, not the DC's

- **Decision:** the fabric host's domain identity is its own computer account, `<HOSTNAME>$`, a member like any other
  machine: setup's `member` step pre-creates it as `fabricctl domain add-machine` makes one, in the site's
  `OU=machines/OU=linux`, exports its keys (and only its) to `/etc/krb5.keytab`, and points sssd at it, so the site's
  Group Policy reaches the host (the log-on policy of its OU, fabric's sudo rules and host groups). The DC keeps its
  own account, `<DC_NAME>$`, and its keys never reach the host. `fabricctl setup --undo member` removes the account
  with the files; an install whose keytab holds the DC's account moves to its own at the next setup
  ([1.6.11](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.11-host-access.md).8).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.2, 3.3, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner: "Separate and distinct identities so host may be managed by GPO settings."
- **Why:** the DC's account sits in AD's `OU=Domain Controllers`, where no site's policy reaches, so the host could
  not be managed as the site's other machines are. Since 0.7.0 the DC has a name of its own (2.1.6.45), so the host
  and its DC can be two accounts, as any member and DC; and the DC's keys no longer sit in a file on the host.
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-12** for 0.7.1 (1.6.11.8 lists what, and the choices made while building).
  Revises 2.1.6.34 (the host's identity). Open points from the build: [2.3.6.7](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#2367-the-hosts-own-account-open-points-2026-10-12).
  **Revised by 2.1.6.49 (the host's OU, and managed by fabric), 2.1.6.50 (its password's renewal), 2.1.6.51 (no
  DC's account for a DC named as the host), 2.1.6.52 (no DC's account at a read-only DC's site) and 2.1.6.53 (no
  access filter: the OU's policy alone) on 2026-10-12.**

## 2.1.6.49 The fabric host is a machine fabric manages, in an OU of its own

- **Decision:** the fabric host is a machine fabric manages (2.1.6.43), and its computer account lives in
  **`OU=fabric-hosts`**, in the site's OU beside `OU=machines`, so Group Policy can be aimed at fabric's hosts apart from
  the site's other machines. Setup's `member` step places the account there once (and in `<site>-fabric-machines`;
  an admin's later move or removal is kept) and, while it is in that group, applies fabric's management on the host:
  Kerberos for SSH, a domain account's password not taken over SSH, the domain's names from fabric's DNS, and an hourly
  timer that follows the group. **The host's exception to 2.1.6.39:** local accounts' SSH sign-in is left as the host
  had it (fabric never turns `PasswordAuthentication` off there), so the appliance can always be reached when its own
  domain controller is down; a configuration sshd refuses is never loaded
  ([1.6.11.11](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.11-host-access.md#161111-the-fabric-host-as-a-machine-fabric-manages-071)).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.2, 3.3, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner: "yes and it should be in a AD host OU" (the fourth point of
  [2.3.6.7](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#2367-the-hosts-own-account-open-points-2026-10-12)). Settled while building: the
  OU's name and place, and how the host is kept from locking its owner out (below).
- **Why:** the owner manages the appliance as the site's other machines, with policy of its own. Beside `OU=machines`,
  not below it: GPOs and the log-on policy meant for every machine reach the appliance only where an admin links them,
  and `<site>-machine-admins` (full control of `OU=machines`) get no control of the appliance's account. Local
  accounts keep their SSH sign-in because the host's KDC is the DC on the host itself: with the DC broken, Kerberos is
  impossible, and a local account is how the DC is repaired. Rejected: turning local passwords off once a Kerberos
  sign-in has been seen to work (it proves Kerberos worked once, not that it will when the DC fails); turning them off
  with a list of local exceptions (one account forgotten and the appliance needs its console).
- **Impact:** a new OU in every site (converged), a new directory operation (`place_fabric_host`), a new command
  (`fabricctl domain host-managed`) and timer, the `member` plan longer (an upgrade asks it again). An account of
  0.7.1's first build is moved at the next setup. Nothing else breaks: lookups of a site's machines include the OU.
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-12** for 0.7.1. Revises 2.1.6.48 (the host's OU, and unmanaged); a deviation
  from 2.1.6.39 on the fabric host alone (local accounts). **Revised by 2.1.6.53 on 2026-10-12** (the OU always
  carries a log-on policy, the fabric admins by default).

## 2.1.6.50 Renewing the fabric host's machine password: a setting, off by default

- **Decision:** a setting, `host_machine_password_renew_days`, **0 (off) by default**: the host's machine password is
  set once and kept. From 1 to 999, setup's `member` step installs `adcli` and sssd renews the password once it is
  that many days old. Anything but a whole number from 0 to 999 is refused at apply
  ([1.6.11.12](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.11-host-access.md#161112-renewing-the-hosts-machine-password-071)).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.1.9, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner: "configurable, not configured by default" (the fifth point of
  [2.3.6.7](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#2367-the-hosts-own-account-open-points-2026-10-12)). The bound (999, Windows' own for
  the same policy) chosen while building.
- **Why:** Samba never expires a machine's password, so nothing breaks without renewal; a site that wants it, as
  Windows does every 30 days, turns it on.
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-12** for 0.7.1.

## 2.1.6.52 The fabric host at a read-only DC's site: its own account, made at the writable DC and cached

- **Decision:** where a site's DC is read-only, the fabric host still has its own computer account (2.1.6.48): setup
  makes it at the writable DC that site replicates from, in `OU=fabric-hosts`, and puts it in the site's
  `<site>-rodc-cache` group, which the site's join puts on the read-only DC's password replication allow list, so the
  read-only DC caches the account and the host signs in with the link down. With the writable DC not answering, the
  `member` step refuses, saying why and what to do; it never falls back to the DC's account. A second run changes
  nothing; undo removes the account (and with it its place in the cached group)
  ([1.6.11.13](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.11-host-access.md#161113-the-fabric-host-at-a-read-only-dcs-site-071)).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9, 1.2, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner: "B" (the second point of [2.3.6.7](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#2367-the-hosts-own-account-open-points-2026-10-12)). Settled while
  building: the group (one per site, so the allow list is written once, at the join, while the join account still
  exists), the preload at once, and the check that the writable DC answers.
- **Why:** one identity model on every site, so Group Policy reaches the host whatever its DC; the DC's keys stay off
  the host. A group on the allow list, not the account itself: only the join account (a Domain Admin for the hour of
  the join) may change a DC's account, and the site's agent may change the site's groups.
- **Impact:** a new group in every site (converged), one write at a read-only DC's join, the host's directory
  operations given a server. A site joined before 0.7.1 lacks the allow-list entry: its preload is refused, said so,
  until a setup with the join account writes it. Not proven end to end with a fabric host on a read-only DC (2.3.6.8).
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-12** for 0.7.1. Revises 2.1.6.48 (its second exception, the DC's account).
  **Revised by 2.1.6.55 on 2026-10-13:** the same group now caches the site's people and its other machines too, not
  the fabric host alone.
  **Revised 2026-10-13 (completing its intent, at the coordinator's direction; 2.3.6.9.4 point 2):** every directory
  write at a read-only DC's site goes to the writable DC, not only the host's account, the machines and the DHCP
  options: one routing point, `directory/run_op`, sends every operation that is not a read (`READS`: people, groups,
  roles, devices, host groups, sudo rules, the address plan, any new operation) to `samba/writable_server`'s DC;
  reads stay at the site's own DC. No writable DC known: refused before anything is asked; it does not answer:
  refused, naming it, nothing changed. Group Policy edits (`fabricctl gpo load|set|clear`, the console), which work
  on a DC's own copy and SYSVOL, are refused at such a site, saying to make them at the site above. Built, untested
  (unit test run, samba suite cases written, not run).
  **Revised again 2026-10-13 (the coordinator, 2.3.6.9.4 point 4, B):** who may sign in per OU is refused there too
  (`fabricctl domain logon set|clear`, the console), since the read-only DC never writes the site's log-on GPOs;
  policies saved before are flagged as never applied (`domain logon show`, status, the console). Writing them from
  the site above (C) stays possible later.

## 2.1.6.53 Who signs in to the fabric host: its OU's log-on policy alone, the fabric admins by default

- **Decision:** the log-on policy of the host's OU, `OU=fabric-hosts`, is the one gate on who signs in to the fabric
  host with a domain account; fabric writes no access filter of its own on the host. That OU always carries a policy:
  the setting's when it names `fabric-hosts`, else by default `fabric-admins` and `<site>-fabric-admins`, at the
  keyboard and remotely (with break-glass and local Administrators, as every fabric log-on policy). Admins change who
  may sign in by editing that OU's policy, in one place, like any OU's; local accounts, root and local sudo stay the
  break-glass way in ([1.6.11.4](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.11-host-access.md#16114-who-may-sign-in-per-ou),
  1.6.11.8, 1.6.11.11).
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.2, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner: option (b), "It's up to policy owners to create policy, put it in a single
  pane." (the third point of [2.3.6.7](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#2367-the-hosts-own-account-open-points-2026-10-12)). Settled while building: the default policy, written on
  every site (upgrades too), so an upgraded site's legacy policy on its site OU never opens the appliance to its users.
- **Why:** two gates (policy and a filter) meant an admin who granted a group on the OU saw it refused with no reason
  in the policy; one gate is where the admin looks. The default keeps the appliance the fabric admins' (2.1.6.41)
  until a policy owner decides otherwise.
- **Impact:** sssd's `ad_access_filter` is gone from the host (the next setup rewrites `sssd.conf`); every site gets
  the GPO `fabric: <site> log-on fabric-hosts`; `fabricctl domain logon show` lists `fabric-hosts`. A `clear` of it
  puts the default back, never its parent's.
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-12** for 0.7.1. Revises 2.1.6.48 (its access filter) and 2.1.6.49 (the OU's
  policy).

## 2.1.6.55 A read-only DC's site: its people and machines cached there, privileged accounts never

- **Decision:** at a site whose DC is read-only, fabric uses the site's `<site>-rodc-cache` group (2.1.6.52, on the
  read-only DC's password replication allow list) for **all** the site's people and machines, so they sign in while
  the link to the writable DC is down:
  - **People:** the site's `<site>-users` and `<site>-admins` are **nested** in the group. Samba's password replication
    policy reads the account's `tokenGroups`, its transitive group membership, so a member of a nested group is
    allowed (checked in Samba's source and Microsoft's attribute reference: 2.3.6.9.2).
  - **Machines:** every computer account in the site's `OU=machines` (and below) and `OU=fabric-hosts` is a direct
    member (no group holds a site's machines), kept in step by fabric.
  - **Nothing else:** a direct member fabric did not put there (a machine that left the site, anything added by hand)
    is taken out. An account the nested groups reach that is not the site's own (another site's person, an
    organisation account) is never preloaded and is reported loudly, naming the group to remove it from.
  - **Preloaded** (`samba-tool rodc preload`, from the writable DC) at the join (setup), at every apply, hourly
    (`fabric-rodc-cache.timer`) and by hand (`fabricctl domain rodc-cache`); idempotent: a second run changes no
    membership.
  - **Removed from the site, removed from caching:** fabric removes a person or machine by deleting the account at the
    writable DC; the deletion replicates and the read-only DC holds it no more, and the allow list no longer reaches
    it.
  - **Never cached:** the read-only DC's denied list stays as Samba's join writes it (the Denied RODC Password
    Replication Group, with Domain Admins, Enterprise Admins, Schema Admins and krbtgt in it, and the built-in
    Administrators, Server, Backup and Account Operators). The denied list wins over the allow list, so a Domain Admin
    among a site's admins is still refused. Fabric never writes the denied list, and checks it before every run:
    weakened, nothing is changed or cached and the run says what is missing. Other sites' fabric admins are never
    nested: `<site>-fabric-admins` (which holds the organisation's `fabric-admins`) is not in the group.
  ([1.6.11.14](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.11-host-access.md#161114-a-read-only-dcs-site-its-people-and-machines-cached-09),
  [4.6.5.6](../volume_4_operations/4.6.5-windows-domain.md#4656-a-read-only-dcs-site-who-is-cached-09).)
- **Shapes:** 1.6, 1.9, 4.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-13, by the owner: option (a), answering point 4 of 2.3.6.8 (caching people at read-only DC
  sites). Settled while building (2.3.6.9): the nesting (Samba honours it), what "the site's own" means (a person in
  the site's `OU=people`, a computer in its machine OUs), the denied list checked from the read-only DC's own copy,
  an account refused by the denied list reported and not retried, the hourly timer.
- **Why:** 1.9.8.3 promised that a read-only DC's site keeps signing in its cached people and machines with the link
  cut; 2.1.6.52 built only the host's part. One group, already on the allow list since the join, means no further
  write to the DC's account (which only a Domain Admin may make). Nested groups follow the site's people with no
  copy to drift; machines have no group of their own, so fabric keeps them. Rejected: (b) every person a direct
  member (a copy of `<site>-users` to keep in step); (c) only the site's admins (the site's users could not sign in
  when the link is down).
- **Impact:** a new directory operation (`sync_rodc_cache`), a new command (`fabricctl domain rodc-cache`) and timer
  (only at a read-only DC's site), a step at setup and at every apply. A site joined before 0.7.1 (its allow list
  without the group) is refused, said so, until a setup with the join account writes it. Known limit: a person taken
  out of `<site>-users` but not removed keeps the password the read-only DC already holds until it changes
  (2.3.6.9.4).
- **Status:** In force; **built 2026-10-13** for 0.9, tests written, **not run** (the owner's direction of
  2026-10-12: 2.3.6.9.3). Revises 2.1.6.52 (the group caches the site's people and machines, not the host alone).
