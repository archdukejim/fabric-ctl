## 2.1.11.1 Which switches and access points 802.1X must support

- **Decision:** **UniFi first** (the UniFi Network application, its switches and access points), **Cisco supported
  too** (Catalyst switches on IOS-XE); any other switch as `generic` (RFC 3580 VLAN attributes, RADIUS/UDP, no CoA).
  Asked 2026-09-27 to 2026-10-01 as "needs the owner's inventory (the vendor affects VLAN attributes, CoA, RadSec)".
- **Shapes:** 1.11 (1.11.1.2 to 1.11.1.4), 4.11
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the owner: "it will largely be unifi at home, but I'd like to add Cisco support".
- **Why:** the vendor decides which VLAN attributes, MAB format, CoA and RadSec fabric must speak; what each vendor
  documents is in [2.3.11.5](2.3.11-radius-notes.md#23115-09-8021x-vendors-radsec-coa-and-the-lab-suite).
- **Status:** In force (decided 2026-10-12; Open until then). Built through 2.1.11.4 to 2.1.11.6.

## 2.1.11.2 How FreeRADIUS decides

- **Decision:** fabric's own policy (python3 module) asks 389-DS on every request, as the read-only `cn=radius_reader`, over verified LDAPS: nothing is cached or exported, so a disabled device or an unlinked certificate is refused at its next authentication. EAP-TLS devices are found by the SHA-256 fingerprint of the presented certificate (recorded during verification, keyed by serial, since FreeRADIUS exposes no fingerprint); MAB by MAC. People (EAP-TTLS/PAP) are checked by binding as the person, then by membership of a mapped group (`radius_people`). The directory unreachable means Reject (fail closed).
- **Shapes:** 1.11, 1.6
- **Made:** 2026-09-27 to 2026-10-01, while fabric was designed, by the owner.
- **Why:** a change in the directory takes effect at the next authentication, with nothing to sync.
- **Status:** In force, revised by the move to Samba AD (2.1.6.6): the same policy asks AD (2.3.6.1.16).

## 2.1.11.3 Which domain machines PEAP admits, and on what VLAN

- **Decision:** The `radius_people` group mappings, as for people: an enabled computer in the site's `OU=machines` is admitted when one of its groups (e.g. Domain Computers, or one the admin makes) is mapped, on that mapping's VLAN; no mapping, no machine access.
- **Shapes:** 1.11, 1.6
- **Made:** 2026-10-05, by the owner (S3.3).
- **Why:** [2.3.6.1.16](2.3.6-identity-notes.md#236116-s3-freeradius-on-ad).
- **Status:** In force.

## 2.1.11.4 A RADIUS client names its vendor, and the vendor's profile drives FreeRADIUS

- **Decision:** every RADIUS client has `vendor: unifi | cisco | generic` (default `generic`, so clients from before
  0.9 keep working as they did). One table, `fabriclib/radius/nas/profiles.py`, holds each vendor's facts: the VLAN
  reply attributes, how a genuine MAB request looks (Cisco: `Service-Type` Call-Check; UniFi: the MAC as user name
  and password), whether RadSec and CoA are offered and on which port, and which setup page applies. FreeRADIUS's
  policy gets the profile's policy part per client in `fabric-radius.json`, keyed by the client's `shortname`. The
  console's client form and `fabricctl radius add-client / set-client` take the vendor; Volume 4 has a UniFi page and
  a Cisco page whose IOS-XE configuration is generated per switch with the shared secret left out (shown once, on its
  own page, as before).
- **Shapes:** 1.11.1.2, 4.11.1.2, 4.11.1.3
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the builder on the owner's direction for 0.9 (2.1.11.1).
- **Why:** one place for vendor facts keeps the policy, the console and the pages in step; a MAB shape check per
  vendor refuses a password login that only pretends to be MAB.
- **Status:** In force; built, untested (2.3.11.5).

## 2.1.11.5 RadSec for the vendors that support it; RADIUS/UDP shown as a relaxation

- **Decision:** a client may use `transport: radsec` when its profile offers it (Cisco, IOS-XE 17); FreeRADIUS then
  listens on TCP 2083 (published and let through the firewall only while a RadSec client exists), requires a client
  certificate from the fabric CA with CN `radsec-<client name>`, revocation checked, and the TLS shared secret
  `radsec` (RFC 6614). The switch makes its own key; its request is signed on the Step-CA tab. A client on UDP keeps
  its shared secret on the LAN and is listed as a relaxed setting in `fabricctl status` and the console's Overview.
  UniFi is not offered RadSec: Ubiquiti's RadSec (UniFi Network 8.4+) is documented for Wi-Fi profiles, not switch
  ports (2.3.11.5).
- **Shapes:** 1.11.1.3, 1.2.8, 4.11.1.3
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the builder on the owner's direction for 0.9.
- **Why:** UDP RADIUS protects the secret with MD5 only; certificates from fabric's own CA give the switch and the
  server a proof each, with nothing new to trust. The CN binds a certificate to one client, so another fabric
  certificate at the same address cannot act as the switch.
- **Status:** In force; built, untested (2.3.11.5).

## 2.1.11.6 Disconnect and CoA from the console, for the vendors that support them

- **Decision:** the FreeRADIUS tab's Sessions view (and `fabricctl radius sessions / disconnect / reauthenticate`)
  sends RFC 5176 Disconnect-Request and CoA-Request (Cisco: `subscriber:command=reauthenticate` and
  `bounce-host-port`) from fabric-agent, over UDP to the session's NAS on the profile's port (Cisco: 3799, which
  fabric's IOS-XE configuration sets; IOS-XE's own default is 1700), signed with the client's shared secret and
  carrying a Message-Authenticator. For a vendor without CoA (UniFi, generic) it is refused with why and what to do
  instead; nothing is sent.
- **Shapes:** 1.11.1.4, 1.6.8, 4.11.1.4
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the builder on the owner's direction for 0.9.
- **Why:** disabling a device then takes effect at once on a switch that can be told; sending from the agent keeps
  FreeRADIUS's container unprivileged and the secret in one process. CoA over the RadSec tunnel itself (IOS-XE's
  `tls watchdoginterval`) is not used: it needs the server to send requests on a connection the switch opened,
  which fabric's FreeRADIUS set-up has not been shown to do; UDP from the agent works for every client alike.
- **Status:** In force; built, untested (2.3.11.5).

## 2.1.11.8 Sessions come from accounting lines in the journal; CoA is its own permission

- **Decision:** accounting is logged by fabric's policy as one `fabric: ACCT` line per request in FreeRADIUS's
  journal; open sessions are read from the last 26 hours of them, with no database. Sending Disconnect or CoA needs
  the new permission `radius:coa` (Admin and Equipment operator bundles); seeing sessions needs `radius:read`.
- **Shapes:** 1.11.1.4, 1.6.8
- **Made:** 2026-10-12, by the builder on the owner's direction for 0.9.
- **Why:** the journal already holds the decisions the console shows, and keeps nothing a restart would need;
  cutting someone off the network is a stronger act than reading it, so it is granted on its own.
- **Status:** In force; built, untested (2.3.11.5).
