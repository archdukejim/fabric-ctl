## 4.8.4.1 Changes wait for Apply

Design: [1.8.6.5](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.8.6-console-redo.md#1865-apply-per-page). From 0.8.0 the
Directory's pages (People, Groups, Computers, OUs, Access, Kerberos' ticket policy, Policies) and 802.1X's Devices, Device roles and RADIUS clients (but adding a client and a new secret, which carry the secret, 2.1.8.17), and DNS → Zones (but a TSIG key's creation and new secret) do not change anything when you press a form's button: the
change waits on its page until you apply it. Other pages join as they are rebuilt.

1. **Make the changes.** Each says "Waiting for Apply: …" and the top bar shows "N changes pending" with **Review**,
   **Apply** and **Discard**; the navigation shows the number beside the page. Your pending changes are yours alone:
   nobody else sees them, and another page's Apply never applies them.
2. **Review** lists each change in the order it will run, what it was and what it becomes, with **Drop** to remove
   one.
3. **Apply** runs them in order as one job. The Review shows each change's progress (queued, running, done, failed
   with why) and refreshes itself until the job ends. The first change that fails stops the job; it and the changes
   after it stay pending, to drop or apply again.
4. A change to something another person changed since you made it is refused, and shown again against how it is
   now ("Changed by someone else since"): check it, then **Apply** again.
5. A one-time password (a new person's, a reset sign-in, a new machine's join password) is shown once on the
   Review after Apply, under "Shown only now". It is not kept: write it down or hand it over then.
6. **Discard** drops every pending change of the page.

Pending changes live in fabric-agent's memory: a restart of the host or of `fabric-agent` drops them (make them
again). Everyone who may see a page sees its queue (who applied what, and how it went), not its pending changes.
Changing your own password is never pending: it is done at once.

DNS → Zones' **Apply** runs its changes, then gives BIND its new files and writes the landing page: BIND is reloaded,
nothing restarts, and another service's changed setting waits for the whole apply on the host (`sudo fabricctl
--apply`). If BIND does not take a change, the Review says which, and nothing after it runs. Two deletes of the same
record type in one zone, applied together, may refuse the second as changed: delete it again. DNS' Apply issues no
certificate: when a change renamed a name the landing page answers at, its last row on the Review says which names
the landing page's certificate lacks or still holds; run `sudo fabricctl --apply` on the host to issue it again
(2.1.8.29).

## 4.8.4.2 When an Apply's apply fails

Design: [1.8.6.13](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.8.6-console-redo.md#18613-a-failed-applys-error)
(2.1.8.31). Policies' and Kerberos' Apply end in an apply of the services they touch. When that apply does not
finish, its row on the Review is **failed** with "the apply did not finish:", the apply's last 10 lines, and where its
full output is kept:

1. Read the 10 lines: the cause is usually there (the step that failed, or the end of a traceback).
2. For the rest, on the host: `sudo less /opt/fabric/archive/apply-failed/apply-<time>.log` (the path the Review
   names; only root may read it). The newest 20 are kept.
3. The changes themselves were made (their rows are done); only the apply that writes them into the services did not
   finish. Fix what it names, then run `sudo fabricctl --apply` on the host.
