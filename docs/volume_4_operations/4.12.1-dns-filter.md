## 4.12.1.2 The DNS filter: the BIND resolver

Setup turns it on unless you say otherwise (`dns_filter: none` in a vars file or the settings after the install,
2.1.2.16; design
[1.12.2](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.12.2-bind-resolver.md#11221-status)): a second BIND answers the
network's DNS on `host_ip:53` and BIND moves to port 5053 (`bind_dns_port`), where RFC2136 clients and linked
federation sites find it; fabric's names are never filtered. DHCP hands out the host's address as before.

**An install of 0.6 or 0.7** does not upgrade to 0.8.0: it moves by its settings file (3.3.5), which keeps the DNS
filter's settings.

- **In the web console:** the *DNS filter* tab — statistics, the lists (a checkbox each: 4.12.1.4), your rules, the
  query log (admins: who looked up what, and *Allow* / *Block* from a line) and the upstreams; from 0.9.5 every change
  waits for one *Apply* (4.12.1.7), which takes a second or two, nothing restarts. Managing it needs `dns:filter` (admins, network operators); the query
  log, `dns:querylog` (admins only).
- **Lists, rules and upstreams** are settings ([1.1.9.9](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.1.9-optional-parts.md#1199-dns-filter-the-bind-resolver)):
  `dns_filter_lists` (`{name, url, enabled}`; the AdGuard DNS filter by default), `dns_filter_allow` and
  `dns_filter_block` (each name with every name below it), `dns_filter_upstreams` (Cloudflare over DNS-over-TLS by
  default; at least one). Changed in the vars file, they take effect with `sudo fabricctl dns-filter apply` (seconds,
  nothing restarts) or the next `sudo fabricctl --apply`.
- **`sudo fabricctl dns-filter status`**: whether the resolver and AdGuard Home answer, and for each list when its
  copy was fetched, its rules and its memory. Exit 1 while a ticked list has no good copy or AdGuard is not answering.
- **`sudo fabricctl dns-filter lists`**: fetch every list now (the site's and AdGuard's catalogue); AdGuard rereads
  only the copies that changed. The `fabric-dns-lists` timer does the same every night; a list that cannot be
  fetched, or comes back less than half its size, keeps its last good copy.
- **Why was a name blocked?** The query log says: `owner.rpz` (your rules), `group-<name>.rpz` (a group's), or the
  list's name for a list (AdGuard said which). `<base>/resolver/log/rpz.log` has the resolver's own rewrites.
- **The query log** (with Postgres, on by default): `sudo fabricctl dns-filter log [--client ADDRESS] [--name NAME]
  [--blocked] [--own] [--limit N]` — who looked up what, newest first, and for a blocked name the list that blocked
  it; kept 7 days. fabric's own queries show only with `--own` (4.12.1.6). `sudo fabricctl dns-filter stats`: the last
  24 hours, the last 7 days, per list, the most blocked names (no client addresses; kept 90 days). Both show the log as
  the `fabric-dns-log` timer last read it (every 5 minutes; the console's tab says when that was); for what happened in
  the last minutes, run `sudo fabricctl dns-filter ingest` first (2.1.12.10).
- Memory: the lists are held by AdGuard Home within its memory limit (planned with the others, at least
  768 MiB, more as the budget allows: `sudo fabricctl status`, 4.2.1); the lists page shows each list's
  estimate and the total, and refuses ticks that would pass the limit together (4.12.1.4, 4.12.1.8).

## 4.12.1.3 Client groups

A group gives some clients their own rules and safe search (design 1.12.2.15, 1.12.3.6); every other client is in
*everyone*. The YouTube level beside *Strict safe search* is used only while safe search is on: while it is off the
level is greyed out and kept as saved; to switch on with another level, tick the box and save, then choose the level
and save again (2.1.12.13). The lists are the site's and apply to every group; a group's *allow* passes them for its clients only. Groups are made in the web console's DNS filter tab (`dns:filter`). A group's clients are addresses and
subnets, and since 0.8 also references to DHCP's settings, turned into addresses on every apply (1.10.3.7):

| Client | Means |
|---|---|
| `192.168.4.50`, `192.168.6.0/24` | that address, that subnet |
| `dhcp:kids` or `dhcp:10.20.0.0/24` | a DHCP subnet, by its name or network |
| `device:printer` or `device:aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff` | a DHCP reservation's address, by its hostname or MAC |
| `vlan:30` | every DHCP subnet recorded with VLAN 30: where an 802.1X role with that VLAN puts its devices |

A reference that names nothing DHCP serves is refused when the group is saved, and so is any while DHCP is off.

## 4.12.1.4 The lists: AdGuard Home behind the resolver (0.7.0)

From 0.7.0 the lists are matched by AdGuard Home (`adguard-filter`) behind the resolver (design
[1.12.3](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.12.3-adguard-behind-bind.md#11231-status)). You never open
AdGuard's own page: it is not published, and everything is in fabric's *DNS filter* tab.

- **Lists:** AdGuard's catalogue as a table by category, a checkbox per list with its rules, its memory estimate and
  when its copy was fetched. Tick to use a list, untick to stop (it stays, with its copy, ready to tick again); from
  0.9.5 the ticks take effect when you press *Apply changes* (4.12.1.7). *Add by URL* adds a list of your own; only
  those have *Remove*. A ticked list whose copy has not been fetched yet is fetched at Apply, then used.
- **Memory:** an Apply is refused when the ticked lists together would need more than AdGuard Home's share of
  fabric's memory budget (its limit is 2.5 times that, for the minute it compiles them; 1.2.4.3.5); the message names
  the lists and the gap (4.12.1.8). Untick another list first, or give fabric more memory: `host_ram_capacity`, or a
  larger `memory_floors.adguard` (`sudo fabricctl status` shows the plan, and warns how far past the budget fabric
  may briefly go).
- **While AdGuard restarts** (an image update, or an apply that changes its configuration file) internet names fail
  (SERVFAIL) for the seconds it takes; fabric's own names keep answering. Nothing unfiltered passes.
- **Upgrading from 0.6:** `sudo fabricctl setup` moves AdGuard's settings as before (4.12.1.2); the new AdGuard is a
  different container, folder (`<base>/dnsfilter`) and account (`fabric-adguard`).
- **A group's own lists** (a 0.8.0 candidate only) are not carried over: the upgrade lists them as not moved.

## 4.12.1.5 Right after setup: the lists arrive by themselves (0.7.1)

Design: [1.12.2.17](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.12.2-bind-resolver.md#112217-the-tab-refreshes-itself-while-a-fresh-installs-first-fetch-runs-071).
On a fresh install AdGuard's catalogue and the ticked lists are fetched a minute or so after setup. Until then the
*DNS filter* tab says the first lists are still being fetched and refreshes itself every 10 seconds; nothing needs to
be done. It stops once every ticked list has a copy and the catalogue is read. If after five minutes they are still
missing, it stops and says so: press *Fetch every list now* on *Lists*, or run `sudo fabricctl dns-filter status` on
the host to see why.

## 4.12.1.6 fabric's own queries in the query log (0.9.5)

Design: [1.12.3.16](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.12.3-adguard-behind-bind.md#112316-fabrics-own-queries-hidden-by-default-095),
decision 2.1.12.12. The query log shows your network's clients. fabric's own lookups are hidden by default: those from
its containers (`fabric_subnet`, `10.255.0.0/24` unless you changed it, which includes `10.255.0.1`, where the
domain controller's own checks of its records come from), from the host's own address (`host_ip`) and from loopback.
They are still kept, for 7 days like every query.

- **To see them in the console:** *DNS filter* → *Query log*, tick *Show fabric's own queries*, *Search*. The box
  stays ticked while you search again; untick it to hide them. Under the results the page says when they are hidden.
- **On the host:** `sudo fabricctl dns-filter log --own` (with the other options as usual).
- **One of fabric's own addresses on its own:** search for it as the client (`10.255.0.1`, or
  `sudo fabricctl dns-filter log --client 10.255.0.1`): it shows without the box. A wider search, such as your LAN's
  `/24` holding the host's address, still hides them unless the box is ticked.
- **Statistics:** the totals count every query; *Queries, last 7 days* says how many of them were fabric's own, and
  so does `sudo fabricctl dns-filter stats`. *Most blocked* leaves fabric's own queries out.
- A device on your network asking for a domain name (an AD name, for instance) is a client and always shows.

## 4.12.1.7 Change the filter, then Apply (0.9.5)

Design: [1.12.3.17](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.12.3-adguard-behind-bind.md#112317-the-tabs-changes-wait-for-one-apply-095),
decisions 2.1.12.14 and 2.1.12.15. Nothing on the *DNS filter* tab changes the filter until you apply it. Needs
`dns:filter`.

**The lists:**

1. *DNS filter* → *Lists*. Tick the lists to use and untick those to stop. The boxes change at once; nothing is sent
   and the page does not reload.
2. Press **Apply changes** under the lists. Every tick is sent at once, with any other change of the tab that waits,
   and all of them are made together: one save, one apply of the filter (seconds, nothing restarts), and one fetch of
   any list ticked that has no copy yet.
3. The page comes back to *Lists* saying how many changes are being applied; while they are, it refreshes itself
   every 2 seconds and then shows them made.

Ticks you have not applied are lost if you leave the page, without a warning (the console runs no script). Under the
button the page says whether other changes wait.

**Rules, groups, safe search, upstreams** (and *Add a list by its URL*, *Remove*, *Allow* or *Block* from the query
log):

1. Make the change and press its button (*Block*, *Save addresses*, *Save safe search*, ...). It is held for Apply: the
   page comes back to the card you were on and says *Waiting for Apply*, and the bar at the top counts the changes
   waiting on the tab.
2. Make any other changes the same way.
3. Press **Apply** in the bar at the top (or *Apply changes* under the lists, which applies them with your ticks). The
   bar's *Review* lists each change and follows the apply step by step.

**To drop what waits:** press **Discard** in the bar at the top (or drop one change from *Review*). Nothing has
changed.

*Fetch every list now* is not a change: it starts the lists job at once.

## 4.12.1.8 Troubleshoot an Apply of the DNS filter refused (0.9.5)

| What the page says | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| *the ticked lists do not fit: ... MiB past its limit of ...*, the lists page again with your ticks kept | Together the ticked lists would pass AdGuard Home's memory limit (4.12.1.4). Nothing changed. | Untick about the size it names (each list's estimate is beside it) and press *Apply changes* again, or give fabric more memory (`host_ram_capacity`, `memory_floors.adguard`). |
| *the lists changed since this page was shown*, the lists shown as they are now | Someone else (another person, or `fabricctl` on the host) ticked, added or removed a list after you opened the page. Nothing changed, so their change is not undone. | Tick again on the page as it is now and press *Apply changes*. |
| A change refused when you press its button (*not a DNS name*, *outside the networks answered*, ...) | The change itself is wrong; it was not held. | Correct it and press the button again. |
| *Review* shows a change failed: *it changed since you made this change* | Someone changed the same rules, group or setting between your change and your Apply; that change and the ones after it still wait. | Open *Review*, look at the change against the page as it is now, drop it or keep it, and apply again. |
| *your apply of this page is still running* | Your last Apply has not finished. | Wait a few seconds; the page refreshes itself until it is done. |
| The apply's last step says *saved, but not in use yet: ...* | The settings are saved but AdGuard Home did not answer, or the lists job could not start. | `sudo fabricctl dns-filter status` on the host; the change is made at the next `sudo fabricctl dns-filter apply` or `sudo fabricctl --apply`. |
