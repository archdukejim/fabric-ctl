## 4.6.5.1 What works so far

fabric's directory is a Samba AD domain on every install, Windows machines or not (design:
[1.6.5](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.5-samba-dc.md), [1.6.3](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.3-samba-directory.md)).
It is moving over from 389-DS area by area (1.6.5.12); today:

- the domain controller on the host's address, hardened (1.2.7), kept as fabric wants it after every setup and apply
  (4.6.5.4), with your password policy;
- Windows machines can join it (tested: offline join, 2.3.6.1.14); its root-CA trust and log-on rights apply.

Moving next: people and groups (S2), devices and roles (S3), the address plan (S4). nginx no longer serves 389-DS on
ports 389/636 (the domain controller owns them): Linux clients set up against `ldap.<domain>` lose 389-DS until S6.

## 4.6.5.2 Setting it up

Setup asks, when it is not set:

- **The AD domain.** It is permanent: it cannot be changed once the domain exists. Suggested: a sibling of
  fabric's domain, apart from the sites' names (`lan.example.org` → `ad.example.org`); fabric's own domain (or a
  parent of it) is refused (2.1.6.11).
- **The domain controller's name** (`dc_name`). It is permanent too: a domain controller cannot be renamed.
  Suggested: `dc-` and the host's name, cut to 15 characters, so it says which machine the DC lives on; any name of
  one label, at most 15 letters, digits or hyphens, may be given. It is not the host's name: the host may be renamed
  later (the vars editor warns, then re-renders its certificates and pages) and the DC keeps its own. An install from
  before 0.7.0 keeps its DC's name, which was its host's (1.6.5.23). A site joining the organisation's domain is
  refused a DC name the domain already has; its setup says which names are taken.

The password policy is not asked: a new domain starts from fabric's default (2.1.6.35), changed any time with
`sudo fabricctl domain password-policy`.

Or set them in the settings file (an unattended setup refuses without the AD domain):

```yaml
ad_domain: ad.example.org          # fabric's domain: lan.example.org
dc_name: dc-pi-core                # default: dc-<hostname>, cut to 15 characters
ad_password_policy: {minimum_length: 14, complexity: true, history: 24, minimum_age_days: 1,
                     maximum_age_days: 0, lockout_threshold: 5, lockout_minutes: 15, lockout_window_minutes: 15}
```

Setup asks before opening the domain controller's ports in the host firewall (the `firewall` host change, 1.2.9).
The ports are opened to the LAN, `security.firewall_allow` and fabric's container network. Every setting:
[1.1.9.8](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.1.9-optional-parts.md#1198-the-directory-samba-ad-required).

## 4.6.5.3 Status and the password policy

```bash
sudo fabricctl domain status
```

Shows:

- the domain and this domain controller, and whether it runs;
- the domain-wide roles it holds;
- the password policy in force.

```bash
sudo fabricctl domain password-policy --minimum-length 16 --lockout-threshold 10
```

This changes those values and applies them. The whole policy is checked again; for example, a lockout shorter than
its reset window is refused, as AD requires. Without options it shows the policy in the settings. `--no-apply`
records the change for the next `sudo fabricctl --apply`.

## 4.6.5.4 What fabric keeps in the domain

fabric re-checks these after every setup and apply. It changes only what differs and leaves your own objects alone.

- **Schema:** fabric's and sudo's attributes in AD's schema. They are added once and never removed: AD's schema is
  permanent.
- **Layout:** `OU=sites`, with one OU per site (people, groups, machines, devices, device roles, networks, sudo rules,
  service accounts). The root site's OU also holds `OU=organisation`. New users and computers made with Windows'
  own tools land in the root site's OU.
- **Groups:** `<site>-users`, `<site>-admins`, `fabric-admins` and `fabric-break-glass`.
- **Site admins:** `<site>-admins` may change their own site's OU and nothing else, and not its service accounts.
- **AD site:** the AD site of this fabric site, with this site's networks as its subnets.
- **Password policy:** the policy you chose.
- **Group Policy:** *fabric: trust in fabric's root CA* (linked to the domain) and who may log on to the site's
  machines: on a new install *fabric: `<site>` log-on machines*, letting `<site>-machine-admins` log on to the
  machines in its `OU=machines`; on a site upgraded from before 0.9, *fabric: `<site>` log-on rights*, letting the
  site's people and admins and `fabric-admins` log on. Either way `fabric-break-glass` and each machine's own local
  Administrators always may. Change who may sign in per OU: 4.6.8.6.

## 4.6.5.5 The password policy and the roles in the web console (0.7.1)

Design: [1.6.5.25](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.5-samba-dc.md#16525-the-domain-section-the-roles-named-plainly-the-password-policy-edited-071).

**Directory → Domain** says which domain-wide roles this domain controller holds: "This controller holds all five
roles" on a single site, or which ones another controller holds.

To change the password policy there (permission `domain:admin`, the admins):

1. Set the values in the *Password policy* form: minimum length, passwords remembered, minimum and maximum age (0:
   never expires), failed sign-ins before a lockout (0: never), how long an account stays locked (0: until an admin
   unlocks it), after how long failed sign-ins are forgotten, and complexity on or off.
2. **Save the policy.** The whole policy is checked as `sudo fabricctl domain password-policy` checks it; a refusal
   says why (for example a lockout shorter than its window) and changes nothing.
3. **Apply changes** writes the saved policy into the domain.

## 4.6.5.6 A read-only DC's site: who is cached (0.9)

Design: [1.6.11.14](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.11-host-access.md#161114-a-read-only-dcs-site-its-people-and-machines-cached-09)
(decision 2.1.6.55). At a site whose domain controller is read-only, its people (`<site>-users`, `<site>-admins`)
and its machines are cached there, so they sign in while the link to the writable DC is down. fabric keeps this at
setup, at every apply and hourly; you do not need to do anything after adding or removing a person or machine. To do
it now, or to see what it does:

```
sudo fabricctl domain rodc-cache
```

It says what changed in `<site>-rodc-cache` and how many of the site's accounts the read-only DC now holds. Run it
with the link to the writable DC up: it writes there.

What it may say, and what to do:

- **"…-rodc-cache is not on this site's read-only DC's password replication allow list"**: the site joined before
  0.7.1. Run setup with the join account (a re-join, 1.9.8.4) to write it.
- **"…denied list is weaker than fabric allows (missing: …)"**: someone took a group off the read-only DC's denied
  list, or Domain Admins (or another privileged group) out of the Denied RODC Password Replication Group. Put it back
  at the writable DC (Active Directory Users and Computers: the read-only DC's *Password Replication Policy* tab, or
  the group's members). fabric caches nobody until then; it never writes the denied list itself.
- **"Reached through the site's groups but not the site's own … (through lab-users)"**: someone nested another site's
  person or an organisation account into the site's users or admins. Take them out of that group; fabric never
  preloads them, but while they are in it the read-only DC may cache them.
- **"not cached (refused by its denied list): …"**: a privileged account (for example a Domain Admin who is also a
  site admin). This is intended: privileged accounts are never cached at a read-only DC; they sign in only while the
  link is up.
- **"this site's domain controller is not read-only"**: nothing to do; every account signs in at it already.

**Changing the directory at a read-only DC's site:** people, groups, roles, devices and the address plan are
changed as anywhere else (the console or `fabricctl`); fabric makes each change at the writable DC, so the link to it
must be up. With it down a change is refused ("the writable domain controller … does not answer") and nothing is
changed: try again later. A change shows in the site's own lists after the next replication (a few minutes). Group
Policy (`fabricctl gpo set|clear|load`) and who may sign in per OU (`fabricctl domain logon set|clear`, the
console's form) are refused here: make them at the site above. If `fabricctl domain logon show` warns that this
site's per-OU policies are never applied, they were saved before this refusal and have no effect: make them at the
site above, then remove `logon_policies` from this site's settings.

**Taking someone off a read-only DC's site:** remove the person (Directory → People, or `fabricctl people remove`) or
the machine; that deletes the account and the read-only DC drops it at the next replication. Only taking a person out
of `<site>-users` stops new caching, but the password the read-only DC already holds stays until it changes: reset
their password too.
**Revised 2026-10-13 (the owner, 2.3.6.9.4 point 1):** the reset above is wrong and is withdrawn. A person moving
off a read-only DC's site (taken out of `<site>-users`, kept in the domain) **needs no action**: they still sign in,
and their account and group changes replicate everywhere; a reset would only disrupt them. The copy of their password
left on that read-only DC is harmless in normal operation; it matters only if that read-only DC is lost or stolen,
which is a separate procedure (not built yet: 2.3.6.9.4 point 1).
**Revised 2026-10-13 (2.1.6.58):** that procedure is built: [4.9.1.3](4.9.1-federation.md#4913-a-branchs-read-only-dc-is-lost-or-stolen-09).

## 4.6.5.7 A read-only DC's site: more than one writable DC (0.9)

At a site whose DC is read-only, every change goes to a writable DC
([1.6.11.13.1](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.11-host-access.md#161113-the-fabric-host-at-a-read-only-dcs-site-071),
decision 2.1.6.57). Built for 0.9; its tests are written and not yet run (2.3.9.3). By default that is the one DC the
site joined through. To keep changes working while it is down, list more, in the order to try, in the site's
settings (`vars.yaml`):

```yaml
ad_writable_servers: [dc-root.ad.example.net, dc-hub.ad.example.net]
```

then `sudo fabricctl apply`. Use names (machines write their SSH host keys with Kerberos, which needs a name);
at most 8. Machines learn the list when they are joined again (`/etc/fabric/managed.env`, `WRITE_DCS`); until then
they use the DC they were joined with. A change made at any of them shows at this site after the next replication
(a few minutes). When none answers, the refusal names each DC tried; nothing was changed.
