## 4.6.1.1 `--keycloak-sync`
Re-run `keycloak_bootstrap.py`: realm, LDAP federation to the domain (Samba AD, [1.6.2](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.2-keycloak.md)), group mapper and sync, `fabric-console-admin` role → `admins` group, the `fabric-webui` OIDC client and its TOTP flow. Idempotent — use after changing `webui_*` vars or to repair drift made in the admin console. It reads fabric's secrets, so OpenBao must be unlocked.

```bash
sudo fabricctl --keycloak-sync
```

## 4.6.1.3 `fabricctl people`

The site's people from the host, as root (the audit names the CLI; root has an admin's rights). The web console's
Directory → People does the same per permission ([1.8.2](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.8.2-webui-security.md)).
What each change does: [1.6.3.16](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.3-samba-directory.md#16316-disabling-removing-and-regrouping-people).

```bash
sudo fabricctl people list
```

```bash
sudo fabricctl people add jsmith --first Jane --last Smith --email jane@example.org
```

The one-time password is printed once on the terminal, never logged; they choose their own at the first sign-in.
`reset <uid>` prints a new one, removes their TOTP and ends their sessions.

```bash
sudo fabricctl people disable jsmith
```

Every sign-in is refused at once and their sessions end; `enable` allows it again.

```bash
sudo fabricctl people remove jsmith
```

It asks for the user name typed back, then removes the account and revokes the certificates fabric issued to them
(`--confirm jsmith` answers unattended). `groups <uid>` lists their groups; `group add|remove <group> <uid>` changes
one. Refused, with the reason: another site's person, a service account, the last admin who can sign in.

## 4.6.1.4 Unlocking, and the site helpdesk

A person locked out by too many wrong passwords can sign in again with the password they have:

```bash
sudo fabricctl people unlock jsmith
```

It clears the lockout in the domain and in Keycloak's own brute-force protection; their password, TOTP and sessions
stay (a forgotten password is `reset`). In the console: Directory → People → **Unlock** (shown for a locked person),
permission `people:unlock` (the helpdesk and admin bundles).

**A site's own admins (`<site>-admins`) are its helpdesk** (0.9, decision 2.1.9.21,
[1.6.8.2](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.8-rbac.md#1682-the-site-helpdesk-and-roles-granted-from-above)):
signed in to their own site's console they see that site's people only, and may add a person, reset a sign-in,
unlock, and disable or enable — never a person of another site, nor one who holds a role (Central IT, any admin or
operator); the page says why when it refuses. With Windows' directory tools they have the same rights, and no more
([1.6.3.18](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.3-samba-directory.md#16318-layered-roles-in-the-directory-09)).
More is granted from a site above: [4.9.1.2](4.9.1-federation.md#4912-roles-granted-from-above).

## 4.6.1.5 Where an account lives, and moving it

Design: [1.6.3.18.8](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.6.3-samba-directory.md#16318-layered-roles-in-the-directory-09)
(decision 2.1.9.24). An account may hold a role only at the site it lives at or a site below it: Central IT's
accounts live at the top site. Granting a role, or adding someone to a role's group, is refused for an account that
lives lower, saying where it must live. Move it first, on a site above both its place and the target (usually the
top):

```bash
sudo fabricctl people move jdoe --site hq          # jdoe's account now lives at hq (also in hq-users)
```

The account keeps its name, password and groups. A branch cannot move anyone above itself.

**Passwords of people with roles above a branch.** A branch's own sign-in page cannot store a new password for a
person whose role reaches beyond that branch. Such people change their password at a sign-in page of their own level
(Central IT: the top site's), or on a domain-joined Windows machine.

**Misplaced accounts.** Accounts placed before this rule are not moved for you: every convergence, `sudo fabricctl
federation roles` and the Federation tab's Roles card list them (`misplaced: … lives at … but holds a role at …`).
Move each one, or take the role away.
